## Conversation archive rule

Because this project is the writing of a book, conversation is primary research material, not disposable coordination.

From this point forward, significant conversations about the New Westside visual system, its theological origin, design research, Doré's learning, manuscript development, Journal planning, critique, rejected ideas, naming, color meanings, Scripture, prayer, discernment, and publication direction must be preserved as a **user-visible conversation transcript**.

The archive must preserve:

- the user's wording, questions, corrections, judgments, theological statements and design intentions;
- the assistant's user-visible answers and formulations;
- explicit moments where a concept is corrected, renamed, rejected, expanded or promoted;
- uploaded-source events that materially change the project, including the file name and what it was used for;
- dates and ordering;
- links to resulting canonical files/commits where relevant.

Do **not** replace a transcript with a summary. Summaries may exist as indexes, but the full dialogue remains primary evidence.

Do not archive hidden chain-of-thought, tool payloads, secrets, private runtime internals or unrelated personal data. The intended record is the complete **user-visible project dialogue**.

## Why this matters for the book

The book is not only the final theory. It is also the history of how the theory was received, questioned, clarified, corrected, tested and written.

Dialogue can later become:

- manuscript quotations or reconstructed exchanges;
- editorial sidebars;
- design critique case studies;
- theological reflection;
- Journal marginalia;
- evidence of how a design principle emerged;
- evidence of where Doré misunderstood and learned;
- a provenance trail separating original spiritual/design intent from later technical interpretation.
