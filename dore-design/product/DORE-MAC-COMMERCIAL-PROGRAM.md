# Dore / 多寫 — Mac Commercial Product Program

Status: CANONICAL PRODUCT DIRECTION
Established: 2026-09-04

## 1. Provisional product name

- English: **Dore**
- Chinese: **多寫**

Both names are provisional until naming, trademark, App Store metadata and market validation are completed.

## 2. End state

The editor project's product destination is a real macOS application released through Apple's Mac App Store.

Core proposition:

> **Write directly on the book. Design directly on the publication. AI works around the author's canonical work without taking authorship away.**

Dore / 多寫 should mature from an internal editor into a commercial local-first publishing studio for books, Journals and visible web publishing.

## 3. Product thesis

The primary object is not a Word file, Markdown file or detached mockup. It is the publication itself.

For authors:
- write directly in the rendered book surface;
- canonical manuscript remains protected and versioned;
- AI can proofread, research, suggest, translate and align bilingual editions;
- Dore resources can assist typography, cover design, visual systems and publication design;
- one canonical work can produce HTML book, web, PDF and print-oriented outputs.

For web publishing:
- edit the actual visible webpage state;
- preserve source integrity and revision history;
- promote deliberately to production.

## 5. Market evidence must influence product detail

Product decisions should not be frozen from internal preference alone.

For every material commercial decision, maintain:

**Hypothesis → Target user → Evidence → Prototype → Test → Conversion/usage signal → Learning → Product decision**

Market evidence may influence onboarding, information architecture, feature packaging, AI workflow, visual presentation, pricing, trial design and marketing language.

Marketability does not override authorship protection, source integrity, privacy, accessibility or core product quality.

## 6. Commercial phases

### Phase A — Product truth

Goal: make the core promise undeniably real.

Required proof:
- direct-on-book writing;
- Author Lock and recoverability;
- AI suggestion without silent overwrite;
- bilingual workflow;
- real book import and sustained long-form use;
- deterministic preview/export;
- cover/publication design workflow;
- visible web-editor path;
- reliability under real author use.

### Phase B — Productization

Goal: turn the working studio into a distributable Mac application.

Work:
- native/package architecture decision;
- sandbox/filesystem model;
- local database/storage and migration;
- crash recovery;
- backup/export;
- AI provider/model abstraction;
- credentials/keychain/security;
- privacy model;
- code signing/notarization;
- App Sandbox and entitlements;
- App Store update path;
- accessibility/localization;
- telemetry policy;
- onboarding;
- licensing/IAP/subscription architecture.

### Phase C — Market discovery

Goal: decide what form of Dore is easiest to understand, desire and pay for.

Research segments initially around serious authors, bilingual authors/editors, independent publishers and publication designers. Do not lock the target segment until evidence exists.

Deliverables:
- problem interviews;
- competitor/category map;
- jobs-to-be-done map;
- landing-page positioning experiments;
- pricing/willingness-to-pay evidence;
- feature-value ranking;
- objections and trust requirements;
- first commercial packaging recommendation.

### Phase D — Brand and selling system

Goal: make the product recognizable and sellable.

Deliverables:
- Dore / 多寫 naming validation;
- app icon/logo;
- wordmark;
- product design system;
- brand/design manual;
- App Store screenshots;
- demo story/video;
- product website/landing page;
- bilingual sales copy;
- onboarding narrative;
- launch press/creator kit;
- support and documentation system.

### Phase E — Beta and business validation

Goal: prove real people can adopt and retain the product.

Track at minimum:
- activation;
- first successful writing session;
- first protected manuscript save;
- first AI-assisted editorial action;
- first export/publication;
- repeat writing sessions;
- retention;
- conversion/trial-to-paid where applicable;
- support burden;
- AI cost per active/paid user;
- gross margin assumptions.

### Phase F — Mac App Store launch

Goal: ship a review-ready, commercially coherent macOS app.

No submission until app completeness, review notes/demo access, privacy, payments, entitlements, metadata, screenshots, support/refund path, stability and product claims are verified against then-current Apple rules.

### Phase G — Learning business

After launch, Dore continuously learns:

**Acquire → Activate → Publish → Retain → Pay → Refer → Learn → Improve**

Product, design and commercial loops feed each other without allowing short-term conversion optimization to damage the core authorship promise.

## 7. Business model is deliberately unresolved

Do not prematurely freeze paid-upfront vs subscription vs hybrid vs BYO-AI.

The decision must model:
- App Store rules;
- ongoing AI inference costs;
- user willingness to pay;
- local-first value;
- recurring value required for subscriptions;
- provider/API terms;
- gross margin;
- privacy expectations;
- support and update cost.

A likely architecture should remain model-agnostic and capable of supporting product-managed AI and/or user-supplied model credentials where policy and UX permit, but implementation requires explicit legal/security/App Store validation.

## 8. Apple distribution constraints are product inputs

Before commercial implementation, re-check current Apple rules rather than relying on remembered policy.

Current research baseline (2026-09-04):
- Apple Developer Program membership is currently USD 99/year;
- App Store digital-goods commission is generally 30%, with 15% under qualifying programs/cases; Small Business Program eligibility can reduce paid-app/IAP commission to 15% for qualifying developers;
- unlocking digital app functionality generally requires In-App Purchase under App Review rules, subject to storefront/category exceptions;
- Mac App Store apps have sandbox/update/distribution requirements that affect architecture;
- subscription value and disclosures must satisfy App Store requirements.

These are a dated research baseline, not permanent rules. Re-verify immediately before pricing, payment implementation and submission.
