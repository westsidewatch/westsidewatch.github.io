# Doré Exploration — Motion Language for New Westside

Date: 2026-09-09
Status: EXPLORATION RECORD — NOT YET ARCHITECTURE

## Research question

New Westside has already accepted that the site should feel alive. The open problem is no longer whether it moves, but:

> **how it moves, what moves, who controls the movement, at what speed, and what editorial meaning that movement produces.**

The key correction is to treat this as a **Motion Language**, not a collection of animation effects.

## Core hypothesis

Motion should be semantic.

Different editorial currents may inhabit the same spatial architecture while carrying different temporal identities:

- fast current = breaking / rolling news / 三更報導 / live developments;
- medium current = current issue / editorial browsing / features;
- slow current = theology / reflection / prayer / long-form visual essay;
- near-still current = archive / sacred pause / text-led reading / memorial or contemplative content;
- cinematic current = Scripture Cinema / major feature opening / designed narrative sequence.

The metaphor of living water is therefore expressed through **different flow rates**, not literal water graphics.

> **Same river, different current speeds.**

## Mature precedents and transferable principles

### 1. MERSI — motion as one coherent physical vocabulary

The MERSI 2026 case study is especially mature because its movement is not decorative. The project cover is a transitional object; it flips and becomes the next space. Project layouts progressively reposition from a freer composition into an ordered grid. Horizontal project reading continues the same editorial/architectural language.

Its implementation also shows a useful division of labor: high-value transitions are deliberately choreographed while ordinary layout remains ordinary layout.

Transferable rule:

> **Use a small number of recurring physical verbs — open, flip, expand, align, flow, return — instead of many unrelated effects.**

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/27/between-print-and-digital-the-making-of-mersis-website/

### 2. MacGuffin — motion direction carries information meaning

MacGuffin deliberately assigns horizontal movement to magazine/article navigation and vertical movement to practical/informational content. This is important because axis direction is not an effect but part of information architecture.

Transferable rule:

> **Every direction of movement should have a stable semantic role.**

Source: https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/double-click-may-2022-online-magazine-digital-310522

### 3. More or Less — scale change creates tempo

The More or Less digital magazine uses very large photography, tiny-to-huge scale changes, and viewport-relative sizing to create a dynamic reading rhythm. The key lesson is that motion sensation can come from **scale rhythm and composition**, not constant animation.

Transferable rule:

> **Temporal energy can be produced by changes in scale, density, crop and spacing even when an object is not continuously moving.**

### 4. Noon — continuous navigation with no dead ends

Noon’s redesign was conceived as a fluid archive where visitors can continue from images to words and sidestep into contributors without reaching dead ends. Its visual system is intentionally restrained so that ambitious navigation remains understandable.

Transferable rule:

> **Continuous motion must preserve continuous orientation. Fluidity without navigational continuity becomes confusion.**

### 5. SFMOMA — clarity first, motion second

SFMOMA’s digital-publication framework combines immersive cover imagery, scrolling, in-page navigation and publication-specific color identity. Immersive identity remains anchored by explicit navigation.

Transferable rule:

> **The site may move continuously, but the reader’s current location and available next actions must remain visually explicit.**

### 6. Obys 2026 — a stable point inside changing motion states

The new Obys identity is a particularly strong precedent for the current New Westside question. Obys defines itself as a `space` containing projects, ideas and different states. Its logo remains present while layouts shift among vertical, horizontal and grid arrangements. Motion connects those states rather than decorating them. The team explicitly describes small timing, spacing and behavior adjustments as decisive to the final feeling.

Transferable New Westside principle:

> **A highly dynamic site needs a persistent orientation invariant.**

For New Westside this invariant does not need to be a logo. Candidates to test include the current/section marker, a minimal Scripture coordinate, a persistent edge/line, or a stable navigation anchor. The purpose is to let radically different editorial currents move while the visitor always feels that the same publication world persists.

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/14/designing-ourselves-the-new-obys-identity-and-website/

### 7. Má Sài Gòn — poetic space plus linear escape route

Adrien Vanderpotte describes Má Sài Gòn as a sensory space rather than a conventional promotional site. Users can drift freely through a mosaic of stories, but the designers deliberately added a toggle between free-roaming and linear navigation so poetic exploration never becomes an access barrier.

This is directly relevant to New Westside spatial navigation.

Transferable rule:

> **Immersive movement and explicit navigation should be parallel modes, not competitors.**

A visitor can drift through Living Magazine currents, while an explicit route remains available for people who know what they want.

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/27/whooshes-snaps-and-shaders-adrien-vanderpotte-and-the-feeling-of-the-interface/

### 8. Telescope — motion vocabulary derived from the brand metaphor

The same case study describes Telescope, whose motion system derives from optical behavior: radial effects and deep zoom pull users into thematic worlds. The important lesson is not the zoom effect itself. It is that a brand noun becomes a small set of repeatable interface behaviors.

Transferable rule:

> **Translate the Living Water / Scripture idea into behaviors, not illustrations.**

For New Westside, possible abstract behaviors are flow, gather, separate, deepen, surface, accelerate, settle and confluence. These should be tested as interface verbs rather than rendered as literal water.

### 9. Silent House — sophisticated motion can be almost invisible

Silent House uses a clean cinematic stage for very strong source material. Its identity is carried partly by subtle gradients and smooth continuity between divisions; the developer describes the sophistication as something felt through rhythm rather than visual complexity.

Transferable rule:

> **Not every current needs a visible effect. Motion language can be strongest when it is felt as continuity, timing and ease.**

This is especially important for theology, long-form, prayer and premium editorial work.

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/05/27/whooshes-snaps-and-shaders-adrien-vanderpotte-and-the-feeling-of-the-interface/

### 10. Bisous — one spatial grid, heterogeneous cinematic content

Bisous uses a four-column system as a persistent spatial backbone across sections/pages while allowing large imagery, cinematic transitions and different content formats to coexist. The palette is restrained so imagery and motion can carry emotional tone.

Transferable rule:

> **A stable spatial skeleton can make heterogeneous motion feel coherent without making every object look alike.**

This strengthens the current New Westside thesis: shared 8:5/spatial laws can coexist with radically different editorial identities.

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/29/inside-bisous-designing-an-editorial-experience-for-cinematic-cgi/

### 11. Frequency Breathwork — rhythm can become architecture

Frequency Breathwork is useful because its designers rejected merely adding a visual metaphor for breath. They treated rhythm as a structural problem: the interface shifts, expands and contracts, and movement is introduced only when it extends the meaning of a section. Its interactive WebGL scene is lifecycle-managed and disposed when not needed on mobile.

Transferable rule:

> **Motion belongs where it carries meaning; absence of motion is part of the same system.**

This is a mature confirmation of the New Westside `fast / slow / still` direction.

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/12/29/frequency-breathwork-translating-the-invisible-rhythm-of-breath-into-digital-form/

### 12. Green Stack — constrain motion principles before choosing effects

Green Stack deliberately fixed three animation principles early: avoid generic opacity fades, use a consistent directional motion, and make variable typography part of motion. Those principles became a filter for deciding which effects belonged and which did not.

Transferable rule:

> **New Westside needs a short motion constitution before it needs a large animation library.**

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/03/26/design-in-motion-the-animation-principles-behind-green-stack/

### 13. Horeca — performance improves by animating less

The Horeca case study is valuable as a production warning. Complex motion became more robust when GSAP pinning was replaced by CSS sticky, per-frame tweens were reduced to direct sets, references were cached, and Lenis was removed from mobile. The authors conclude that major performance gains came from doing less work per frame rather than adding optimization machinery.

Transferable rule:

> **A living site should animate the minimum necessary state, not the maximum possible DOM.**

This strongly supports viewport leadership: only the active current should receive full temporal treatment; background currents should settle or pause.

Source: https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/10/building-horeca-advanced-motion-design-in-webflow-without-the-performance-trade-offs/

## Round 03 — motion orchestration, not merely motion tokens

The previous round identified properties such as speed, agency, continuity, attention, pause and acceleration. This round adds a more important layer: **orchestration between currents**.

A page containing multiple moving editorial fields needs a temporal hierarchy analogous to typographic hierarchy.

### Candidate law A — viewport conductor

At any moment, the viewport has one `lead current`.

- The lead current receives full intended velocity, contrast and transition behavior.
- Adjacent currents become `support currents`: slower, quieter or still.
- Distant currents are dormant and need not animate/render continuously.
- Leadership transfers as the visitor crosses a spatial threshold.

This avoids the casino/dashboard failure where everything competes simultaneously.

### Candidate law B — motion focus follows reading intent

Motion should react to user intent rather than simply elapsed time.

Possible states:

`ambient -> approached -> focused -> reading -> leaving`

- `ambient`: current may drift according to editorial temperament;
- `approached`: it begins to become legible and assumes visual priority;
- `focused`: neighboring currents reduce activity;
- `reading`: autonomous movement stops or becomes negligible;
- `leaving`: movement can resume and the next current prepares to lead.

This is more mature than `autoplay true/false`.

### Candidate law C — velocity is editorial state, not only content category

A current may change speed over its life.

Example for 三更報導:

`breaking -> developing -> established -> archived`

could map to:

`urgent -> active -> measured -> still`

The same story therefore literally settles as knowledge stabilizes. This should be explored carefully; it must never imply epistemic certainty merely from animation speed, but it can express publication state when explicitly backed by editorial metadata.

### Candidate law D — entering depth causes deceleration

Across the system, entering an object should normally reduce velocity:

`river -> cover -> opening field -> article/research -> stillness`

This gives the spatial model a natural physical intuition: browsing is flow; reading is dwelling.

The inverse transition can restore motion when returning to the field.

### Candidate law E — confluence rather than collision

When two currents relate, they should not merely overlap visually. Their relationship can be expressed by controlled convergence:

- a Scripture coordinate can surface in a Journal feature;
- a developing report can gather related background pieces;
- a book can surface beside a theology essay;
- ONE can appear as a depth route beneath a feature.

The design problem becomes `how currents meet`, not `how many carousels fit`.

This may become the strongest non-literal expression of Living Water.

## Candidate New Westside motion constitution — version 0.1

Before numerical motion tokens, test these semantic laws:

1. **Meaning before movement.** Nothing moves merely to prove the site is alive.
2. **One lead motion per viewport.** Other motion supports or yields.
3. **Movement yields to reading.** Focus and depth cause deceleration/stillness.
4. **Different editorial identities have different temporal characters.** News can run; theology can dwell.
5. **Direction has meaning.** Horizontal, vertical and depth transitions are not interchangeable decoration.
6. **Objects persist across entry.** Cover becomes opening; it does not vanish into an unrelated page.
7. **There is always an explicit navigation escape route.** Spatial exploration never traps the visitor.
8. **A stable orientation invariant survives changing art direction.** The publication remains recognizable through motion states.
9. **Motion is progressively enhanced.** CSS/native layout first; complex choreography only where it carries meaning.
10. **Stillness is a first-class state.** A living river includes deep and quiet water.

## Motion properties retained from Round 02

### Speed

- `urgent`
- `active`
- `measured`
- `contemplative`
- `still`
- `cinematic`

### Agency

- `system-led`
- `scroll-led`
- `gesture-led`
- `sequence-led`
- `still-until-focus`

### Continuity

- independent cover;
- visually linked neighbor;
- seamless strip segment;
- transitional object into deeper content.

### Attention

Only one foreground motion should dominate a viewport at a time.

### Pause

Pause is intentional editorial behavior for reading, theology, prayer, key Scripture moments and inspection.

### Acceleration / deceleration

Temporal change may carry editorial state and transition from discovery to reading.

## New Storybook experiments — Round 03

### M7 — viewport conductor

Build four vertically adjacent currents with intentionally different temporal characters. Implement one lead current at a time and transfer leadership on viewport thresholds. Compare against all-four-moving baseline.

Acceptance: the page must feel globally alive but locally calm.

### M8 — approach / focus / read state machine

One 8:5 row transitions through ambient, approached, focused, reading and leaving states. Verify whether stopping autonomous motion on reading feels natural rather than abrupt.

### M9 — developing-news deceleration

Use one 三更報導 story in four editorial states. Test whether speed/density/timing can distinguish publication state without becoming a stock-market ticker or implying unsupported certainty.

### M10 — current confluence

Let two visually distinct currents share one canonical content object. Prototype a moment where the related object appears to connect/gather across currents without merging their visual identities.

Acceptance: relationship should be understood spatially before explanatory text.

### M11 — free exploration / linear mode

Borrow the mature Má Sài Gòn principle: same content, two navigation agencies. Test a free spatial Living Magazine mode and an explicit linear/section navigation mode without maintaining duplicate content.

### M12 — stable orientation invariant

Test three possible persistent anchors across Scripture Cinema, Journal art direction, 三更報導 and a quiet theology current. The anchor must survive radically different palettes and motion speeds without behaving like a conventional sticky corporate navbar.

## Production/performance constraints strengthened in this round

- use native scroll/sticky/CSS animation where they express the law sufficiently;
- only the lead/nearby currents should receive continuous frame work;
- mobile is a first architecture, not desktop motion with emergency reductions;
- WebGL must be lifecycle-scoped to places where raster/CSS cannot express the art direction;
- reduced-motion preserves the same spatial/content model;
- in-app browser fallbacks should be designed early;
- motion tokens should be semantic and bounded, not arbitrary per-template durations.

## Current synthesis

Round 03 strengthens the thesis from:

> **different content has different speed**

into:

> **New Westside has a temporal hierarchy. Currents have editorial temperaments, but the page conducts them so that one leads, others yield, and movement decelerates into reading.**

This is much closer to a design language than a collection of animations.

The Living Water metaphor is becoming abstract enough to avoid visual cliché:

- `flow` = discovery continuity;
- `current` = editorial identity;
- `velocity` = temporal/editorial temperament;
- `depth` = entering content;
- `settling` = reading;
- `confluence` = related content meeting;
- `riverbed` = stable canonical IA;
- `orientation invariant` = the persistent sign that the visitor remains inside New Westside.

These are candidate interface laws, not literal water graphics.

## Engineering boundary

Do not freeze numerical speeds, select a permanent animation library, or replace the production homepage from this exploration.

Correct path:

`Doré Exploration -> Storybook orchestration prototypes -> human critique -> motion constitution/tokens -> Design constraints -> promotion gate -> production`

The Doré learning object for this round is:

`different speeds -> attention conflict -> mature orchestration precedents -> viewport conductor + intent state + deceleration + confluence -> Storybook tests -> critique -> retained capability`
