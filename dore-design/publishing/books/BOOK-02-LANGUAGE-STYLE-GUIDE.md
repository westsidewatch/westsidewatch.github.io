# Book 02 Language Style Guide — v0.2

Status: ACTIVE / DESCRIPTIVE-FIRST STYLE SYSTEM
Book: 《神很遠 神很近：天國語言極簡史》
Established: 2026-09-04

## 1. Purpose

This guide defines the language of a **nonfiction theological novel / popular theological narrative**: it may have the narrative force, character movement and scene continuity of a historical novel, but its stories are not invented. Biblical scenes, chronology, quotations, lexical claims and historical claims must remain grounded in Scripture or verifiable source material.

The target is not “AI prose made literary.” The target is a human, calm, readable narrative that carries serious scholarship underneath without making the reader feel trapped inside an academic paper.

Current methodological reference family:

- 《萬曆十五年》 — literary historical narrative + macro-structure from micro-events;
- 《耶路撒冷三千年》 — long sacred/historical chronology made readable through people, episodes and place;
- 《時間簡史》 — difficult specialist knowledge reorganized for non-specialists.

Do not imitate recognizable wording, sentence patterns or authorial fingerprints from any reference book.

## 2. Canonical genre formula

**學術在下面，故事在上面；敘事可以像小說，證據不能像小說。**

And:

**先讓事情發生，再讓概念出現；先讓讀者靠近，再帶他進入深水。**

The popular book must remain separate from the academic appendix. The appendix proves and qualifies; the main book guides and narrates.

## 3. What to learn from 《萬曆十五年》

Transferable methods:

- begin with concrete events, people, places, decisions and small facts before announcing the abstract thesis;
- let large structures emerge from apparently small incidents;
- keep analytical distance even when the material is dramatic;
- use people as lenses into a larger system, rather than turning every chapter into pure biography;
- combine readable narrative with serious argument;
- prefer controlled observation over emotional instruction to the reader;
- allow irony or contradiction to arise from facts rather than from mocking language;
- when reconstructing motives, mark inference carefully rather than converting speculation into fact;
- move between close detail and long historical/theological arcs.

For Book 02, the corresponding move is often:

**one biblical scene / one word / one apparently small textual tension → a much larger redemptive-language arc.**

## 4. What to learn from 《耶路撒冷三千年》

Transferable methods:

- use a durable narrative spine so a very long chronology does not become a list;
- let people and episodes carry epochs;
- maintain orientation when moving across centuries;
- keep place/history/theology connected without turning every transition into a lecture;
- alternate compression and expansion: centuries can pass in a paragraph, one decisive scene can occupy several pages;
- introduce historical burden only when it changes the reader's understanding of the story;
- allow complexity to accumulate rather than explaining the whole system at the beginning.

For Book 02, the durable spine is not a city but a language/redemption arc:

**creation → naming → fracture/Babel → waiting → incarnation/Logos → Pentecost → Corinth → inner renewal → hope.**

## 5. What to learn from 《時間簡史》

Transferable methods:

- assume intelligence, not prior expertise;
- introduce one conceptual burden at a time;
- explain why a difficult term is needed before defining it;
- use analogy as scaffolding, never as proof;
- remove unnecessary prerequisite knowledge;
- let the reader carry one solved question into the next;
- return to large questions after enough groundwork has been laid;
- simplify the route, not the truth.

For Book 02 this means Hebrew/Greek should not arrive as ornamental scholarship. A term appears because the reader has reached a problem ordinary translation cannot fully explain.

## 6. Existing manuscript voice to preserve

The current manuscript already has a strong native pattern:

**scene → small detail → key word → lexical/theological opening → larger biblical arc → personal reflection.**

Examples of recurring strengths to preserve:

- chapter openings that enter a scene before explaining it;
- short paragraphs that create breathing room;
- Hebrew/Greek terms introduced as turning points rather than academic ornaments;
- repeated images such as wind, waiting, speech, silence, light and nearness;
- concrete physical actions that carry theological weight;
- sudden short sentences after longer explanatory passages;
- movement from ancient text to ordinary contemporary experience;
- a final reflective turn that remains simple rather than sermonizing.

## 7. Nonfiction scene rule

Narrative vividness must not manufacture evidence.

Three levels:

### A. Text-given detail
May be stated directly.

### B. Historically/geographically supported reconstruction
May be used sparingly when it helps orientation, but internal research notes should preserve its source/provenance.

### C. Imaginative possibility
Do not present it as fact. If retained at all, it must be clearly framed as imagination/possibility and must never carry the argument.

Do not invent dialogue, motives, private thoughts or causal certainty simply because a scene would read better that way.

## 8. Sentence-level rules

### Prefer nouns and verbs over adverbial inflation

Use the action itself when it is strong enough.

Prefer:
- 他坐在黑暗裡，沒有再說話。

Avoid:
- 他極其痛苦地、深深地陷入一種無法言喻的沉默之中。

### Adverbs

Use fewer adverbs, especially intensifiers such as:
- 非常
- 極其
- 深深地
- 真正地
- 顯然地
- 其實
- 無疑地

Keep them only when they change meaning rather than add emotional pressure.

### Adjectives

Do not pursue “more adjectives.” Prefer **few exact adjectives**. Adjectives should name a visible, historical, material or conceptual quality.

### Verbs

Prefer an exact verb over a weak verb plus explanation.

Prefer:
- 他停下來。
- 人群散開。
- 這個字在七十士譯本裡換了形狀。

Over:
- 他呈現出停止前進的一種狀態。

### Sentence rhythm

Default rhythm:
- medium sentence for information;
- longer sentence for relation/cause/arc;
- short sentence for weight.

Do not make every paragraph one sentence. Do not make every important sentence a standalone slogan.

### Paragraph length

Short paragraphs are part of the manuscript voice, but over-fragmentation becomes AI-like dramatic writing. Use paragraph breaks when the thought, scene, speaker, evidence level or rhetorical function changes.

## 9. Narrative temperature

Default: **cool surface, warm depth**.

The narrator should usually sound calm, observant and patient. Emotional weight comes from the event or image itself.

Avoid telling the reader what to feel:
- 令人震撼
- 令人感動
- 不禁讓人深思
- 這是何等奇妙
- 我們必須深刻認識到

Let the scene carry the force.

The desired effect is not emotional flatness. It is **contained emotion**.

## 10. Exposition pattern

Preferred sequence:

1. What happened?
2. What problem does the ordinary reading leave unresolved?
3. What exact word/text/source matters?
4. What does the source demonstrably establish?
5. What inference is being proposed?
6. Where does it sit in the larger biblical arc?
7. What does that change in how we read the story?

Do not begin every section with the conclusion.

## 11. Technical-term doorway

A Hebrew/Greek word should normally enter through a narrative need.

Weak:
- 本章首先研究 davar 的三個語義維度。

Stronger pattern:
- the reader first encounters an action, contradiction or question;
- ordinary Chinese/English translation proves insufficient;
- the original word becomes the necessary key;
- the book opens the word only as far as this moment requires;
- deeper apparatus can be deferred to notes/appendix.

The reader should feel: **“I needed that word.”**

## 12. Scale movement

A chapter should breathe between three scales:

**close scene → textual/linguistic middle distance → redemptive-history long view.**

Do not stay at macro scale for too long. Do not stay in scenic storytelling so long that the argument disappears.

The reference-model synthesis is:

- 《萬曆十五年》 teaches micro → macro;
- 《耶路撒冷三千年》 teaches continuity across long time;
- 《時間簡史》 teaches specialist → general reader.

Book 02 must combine all three without losing its own voice.

## 13. Claim discipline in prose

Different claims require different verbal temperature.

### Textual fact
Use direct wording.

### Lexical fact
Use direct wording only when lexicon/source support is strong.

### Textual observation
State what is on the page before explaining what it may imply.

### Interpretive inference
Use phrases such as:
- 可以看見
- 這裡可能暗示
- 在這個框架下
- 至少可以這樣理解

### Author hypothesis
State explicitly that this is the book's proposed reading.

### Devotional reflection
Allow first-person or direct address, but keep it distinct from textual proof.

This prevents the popular book from sounding falsely certain.

## 14. Rhetorical questions

Use fewer.

A rhetorical question is useful when it genuinely opens an unresolved problem. It is weak when it merely forces agreement.

Avoid chains such as:
- 為什麼？因為……
- 這意味著什麼？意味著……
- 難道不是嗎？

Prefer direct development when the answer is already known.

## 15. Repetition

Keep motif repetition; remove explanatory repetition.

Good repetition:
- wind returns in a scene;
- waiting returns across chapters;
- a word returns with changed meaning;
- “神很遠，神很近” returns at structural thresholds.

Bad repetition:
- restating the thesis three times with synonyms;
- summary sentence after every paragraph;
- repeated “換句話說”.

## 16. AI-smell blacklist

Use sparingly or remove unless genuinely necessary:

- 值得注意的是
- 不難發現
- 顯而易見
- 這不僅僅是……更是……
- 從某種意義上說
- 可以說
- 深刻揭示了
- 賦予了……新的意義
- 形成了一個完整的閉環
- 在這個層面上
- 某種程度上
- 讓我們回到
- 真正重要的是
- 這正是……的核心

Also avoid:
- excessive em dashes;
- mechanical three-part parallelism;
- repeated concluding aphorisms;
- artificial suspense (“答案令人震驚”);
- constant meta-signposting (“接下來我們將…”);
- fake intimacy (“親愛的讀者”).

## 17. Historical/narrative restraint

When narrating biblical or historical scenes:

- distinguish text from reconstruction;
- use concrete sensory detail sparingly;
- do not invent psychological certainty;
- if adding atmosphere, keep it supportable and non-doctrinal;
- never let invented scenic detail become evidence for a theological claim;
- when chronology is uncertain, preserve uncertainty;
- when a traditional harmonization is disputed, do not write it as uncontested fact.

## 18. Humor and irony

Low dosage. Dry, observational, never cute.

The book may notice contradiction, human limitation or institutional absurdity, but should not sound sarcastic or perform cleverness.

## 19. Theological prose

Prefer:
- image anchored in Scripture;
- argument anchored in text;
- reflection anchored in life.

Avoid stacking abstractions such as:
- 奧秘、維度、能量、頻率、層次、場域
unless the word is technically necessary and defined.

Do not borrow non-Christian mystical vocabulary to make theology sound profound.

## 20. Chapter architecture

A strong chapter should usually contain several of these movements, not necessarily in fixed order:

- scene;
- textual problem;
- lexical hinge;
- historical/biblical bridge;
- counter-reading;
- synthesis;
- quiet application;
- closing image.

The chapter should feel discovered, not mechanically outlined.

A useful longform rhythm:

**scene → question → evidence → widening horizon → resistance/objection → return to scene or image.**

## 21. Relationship to the academic appendix

Canonical rule:

**The academic paper does not merge into the main book.**

The appendix supplies:
- claim maps;
- lexical controls;
- source burden;
- scholarly positions;
- objections;
- footnotes;
- bibliography;
- argument discipline.

The popular manuscript supplies:
- story;
- cadence;
- scene;
- accessibility;
- theological nearness.

A conclusion that survives the appendix may be translated into popular prose. An academic sentence should not simply be copied into the main book.

## 22. English target

English should reproduce narrative temperature, not Chinese syntax.

Target qualities:
- plain but literary;
- concrete verbs;
- restrained modifiers;
- limited rhetorical questions;
- theological precision;
- readable handling of Hebrew/Greek;
- no evangelical marketing language;
- no generic AI cadence.

Chinese remains the primary authored text.

## 23. Editing test

Before accepting an AI-written paragraph, ask:

1. Does it add a fact, image, argument or transition?
2. Could 20% of the modifiers be removed?
3. Does it tell the reader what to feel?
4. Does it sound like a summary of a summary?
5. Is a claim stronger than its evidence?
6. Is there an unnecessary rhetorical question?
7. Does the paragraph contain a phrase common to generic AI prose?
8. Is any scenic detail invented or unsupported?
9. Did a technical term enter before the reader needed it?
10. Does the paragraph move at only one scale for too long?
11. Does it still sound like this manuscript after removing the headline/theme words?

If several answers are unfavorable, rewrite.

## 24. Canonical formula

**冷靜敘述，具體名詞，準確動詞，節制修飾；先讓事情發生，再讓意義出現。**

**大問題從小地方長出來，不靠大聲說出來。**

**學術在下面，故事在上面；敘事可以像小說，證據不能像小說。**
