# Book 02 Academic Appendix Loop

Status: ACTIVE SCHOLARLY SUBPROJECT
Parent: `dore-design/publishing/books/BOOK-02-GOD-FAR-GOD-NEAR.md`
Primary upstream loop: 黎明書局 / research / reading-writing
Source artifact: 《天國語言極簡史｜論文》

## 1. Product role

The academic paper is not prose to be blended into the main book. It is a separate scholarly appendix attached to Book 02.

Its purpose is to test, document and strengthen the book's central theological-linguistic proposal under academic conditions.

The popular book may remain readable, narrative and close to the reader. The appendix must be slower, explicit, sourced, contestable and logically auditable.

## 2. Core distinction

**Main book:** nonfiction theological narrative for general readers.

**Academic appendix:** formal theological/linguistic argument with primary texts, lexical controls, scholarly literature, objections, notes and bibliography.

The appendix must never be used to hide weaknesses in the main book. The main book must never silently upgrade an appendix hypothesis into established fact.

## 3. Loop cycle

**Claim inventory → primary text retrieval → lexical verification → scholarly literature retrieval → position map → evidence table → counterargument search → argument revision → citation/footnote pass → logical closure check → academic prose revision → updated paper draft.**

A cycle is incomplete if it only adds sources without changing the argument map.

## 4. Required claim labels

Every major claim should be classified as one of:

- TEXTUAL_FACT
- LEXICAL_FACT
- TEXTUAL_OBSERVATION
- HISTORICAL_FACT
- INTERPRETIVE_INFERENCE
- THEOLOGICAL_SYNTHESIS
- AUTHOR_HYPOTHESIS
- DEVOTIONAL_APPLICATION

The academic paper should primarily operate in the first seven classes and keep devotional application out of argumentative proof.

## 5. Primary-source burden

For claims involving Hebrew/Greek or intertextual chains, verify against primary texts rather than relying on secondary paraphrases.

Priority source families where relevant:
- Masoretic Text / critical Hebrew editions;
- Septuagint / critical Greek text;
- Greek New Testament critical editions;
- ancient translations only where germane;
- patristic or historical primary texts where a historical-reception claim is made.

Preserve exact forms, transliterations, parsing where needed, and verse/context boundaries.

## 6. Lexical controls

Use appropriate standard lexica rather than pictographic/root speculation as proof.

Potential controls include, where accessible and relevant:
- BDB;
- HALOT;
- BDAG;
- LSJ;
- Louw-Nida;
- TDNT / NIDOTTE or similar secondary theological lexica as secondary controls, not primary lexical authorities.

Ancient-letter pictographs may be discussed only as a historical/interpretive curiosity when responsibly sourced; they must not carry a lexical conclusion by themselves.

## 7. Literature review

The paper needs an explicit scholarly conversation.

For each major thesis area, collect:
- representative mainstream position(s);
- continuationist / cessationist scholarship where the issue concerns tongues;
- Pentecostal/charismatic scholarship;
- historical-critical/exegetical scholarship;
- relevant biblical-language studies;
- dissenting or contrary readings.

The paper should be able to say not only “this is my reading,” but also “this is how it differs from established readings and why.”

## 8. Argument map

Maintain a durable claim graph for the central arc:

**creation / divine speech → human language/naming → Babel → prophetic bridge → Logos/incarnation → Pentecost → Corinth → theological conclusion.**

For every arrow, record:
- exact premise;
- source;
- inferential step;
- strongest objection;
- response;
- confidence level;
- whether the next step actually depends on it.

No rhetorical continuity may substitute for a missing logical step.

## 9. Objection protocol

Search deliberately for disconfirming evidence.

Examples:
- whether `davar` can bear the specific ontological force claimed in each passage;
- whether Genesis 11 supports “withdrawal of a heavenly language” rather than linguistic diversification/judgment;
- how `saphah`, `lashon`, `glōssa`, `dialektos`, and `logos` function in their actual contexts;
- whether LXX correspondences prove conceptual identity or only translation choice;
- whether 1 Corinthians 13:1 is rhetorical/hyperbolic or ontological evidence;
- whether Acts 2 places the miracle in speech, hearing, or both;
- alternative readings of 1 Corinthians 14 and Isaiah 28;
- historical evidence for or against continuity claims.

The paper becomes more persuasive by confronting the strongest objections, not by omitting them.

## 10. Academic prose standard

The appendix uses a distinct register from the main book.

Prefer:
- defined terms;
- numbered claims;
- explicit transitions;
- exact citations;
- cautious verbs where evidence is inferential;
- tables where they clarify language relationships;
- footnotes/endnotes for source burden;
- literature-review paragraphs that distinguish scholars' positions.

Avoid:
- devotional exhortation inside proof;
- dramatic scenic reconstruction;
- unsupported certainty;
- rhetorical questions used as argument;
- inspirational conclusions that outrun the evidence.

## 11. Required durable artifacts

The loop should eventually maintain:
- `claim-inventory.md`
- `primary-text-table.md`
- `lexicon-notes.md`
- `literature-review.md`
- `objection-map.md`
- `argument-graph.md`
- `bibliography.*`
- versioned academic manuscript drafts
- change log explaining why claims were strengthened, softened, removed or reframed.

Exact file format may evolve; the evidence functions may not disappear.

## 12. Relationship to the main book

The main book can draw from conclusions that survive this loop, but must translate them into general-reader language.

The appendix should appear at the end as a clearly labeled scholarly work, not as interleaved academic chapters.

Where the appendix weakens or rejects a claim that appears in the main manuscript, that discrepancy must be surfaced for editorial revision.

## 13. Completion

This appendix is mature only when:

1. the central thesis has a complete argument map;
2. original-language claims have primary-text and lexical verification;
3. major contrary positions are represented fairly;
4. objections have explicit responses or unresolved labels;
5. citations and bibliography are academically usable;
6. unsupported claims have been removed or downgraded;
7. the conclusion states exactly what the evidence establishes and what remains proposal;
8. the paper can stand independently as a scholarly appendix.
