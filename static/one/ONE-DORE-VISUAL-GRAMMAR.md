# ONE — Doré Visual Grammar for Generated Illustration

Status: **LOCKED / REQUIRED BEFORE GENERATION**

This document governs all ONE Studio illustrations intended to live beside Gustave Doré Bible engravings. It does not ask for a generic "engraving look". It requires study of Doré's actual pictorial grammar before an image may be generated, reviewed, or registered.

`ONE-DORE-LEARNING-CURVE.md` governs how references are acquired and weighted. Its hierarchy is mandatory: canonical Doré Bible originals are Tier 1 and always outrank non-Bible Doré works for biblical interpretation and Bible-specific visual grammar. Non-Bible Doré works may supplement a specific unsolved visual problem but may never replace Scripture context or Bible-corpus precedent.

Primary visual reference set:

- the canonical 241 Doré Bible assets already fixed in ONE;
- Wikimedia Commons `Doré's Bible Illustrations` gallery (complete 1866 Bible illustration set);
- historical Doré Bible Gallery scans for comparison of plate rhythm, line density, figure scale and composition.

Secondary reference families are allowed only under the Learning Curve's transfer/anti-contamination rules. Examples include Doré's Dante, Milton, Cervantes, Coleridge, London/social observation, landscapes and securely attributed religious works. These teach transferable visual solutions—composition, value, crowd, anatomy, atmosphere, terrain, architecture—not the meaning of Scripture.

## 1. Research before prompt

Before generating a chapter image, select 3–8 Doré Bible reference plates that match the chapter's **visual problem**, not merely its subject. If a specific visual problem remains underrepresented in the Bible corpus, add carefully identified non-Bible Doré visual ancestors according to `ONE-DORE-LEARNING-CURVE.md` and record the transfer boundary.

References should be chosen by categories such as:

- horse / rider / cavalry;
- solitary prophet or apostle;
- massed crowd;
- enthroned or celestial figure;
- angelic apparition;
- city destruction;
- wilderness / mountain / sea;
- procession / battle / martyrdom;
- supernatural light;
- architectural interior.

The generation brief must record what is being borrowed from each reference at the level of composition, anatomy, gesture, value structure, linework and spatial staging. Do not simply write "in the style of Gustave Doré".

## 2. Human figure grammar

Doré figures are theatrical but structurally clear. ONE Studio must study and reproduce these recurring characteristics:

- readable silhouette before facial detail;
- elongated, weight-bearing poses rather than modern photographic casualness;
- strong contraposto, kneeling, reaching, recoil, procession and vertical blessing gestures;
- robes constructed by gravity, wind and bodily tension, not random decorative folds;
- faces usually subordinate to gesture, group direction and light;
- protagonists are separated by value, height, gesture or negative space rather than by shallow depth-of-field blur;
- secondary figures often form directional masses that lead the eye toward the principal theological action.

Reject: fashion-model anatomy, superhero anatomy, cinematic close-up acting, glossy skin, contemporary portrait posing, random crowds.

### Eyes and gaze — locked Doré grammar

At normal plate scale, Doré eyes are structural accents inside the brow/eye-socket system, not separately rendered glossy eyeballs.

Required:

- establish gaze through head turn, brow ridge, nasal bridge, upper lid and cheek structure before adding iris/pupil detail;
- place the principal dark accent as a short wedge integrated beneath the upper lid, not as an isolated circular black pupil;
- keep the lower lid incomplete and lighter than the upper lid;
- minimize visible sclera; it may be an unaccented paper tone or a narrow broken sliver, never a bright white almond;
- in three-quarter view, make the far eye naturally narrower and more absorbed by nasal/brow shadow;
- ensure both eyes share one anatomically plausible gaze and sit consistently inside the perspective of the skull;
- express grief, anger, attention and authority through the complete facial structure rather than enlarged eye openings;
- inspect every principal face at enlarged resolution before approval, even when the face reads correctly at cover size.

Reject: black-dot or black-hole pupils, separately outlined circular irises, bright eye whites, complete almond outlines, mismatched eye sizes, crossed or divergent gaze, mirrored eyes, staring digital intensity, and expressions created by eyeballs without corresponding brow/cheek/head structure.

**Hard publication gate:** a principal face with any of these eye defects remains `REVISION_REQUIRED`, regardless of whole-plate composition score or thumbnail quality.

Eye grammar remains a continuing research domain, not a completed rule set. Every new principal-face problem should add authenticated Doré evidence across different head angles, distances, ages, roles and emotions. Stable minimum prohibitions stay locked, while finer observations must continue to be tested, recorded and refined rather than forcing every figure into the same “Doré eye.”

### Hands and gesture — structure before exposure

Hands carry Scripture action but are also a high-risk generative failure area. The Studio must not make a hand large or fully exposed merely to prove that it can draw one.

Required:

- derive each hand from shoulder -> upper arm -> elbow -> forearm -> wrist before designing fingers;
- give every visible hand one explicit narrative function: command, blessing, healing, receiving, recoil, prayer, grasp, support or rest;
- choose the least exposed view that still communicates that function: side plane, three-quarter hand, partial occlusion, sleeve overlap, contact with an object/body or silhouette against a value mass;
- use a frontal open palm or foreshortened spread fingers only when Scripture and composition truly require them and authenticated Doré hand evidence has been studied;
- verify one thumb, four fingers, believable knuckle rhythm, palm width, wrist axis, tendon direction and finger overlap at enlarged resolution;
- let sleeves, shadow, another figure or the frame naturally conceal nonessential digits when full exposure adds risk but no narrative information;
- preserve the hand required by Scripture even when another supporting gesture can be simplified or concealed.

Reject: display-palm posing without textual purpose, evenly spread “fan” fingers, duplicated fingertips, merged digits, reversed thumb, extra knuckles, rubber wrist, disconnected forearm, identical paired hands and hands designed independently from the body's weight/action.

**Visibility fallback:** if repeated generation cannot solve a nonessential hand at enlarged scale, redesign the gesture so that the hand is smaller, side-on, partly occluded or absorbed into shadow. This is legitimate pictorial omission, not permission to hide a hand whose action is essential to the passage.

**Hard publication gate:** every principal hand must pass enlarged anatomy and narrative-function review. A composition score cannot compensate for a malformed or purposeless hand.

## 3. Horse grammar

When horses appear, generation must be based on Doré's actual equestrian plates rather than generic fantasy horses.

Required study points:

- compact nineteenth-century engraved anatomy: powerful chest, articulated shoulder, defined hock and fetlock, substantial neck;
- head and neck angle must participate in the emotional vector of the rider and scene;
- legs must obey believable gait and weight distribution; no duplicated or impossible limbs;
- tack is subordinate to mass and action, not modern decorative fantasy armor;
- mane and tail are rendered as directional engraved masses, not soft digital hair;
- horse and rider form one compositional gesture; a rider should not appear pasted onto a separate horse;
- battle horses frequently enter diagonally, compress foreground space, or act as a dark mass against light/smoke.

Reference categories in the Doré corpus include David/Goliath era cavalry and battle scenes, Elijah's chariot, Amorite conflict imagery, Maccabean battle imagery, and Revelation's Pale Horse.

Reject: videogame warhorse proportions, Friesian-fantasy styling, excessive armor, floating hooves, symmetrical parade posing unless Scripture specifically requires it.

## 4. Composition grammar

Doré composition is not a flat illustration centered on a subject. Common structures include:

- **monumental vertical:** small humanity beneath vast heaven, cliff, temple or apparition;
- **diagonal catastrophe:** bodies, horses, architecture, water or clouds driven across the plate;
- **processional depth:** repeated figures or architecture receding toward a focal point;
- **light aperture:** a concentrated supernatural light opening within large dark masses;
- **foreground witness:** one or a few dark figures seen from behind or in profile looking toward the central event;
- **crowd funnel:** masses arranged so shoulders, spears, hands, faces or architecture converge on the action;
- **high/low theological hierarchy:** the vertical position of figures expresses authority, revelation, judgment or dependence;
- **vast negative darkness:** darkness is active structure, not empty filler.

Every generated illustration must name its primary compositional structure before generation.

## 5. Light and value grammar

Doré's drama is built primarily from value rather than colour.

- establish a dominant black/white value plan before texture;
- supernatural light usually has a theological source and direction;
- light should strike selected faces, robes, clouds, stone, water or weapons and leave other forms submerged;
- avoid uniform gray midtones across the whole image;
- large blacks must remain readable as shaped masses;
- highlights should be earned by the narrative focus, not sprinkled decoratively;
- halos and radiant effects must integrate with cloud, line direction and atmosphere rather than resemble digital glow effects.

For ONE Revelation 1–21, generated assets remain monochrome/near-monochrome engraving. Revelation 22 is the deliberate colour exception already approved by the project.

## 6. Engraving line grammar

The objective is not a Photoshop engraving filter.

Required:

- directional hatch follows anatomy, drapery, cloud curvature, stone and terrain;
- cross-hatching increases in shadow and material density;
- line spacing varies with value and atmospheric depth;
- contours may dissolve into shadow rather than being uniformly outlined;
- distant figures and architecture simplify rather than receive equal micro-detail;
- clouds and rays use sweeping directional systems that reinforce the composition;
- image must remain legible at cover size before close inspection of line texture.

Reject: uniform vector hatching, halftone dots as the main texture, edge-detection filters, identical line density everywhere.

## 7. Architecture and landscape

Doré landscapes and cities are dramatic participants in the story.

- terrain creates staging: cliffs, valleys, city walls, stairs, arches and water direct eye movement;
- architecture should feel massive and premodern, with believable gravity and scale;
- city silhouettes use layered towers, walls, roofs and atmospheric recession rather than generic fantasy skylines;
- wilderness is rarely empty; rock, cloud, wind, path and light create spiritual pressure;
- sea and sky are structured by broad movement and value masses.

Historical plausibility matters, but literal archaeological reconstruction is secondary to Scripture fidelity and Doré's monumental biblical staging.

## 8. Angelic and supernatural figures

- supernatural presence is communicated first by scale, placement, light, gesture and environmental reaction;
- wings, when present, must have structural anatomy and directional rhythm;
- angels should not resemble soft modern fantasy characters;
- celestial figures may be calm while the surrounding human field recoils, kneels or looks upward;
- supernatural scenes must retain physical gravity in robes, bodies, clouds and architecture.

## 9. Crowd grammar

A Doré crowd is designed, not populated randomly.

- divide crowd into foreground, middle and far masses;
- establish 2–5 dominant gesture directions;
- repeat selected motifs (raised hands, staffs, helmets, bowed heads) with variation;
- preserve one visual hierarchy; not every face is equally important;
- use partial occlusion aggressively to create density;
- crowd edge and negative space should frame the protagonist/action.

## 10. Revelation-specific reference method

For each generated Revelation chapter, select references by visual grammar. Examples:

- Rev 2–3: Doré prophetic/apostolic single-figure and worship/interior staging; seven churches are not seven isolated icon cards.
- Rev 4–5: throne, heavenly hierarchy, massed worship, concentrated celestial light.
- Rev 7: innumerable crowd staging and high/low hierarchy.
- Rev 8–11: trumpet figures, catastrophe diagonals, plague/battle massing, prophetic witness.
- Rev 13–17: beast scenes require Doré's animal mass, Danielic monster grammar, crowd reaction and monumental scale rather than modern creature-concept art.
- Rev 19: white-horse rider must be studied against Doré equestrian and battle plates before generation.
- Rev 22: colour exception still preserves Doré composition, figure scale, spatial hierarchy and engraved structural drawing beneath the colour treatment.

## 11. Generation brief — mandatory fields

No generation call should be made until these are written:

1. Scripture passage and theological center.
2. Primary visible action.
3. 3–8 canonical Doré Bible reference plates.
4. Non-Bible Doré visual ancestors, if needed, with corpus tier and explicit transfer boundary.
5. Human-figure grammar reference.
6. Horse/animal reference if applicable.
7. Primary composition type.
8. Light source and value plan.
9. Foreground / middle / background structure.
10. Engraving-line strategy.
11. Explicit exclusions (modern cinema, fantasy concept art, glossy digital painting, forbidden cross-corpus transfer, etc.).
12. Cover-safe title zone without baking typography into the source image.
13. Body 5:8 plate legibility check.

## 12. Review gate

A generated image may enter `ONE_STUDIO_ASSET_REGISTRY` only when all are true:

- Scripture content is correct;
- the image resembles Doré at the level of pictorial grammar, not merely monochrome texture;
- Bible-corpus precedent remains the controlling authority and any non-Bible transfer respects the Learning Curve boundary;
- anatomy is sound;
- horses/animals pass structural review where applicable;
- figure gestures and crowd flow are intentional;
- composition has monumental depth and hierarchy;
- linework follows form;
- light has a coherent theological/narrative source;
- no obvious modern fantasy/cinematic visual language dominates;
- image remains readable both as a full-bleed cover and inside the 5:8 body plate;
- provenance remains ONE Studio and never claims Doré authorship.

If any item fails, the asset remains a draft and may be regenerated or replaced. Unlike Doré originals, ONE Studio assets are versioned editorial assets and may be revised after review.
