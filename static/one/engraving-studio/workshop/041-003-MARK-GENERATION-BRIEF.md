## Scripture reading

- Narrative unit: Jesus heals the man with the withered hand in the synagogue while opponents watch for an accusation.
- Theological center: the authority and grief of Christ confront a hardness that values accusation above mercy.
- What happens before: Sabbath controversy over the disciples plucking grain.
- What happens after: the Pharisees and Herodians begin plotting to destroy Jesus; crowds gather; the Twelve are appointed; Jesus redefines his family around obedience to God.
- Key textual details: one withered hand; the man is commanded to stand in the midst and stretch it out; hostile observers remain silent; Christ looks around with anger and grief; the hand is restored.
- Biblical cross-references: Matthew 12:9–14; Luke 6:6–11; Isaiah 42:1–4 as restraint and mercy, without importing extra visible symbols.

## Scene choice

- Exact instant to depict: after Jesus has called the man into the middle and immediately before or as the withered hand begins to open.
- Why this instant: it makes obedience, mercy, hostile silence and the coming restoration visible without turning healing into spectacle.
- Viewer position: low-to-middle eye level from behind a dark foreground shoulder at the edge of the synagogue assembly.
- Viewing distance: medium interior narrative plate.
- First visual read: the man's strained, extended hand crossing a narrow shaft of daylight toward Christ.
- Second visual read: Christ's controlled gesture and sorrowful, searching gaze around the semicircle.
- Late discoveries: folded arms, averted faces and compressed shadow reveal the watchers' refusal to answer.
- Deliberately omitted textual details: later seashore crowd, demons, appointment of the Twelve and arrival of Jesus' family; no visible miracle glow.
