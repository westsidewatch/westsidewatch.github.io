This is the first Revelation test of Doré as Westside Watch Scripture Visual Intelligence. The objective is not fast asset completion. The objective is to generate a useful first training event whose successes and failures can be written back into Doré.

## Scripture reading

### Narrative unit

Christ addresses Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamum and Thyatira. The chapter is not a travelogue of four cities; it is the risen Christ examining his churches, commending, warning, calling to repentance and promising reward to the one who overcomes.

### Theological center selected for this plate

For the first training image, focus on Ephesus: a church that has labor, endurance and discernment, yet has abandoned its first love. Christ's warning that the lampstand may be removed gives a visual action already rooted in Revelation's own imagery.

### What happens before

Revelation 1 establishes John on Patmos, the voice behind him, seven golden lampstands and the Son of Man walking among them. Doré's canonical Rev 1 plate is therefore not to be displaced, but its solitary-prophetic and revelatory atmosphere is part of the immediate visual memory.

### What happens after

The chapter moves through further messages to churches under persecution, compromise and corruption. The repeated refrain is to hear what the Spirit says to the churches and to overcome.

### Key textual details

- Christ holds the seven stars and walks among the seven golden lampstands.
- Ephesus has works, toil and endurance.
- It has abandoned its first love.
- It is commanded to remember, repent and do the works it did at first.
- The lampstand may be removed.

### Biblical cross-references shaping imagery

- Revelation 1:12–20 — canonical lampstand/Son of Man context.
- Exodus 25:31–40 — lampstand ancestry; sacred object rather than decorative candelabra.
- Zechariah 4 — lampstand imagery connected with God's Spirit and witness.

## Scene choice

### Exact instant

Not a literal city panorama and not four church tableaux. Depict a quiet, severe moment among the lampstands: the risen Christ moving among them while one lampstand's threatened place becomes the narrative tension. A small human witness, John, may occupy the dark foreground edge rather than dominate the plate.

### Why this instant

It converts Christ's evaluation of Ephesus into a visual relationship: presence, scrutiny, remembered light and threatened removal. It avoids turning the chapter into an inventory of symbols.

### Viewer position

Low and slightly behind/near the human witness, looking into a deep sacred-dark space where the principal figure and lampstands emerge through controlled light.

### Viewing distance

Medium/intimate revelation, not cosmic panorama.

### First visual read

The commanding figure moving among tall sacred lampstands.

### Second visual read

The asymmetry: one lampstand/place is under visual threat—its relationship to the figure and surrounding darkness carries unease rather than an explicit special effect.

### Late discoveries

The small witness, partial architectural/sacred space, differences among flame intensity, and the path through darkness that links the lampstands.

### Deliberate omissions

- no labeled Ephesus skyline;
- no four-city montage;
- no literal floating text/letters;
- no modern church building;
- no seven isolated icon-card lampstands;
- no attempt to illustrate every promise in Revelation 2 simultaneously.

## Subject grammar

### Principal figure

Readable silhouette and authority through stance, vertical relation and controlled light. Avoid superhero musculature, fashion portrait face or digital halo.

### Hands / face / drapery

Gesture should communicate examination and presence rather than theatrical spell-casting. Drapery follows bodily weight and movement. Face is subordinate to silhouette, gesture and value hierarchy.

### Secondary figure

John, if present, is a witness and scale reference. He does not pose toward the viewer.

### Lampstands

Sacred, weight-bearing metal objects with believable structure. Their repetition creates processional depth. They are not identical duplicated assets. Flame is small relative to the surrounding darkness and must feel physically integrated with the engraving.

## Composition

- Primary structure: processional depth + light aperture + foreground witness.
- Foreground: dark partial witness / architectural edge.
- Middle ground: Christ and nearest lampstands.
- Background: receding lampstands and darkness; architecture only as needed to stage depth.
- Negative space: active darkness around and between lampstands.
- Dominant movement: viewer eye moves from witness toward Christ, then along the lampstand sequence, then notices the threatened/asymmetric place.
- Cover-safe title zone: preserve useful darker/quiet area without designing a poster.
- 5:8 body plate: principal silhouette and lampstand rhythm must survive reduction.

## Light / value

- Theological light source: the revelatory presence of Christ plus physically modest lamp flames.
- Largest dark mass: surrounding sacred darkness / architectural depth.
- Largest light mass: concentrated around principal figure, not an all-over glow.
- Highlights: selected metal edges, robe planes, witness edge, flame tips.
- Avoid uniform gray and digital bloom.

## Engraving strategy

- hatching follows robe volume, metal curvature and architectural recession;
- deepest shadows use controlled cross-hatching and lost contours;
- distant lampstands simplify;
- flame/light is produced by surrounding value contrast rather than white digital effects;
- line direction should reinforce vertical lampstands and the movement of the principal figure.

## Explicit exclusions

- modern fantasy Jesus;
- videogame/cinematic throne-room lighting;
- seven decorative candlesticks arranged symmetrically like a product display;
- random congregation;
- Gothic cathedral unless supported by a deliberate Doré visual solution and stripped of genre contamination;
- halos rendered as digital circles/glow;
- engraving filter applied over painterly concept art;
- text, chapter number, border or Westside Watch cover ornament baked into source artwork.
