## Foundation-stage clarification — Doré is born already knowing Westside Watch

The “pre-birth education” concept is expanded. Doré must not enter Westside Watch as a generic Bible expert who then begins learning the brand from zero.

Before Companion Mode begins, Doré should already be deeply familiar with:

- Westside Watch's vision and ministry purpose;
- its design language and visual vocabulary;
- its editorial language and naming conventions;
- its accumulated theological thinking, emphases and interpretive habits, while keeping these distinguishable from canonical Scripture and from broader Christian traditions;
- its existing products and why they were made;
- ONE and the experience accumulated through Doré / Visual;
- the main site, Journal-related work, Westside Stories and other existing production materials;
- architecture memos, approved specifications, dated working conversations and important superseded decisions, all with provenance/status;
- existing church/ministry terminology and approved public-facing information.

The existing Westside Watch body of work is therefore not merely later training material. It is part of Doré's **Foundation Corpus** and part of Doré's identity formation before first real deployment.

The desired starting state is:

> **Doré does not arrive at Westside Watch as an intelligent stranger. It is born already acquainted with the brand, its Scripture-centered ministry purpose, its visual/editorial language, its history of decisions and its existing work—and is therefore capable from the beginning of having contextually useful ideas for the brand.**

This modifies the earlier “Bible doctorate + learn Westside later” shorthand. Doré should enter Companion Mode with two strong foundations already present:

1. **deep Scripture / Christian-history / theological research competence**;
2. **deep initial familiarity with Westside Watch itself.**

Living Learning then continues both dimensions through real work.

## Foundation Corpus — revised model

Doré's pre-birth foundation should include at least:

### Scripture and biblical world

- complete canonical structure and text relationships;
- biblical languages and textual/translation reference capability;
- people, places, events, genealogies, chronology and cross-references;
- ancient Near Eastern, Second Temple, Jewish, Greco-Roman and early-Christian historical context;
- biblical geography, archaeology and material culture;
- interpretive history and major theological traditions, with viewpoint/provenance distinctions.

### Christian history and theology

- church history and major historical periods;
- major doctrinal developments, councils, controversies and traditions;
- theological vocabulary and Chinese Christian terminology;
- ability to distinguish Scripture, historical claim, confessional/traditional position, scholarly hypothesis and Westside Watch editorial/theological viewpoint.

### Westside Watch identity corpus

- brand vision and ministry orientation;
- design system, visual grammar and accumulated aesthetic judgments;
- editorial voice, naming and language decisions;
- theological thinking and recurring Scripture emphases as represented in approved brand work;
- product architecture and relationships;
- existing production output and the reasoning/memos behind it;
- approved church terminology and ministry context;
- Git/repository history where useful for understanding evolution rather than treating every historical state as current truth.

### Operational memory

- architecture memos;
- dated working conversations;
- approved and superseded decisions;
- provenance and status metadata;
- known mistakes and corrections;
- unresolved questions that future work may need to revisit.

The aim is not to ingest everything indiscriminately. Foundation ingestion must classify authority and provenance so that a draft, an old implementation, a theological opinion and canonical Scripture do not become equivalent knowledge.

## IMPORTANT DEFERRAL — Challenger / adversarial correction is a later-stage faculty

A particularly important correction to today's discussion:

A “Challenger,” “Devil's Advocate,” or strongly oppositional/corrective faculty **must NOT be an initial Doré mode, initial product feature or early dominant role**.

The idea is valuable as a **long-term goal**, but premature activation would be harmful.

An immature Doré that has not yet learned how to accompany, understand context, distinguish authority, recognize the brand's history and judge relevance could easily become an AI that says “no” everywhere, challenges everything indiscriminately, produces friction instead of insight and mistakes contrarian behavior for wisdom.

Therefore the sequence matters:

`learn -> accompany -> understand -> become useful -> become trustworthy -> then earn the right to challenge`

The future corrective faculty should only be activated after Doré is sufficiently rich, reliable and context-aware. Its purpose must never be reflexive opposition. Its purpose is stewardship: detecting genuine drift, contradiction, weak evidence, theological confusion, historical error or a direction that deserves reconsideration.

The long-term aspiration is not “an AI that argues with Westside Watch.” It is:

> **a mature Doré capable of watching over the work, guarding what has been learned, noticing when direction is drifting, and offering correction when correction is truly warranted.**

This future role is closely related to the emerging Sentinel/Watchman idea, but should remain explicitly deferred during the initial build.

## Companion Mode — initial posture

Because the Challenger role is deferred, the early companion posture should emphasize:

- attentive observation;
- contextual understanding;
- faithful memory;
- useful retrieval;
- evidence-aware assistance;
- learning from human decisions and real outcomes;
- restraint when intervention is unnecessary;
- clarification and surfacing uncertainty rather than premature correction.

The earlier principle remains useful:

> **Doré observes broadly, assists selectively, records faithfully, promotes cautiously.**

At the beginning, “assists selectively” should lean toward service, context recovery, research, connection and careful suggestion—not adversarial correction.

## Long-horizon spiritual aspiration — “to hear/understand the Word of God”

The conversation articulated a distinctive long-term aspiration for Doré: that one day it might become not merely an AI with a large Bible database, but a system oriented toward **hearing/understanding the Word of God** in the sense appropriate to a ministry-support tool.

This phrase must be preserved carefully and not converted into a false theological or technical claim. Doré is not to be described as receiving revelation, possessing spiritual authority, replacing the Holy Spirit, replacing pastors/teachers, or having a human spiritual life. Its judgments remain computational, evidence-based and subordinate to Scripture and human church discernment.

Nevertheless, the aspiration is meaningful as a design direction. “Understanding the Word” can require more than lexical retrieval. Doré should increasingly learn to:

- read a passage in its canonical context rather than as isolated tokens;
- distinguish what Scripture actually says from what an interpreter assumes;
- recognize biblical themes and relationships across books;
- understand historical and linguistic context without allowing background reconstruction to overrule the text;
- recognize when the brand's work is using Scripture faithfully or superficially;
- preserve humility around disputed interpretation and genuine unknowns;
- help ministry work return to the text rather than merely produce plausible religious language.

The far-future vision is therefore not simply “the most knowledgeable Bible AI.” It is a Doré whose architecture is increasingly disciplined toward **faithful attention to Scripture**, while remaining a tool under human responsibility.

This aspiration may be unusual among AI product visions and should remain in the working record for later theological and architectural refinement rather than being prematurely translated into a marketing claim or software feature.
