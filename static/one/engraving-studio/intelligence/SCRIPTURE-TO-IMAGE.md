# Scripture-to-Image Intelligence

Status: **LIVING NOTEBOOK**

This file accumulates what the Studio learns about turning biblical text into pictorial narrative. It is not a list of prompt tricks.

## Initial principles

1. **Read the narrative unit, not an isolated verse.** A plate may depict one verse while depending on what occurs before and after it.
2. **Select a moment before selecting objects.** First decide which instant carries the passage; only then decide which figures, architecture, animals or symbols belong in frame.
3. **Theological center controls visual hierarchy.** The largest or brightest object is not automatically the most important; scale, placement, gesture, light and negative space must express the passage's actual center.
4. **Omission is interpretation.** Do not illustrate every noun in the text. Record what is deliberately left outside the frame and why.
5. **Choose viewing distance from the text.** Scripture may call for a hand, face, doorway or small encounter rather than spectacle. Cosmic subject matter does not automatically require a panoramic composition.
6. **Secondary figures are witnesses, not filler.** Every background group must perform a narrative function.
7. **Sequence matters.** Ask what the viewer should discover first, second and later. A plate can unfold like reading.
8. **Symbol and physical world must coexist.** Apocalyptic and prophetic imagery still needs gravity, anatomy, space and coherent visual relationships.
9. **Cross-reference before invention.** Biblical imagery often has visual ancestry inside Scripture itself: Revelation draws on Daniel, Ezekiel, Isaiah, Exodus, Psalms and Genesis. The Studio should understand those textual relationships before designing a creature or scene.
10. **Do not modernize the text by accident.** Contemporary cinema, game, fashion, military or fantasy vocabulary can silently reinterpret Scripture even when all requested objects are present.

## Questions before every scene

- What is this passage actually doing?
- What changes between the beginning and end of the unit?
- Which single instant best reveals that change?
- Where is God / Christ / the prophetic word in the visual hierarchy?
- Who sees, hears, responds, resists, worships, flees or waits?
- What can remain unseen but still be felt?
- Would a smaller detail communicate the passage more truthfully than a larger spectacle?
- Which Old/New Testament visual relationships should inform the scene?

## Promotion rule

A lesson enters this notebook after repeated corpus evidence or a clear generation/review finding. More specific Doré drawing rules belong in specialist grammars; stable project architecture belongs in the master policy.
