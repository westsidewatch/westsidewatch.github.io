# 025-003 — Lamentations 3 — ONE Studio Generation Brief

Status: GENERATION BRIEF / NOT YET APPROVED

## Scripture and theological center

Primary passage: Lamentations 3:1–66, with the visual turn centered on 3:21–24 and 3:31–33.

Theological center: the afflicted speaker remains inside judgment, grief and ruin, yet deliberately recalls the LORD's steadfast love, mercy and faithfulness. Hope appears inside darkness; the chapter does not erase suffering or become triumphant.

## Primary visible action

A solitary afflicted Judean figure sits/kneels low among the ruined stones of Jerusalem at the end of night. His body is still bent by grief, but his head and upper torso begin to turn toward a narrow opening of first morning light beyond/above the ruins. The visual instant is the inward turn of Lamentations 3:21: "Yet this I call to mind and therefore I have hope."

Do not literalize abstract mercy as a person or object. Do not depict a victorious sunrise over a restored city. Jerusalem remains broken.

## Canonical Doré Bible visual ancestors

Tier 1 references controlling the plate:

1. Doré 127 — *The People Mourning Over the Ruins of Jerusalem*: ruined-Jerusalem environment, mourning bodies, devastated masonry, historical continuity with Lamentations 1/2/4/5 in ONE.
2. Doré 125 — *Jeremiah*: solitary prophetic body, robe mass, inward gravity, prophet-in-darkness figure grammar.
3. Doré 121 — *Job and His Friends*: bodily affliction, lowered human scale, grief expressed through posture rather than theatrical facial close-up.
4. Doré 085 — *David Mourning the Death of Absalom*: compressed grief, bowed body, darkness as emotional structure.
5. Doré 114 — *Ezra in Prayer*: kneeling/prayer posture and human dependence within sacred-historical ruin/recovery context.

These are structural/visual ancestors, not claims that the Lamentations narrator is literally Jeremiah in the image.

## Non-Bible Doré references

None required for first generation. Tier 1 Bible corpus sufficiently covers grief, ruined city, solitary prophetic figure, prayer posture and theological light.

## Human-figure grammar

One dominant foreground/midground figure; weight-bearing kneel/sit, shoulders collapsed but not melodramatically contorted. Robes must obey gravity and stone contact. Face secondary to silhouette, hands and turn of the head.

Optional distant mourners may remain as very small shadow masses only if they reinforce the ruined-city context; no random crowd.

## Primary composition

LIGHT APERTURE + VAST NEGATIVE DARKNESS + LOW/HIGH THEOLOGICAL HIERARCHY.

The afflicted figure occupies the lower third/center-left or center-right, enclosed by broken masonry and large dark masses. A narrow opening of dawn/light enters from the upper distance, not as a full bright sky. Ruins form a funnel toward the light while still visually dominating the plate.

## Light and value plan

Monochrome / near-monochrome Doré engraving.

- 70–80% of the plate remains dark or middle-dark.
- Deep blacks in ruined foreground masonry and robe shadow.
- A concentrated pale first-light aperture touches only selected edges of face/shoulder/stone.
- The light is restrained: hope has entered, but morning has not yet flooded the city.
- No digital glow, halo, sun disk, radiant fantasy beam or decorative sparkle.

## Foreground / middle / background

Foreground: broken stones, dark masonry fragments, possibly one sharply lit stone edge; no decorative props.

Middle: solitary afflicted figure, readable silhouette, hands resting/clasped naturally rather than raised in victory.

Background: devastated Jerusalem architecture/walls receding into shadow; one narrow opening of pale dawn above/beyond the ruin.

## Engraving-line strategy

- dense directional cross-hatching in robe folds and masonry shadow;
- broken irregular stone hatch, not uniform texture;
- long directional atmospheric lines drawing the eye toward the light aperture;
- distant city simplified with atmospheric recession;
- strongest line density around the human figure and dark ruin masses;
- light region created primarily by absence/reduction of line, not white digital bloom.

## Explicit exclusions

- no modern cinematic lens language;
- no fantasy-concept-art ruined city;
- no superhero prophet anatomy;
- no sentimental devotional-poster sunrise;
- no restored Jerusalem / New Jerusalem imagery;
- no angel unless Scripture scene choice requires one (it does not here);
- no cross, church building or anachronistic Christian symbol;
- no text, verse lettering, title or gold frame baked into the source plate;
- do not simply duplicate Doré 127; the new image must depict the chapter's inward turn from affliction toward hope.

## Cover / plate safety

Compose as a vertical 5:8 master plate. Keep the principal face/gesture away from extreme edges. Maintain enough quiet value area in the upper region for ONE's separate overlay/title/frame system without baking typography into the artwork. The image must remain legible both full-bleed and as a framed 5:8 body plate.

## Lost Plate Test target

The result should plausibly sit beside Doré 125/127 as a missing Lamentations plate: same biblical picture-world, same gravity, same value-driven storytelling, but with a distinct narrative idea — darkness still dominates while a restrained first light makes the chapter's remembered hope visible.
