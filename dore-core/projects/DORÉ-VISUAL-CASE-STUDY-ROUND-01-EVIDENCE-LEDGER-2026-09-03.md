## Current quality judgment

The learning design is strong because it deliberately compares conflicting successful systems and distinguishes utility, discovery and immersive editorial pacing. The hypotheses are useful and already align with later Westside principles such as variable visual intensity, curated entrances over raw database exposure, source-connected editorial curation and different interaction pace by product.

The weakness is evidence maturity: the file records a well-designed learning agenda plus first synthesis, not a completed research round. Treating the named references or seven hypotheses as proof of finished design literacy would violate the source's own completion contract.

## Durable learning retained

1. `database != entrance`; deep structure can sit behind a curated first encounter.
2. Search, curated discovery and editorial interpretation can coexist as distinct modes.
3. Strong identity does not require applying the strongest motif everywhere.
4. Cards belong mainly to discovery/choice; deep reading should reduce fragmentation.
5. The same archive can support formal taxonomy, themes, people, stories and reader-need entry paths.
6. Editorial interpretation should remain connected to source objects and provenance.
7. Visual pace should differ across Search, Library and Journal while preserving one recognisable grammar.

These remain hypotheses / learning principles until tested in real Westside prototypes where the governing source requires that evidence.
