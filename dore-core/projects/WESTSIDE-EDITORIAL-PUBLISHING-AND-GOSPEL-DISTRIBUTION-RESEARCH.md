## 1. Editorial precision thesis

Westside Watch distinguishes independent-magazine design from generic traditional-media and generic website design. The target is not minimalism for its own sake. Independent editorial design may be complex-precise or simple-precise; precision comes from exact content positioning, reader positioning, editorial voice, typography, image treatment, pacing, materiality, and issue identity.

A working thesis from The Gentlewoman study: independent editorial design magnifies design judgment until the publication is recognizable and memorable at a glance. Westside Journal should therefore be understood as an intentional magnification of the broader Westside Watch visual language, not as an unrelated sub-brand. The main site contains the grammar; Journal intensifies it.

Research requirements: The Gentlewoman and comparable leading independent magazines; editorial grids; typography; art direction; cover systems; pacing; paper/material/printing/binding; commissioning; photography/illustration; issue architecture; bilingual Chinese/English typography; collectible-object logic; digital-to-print and print-to-digital relationships.

## 2. Christian publishing: study content before copying appearance

Do not assume existing Christian magazines are visual models for Westside Watch. Study them primarily for editorial content architecture, theological/editorial scope, recurring departments, commissioning, audience, frequency, distribution, church/community relationship, funding, and longevity.

Initial cases include Imprint Magazine (Toronto), Ex Fonte, Christianity Today, Faith Today, Evangelical Magazine, Christian Culture and other Bible/Christian periodicals discovered by Doré. Christian Culture is also a useful special case because its print/online identity explicitly uses historical woodcuts; compare its method with Westside Watch's much broader Doré engraving-source-grammar project without copying it.

Research question: Is there a Chinese- or English-language Christian publication/site that truly behaves like a contemporary independent magazine rather than a conventional ministry magazine? If the answer remains 'few or none', document the evidence and treat the gap as a possible Westside Watch opportunity rather than assuming uniqueness prematurely.

## 3. Chinese/Asian independent editorial design corpus

Build a serious corpus from Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Japan, Chinese-language publishing in Canada and elsewhere. Study both living publications and historically influential independent titles.

Seed cases: TO Magazine (Japan), Science of the Secondary (Singapore), Jiazazhi/假杂志, QDY Mag (Taiwan), IDEA (Japan), BranD (Hong Kong), and further titles discovered through Stack, Golden Pin, design institutions, bookstores and publishers.

For each case record: editorial proposition; reader; cover logic; grid; typography; Chinese/Latin/Japanese handling; photography/illustration; white space/density; recurring visual grammar; material production; issue frequency; distribution; business/survival model; what Westside should learn; what Westside should explicitly reject.

## 4. Independent magazine survival/business research

Design cannot be separated from survival. Study how independent magazines remain alive without becoming generic traffic media.

Model categories to compare:
- paid subscription / premium subscription;
- single-issue and specialist bookstore/newsstand sales;
- membership/donation/supporter models;
- advertising/sponsorship with editorial boundaries;
- events, talks, conferences and exhibitions;
- books, back issues, merchandise and limited editions;
- shops/cafes/physical spaces where relevant;
- creative studio/agency adjacency;
- grants/institutional/church support;
- digital membership/newsletters;
- print as collectible physical artefact rather than disposable news carrier.

Do not optimize Westside solely for revenue. Produce a sustainability model compatible with ministry purpose, editorial independence, realistic production costs, and the Great Commission.

## 5. Gospel distribution / attention systems as a Doré curriculum

Doré must learn ethical digital distribution as part of becoming a networked gospel-serving AI. This is not merely 'SEO' and must not become engagement manipulation.

Research and learn:

- recommendation/ranking systems;
- content graphs and Scripture-resource relationships;
- social/referral distribution;
- newsletter/subscription/return pathways;
- audience segmentation without coercive profiling;
- accessibility, performance and mobile reach;
- multilingual discovery;

- experimentation/A-B testing where appropriate;
- privacy, consent and data minimization;
- anti-spam and anti-dark-pattern principles;
- evaluation of whether distribution actually leads to useful Scripture/gospel engagement rather than empty clicks.

### Mission metric hierarchy

Traffic is a means, not the mission. Doré should distinguish:
1. Reach — was the material discoverable?
2. Useful engagement — did the visitor actually receive the promised help/content?
3. Depth — did the interaction naturally lead to Scripture, understanding, relevant resources, or continued study?
4. Return/relationship — did the person choose to return, subscribe, study further, or enter another appropriate Westside surface?
5. Gospel opportunity — when context calls for it, could Doré faithfully explain the gospel and respond wisely to questions/challenges?

Never claim conversion from analytics. Never optimize spiritual pressure, outrage, addiction, deceptive click-through, or manufactured controversy.

## 6. One distribution architecture across products

Liming Library, Journal, Daily Devotional, ONE, Search, Westside Stories and future Doré Conversation should not each invent isolated traffic logic. Build a shared ethical distribution/attention layer while preserving each product's editorial purpose.

Working flow:
`external discovery → clear visual/editorial entry → useful content → contextual related path → Scripture/resource graph → optional deeper study/conversation/community`

Search is a special research priority because it may become the first public place where a visitor moves from retrieval into genuine conversation with Doré. Design and ranking must therefore support both excellent retrieval and non-coercive pathways toward Scripture and gospel explanation.

## Initial evidence notes

- Contemporary independent publishing shows multiple survival models rather than one formula: premium subscriptions, newsstand discovery, events and adjacent retail/brand activity can coexist.
- Asian independent editorial practice provides strong examples of culturally specific typography and editorial identity rather than simply importing Anglo-American magazine conventions.
- Christian Culture explicitly combines print/online publication with historical woodcut imagery, making it useful as a comparison case for how religious editorial identity can use inherited visual material.
- Imprint Magazine in Toronto publishes an annual print issue and then releases selected pieces online, a useful print/digital division to study for Westside.

All hypotheses remain subject to deeper Doré research and real Westside prototypes.
