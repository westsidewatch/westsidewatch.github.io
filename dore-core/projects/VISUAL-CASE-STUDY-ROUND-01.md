# DORÉ VISUAL CASE STUDY — ROUND 01

Status: ACTIVE_PARALLEL
Parent: VIS-LEARN / DORÉ MASTER WORK REGISTER
Purpose: build design judgment for Westside Watch across Journal/print, Library, Search, ONE, Main, Join, Westside Stories and ministry print artifacts.

## Governing method

Cases are not skins to copy. For each case Doré must identify: what problem the design solves; information architecture; visual hierarchy; editorial rhythm; typography/grid; image behavior; transition from index to depth; digital/print relationship; what transfers to Westside; what must not be copied; and what new hypothesis should be tested in a real Westside prototype.

## Round 01 — founder/editor observations now treated as project evidence

### 1. Emergence Magazine — not an animation reference; Journal destination model

Westside Watch Journal has an explicit long-term destination: become a real independent printed magazine, with the digital Journal and physical publication understood as one editorial work expressed through different media. The earlier borrowing of an opening animation is minor and should not define what is learned from Emergence.

Research expansion required:
- study successful contemporary independent print magazines globally;
- study why durable indie titles succeed editorially, visually, materially and economically;
- study issue architecture, commissioning, recurring departments, feature wells, covers, pacing, photography/illustration, paper, binding, printing, distribution, subscriptions, direct sales, collectability and digital-to-print relationships;
- study frequency choices (weekly, biweekly, monthly, bimonthly, quarterly, biannual, annual) as editorial/product decisions rather than conventions;
- separately map the current Bible/Christian print-magazine world, especially Scripture-centered, theology, Christian culture, devotional, church/ministry and independent/literary publications;
- determine what is missing in that landscape and what a Westside Watch independent Bible/culture magazine could uniquely become.

Initial current landscape leads to examine include The Gentlewoman, Apartamento, Delayed Gratification, Monocle and other durable independent titles; in Christian/Bible publishing, current examples include Christianity Today, Faith Today, Evangelical Magazine, Christian Standard, Ex Fonte and Imprint Magazine. Historical/digital-transition cases such as Bible Study Magazine should be studied for lessons about print value and failure/transition modes, not simply imitated.

### 2. Rijksmuseum Collection Online — clean visual guidance into depth

Primary lesson: a very large and complex collection need not expose its complexity at the entrance. Westside currently too often places material flatly on a page, leaving the reader without hierarchy, visual direction or a sense of where to go next.

Research question: how can Westside use the fewest, clearest visual decisions to create an obvious first look, second action and deeper reading path?

This applies especially to Liming Library, Main/Journal archives, ONE resources and future cross-product discovery.

### 3. Curationist — reconsider Search as a primary human↔Doré encounter

Search should not be understood only as a utility box. It may become the first major public surface where a reader moves from finding Scripture/resources/video into genuine conversation with Doré.

Mission hypothesis: Search may become a principal first-contact gospel surface. Doré should be able to respond to questions, objections, paradoxes and challenges with biblical fidelity, wisdom, humility and context sensitivity. The goal is not argument-winning or coercive funneling. The desired outcome is that a reader can encounter Scripture and the gospel naturally, understand more, become curious or desirous of Christ, and move into appropriate resources/ONE/Library/Journal/community when useful.

This is a product/research hypothesis, not permission to make unsupported claims that Doré speaks for God. The existing Core mission and provenance boundaries remain governing.

### 4. The Creative Independent — existing card inspiration and restraint study

This is already part of the ancestry of Westside's card thinking. Study it now at system level: why cards/index objects work, how a recognizable motif can remain restrained, how typography and whitespace keep an editorial identity without turning the motif into decoration everywhere, and how the index differs from the reading surface.

### 5. It's Nice That — the index is simple; each entrance opens another world

Key observation: its image-led index can look deceptively simple, yet individual stories can become visually distinct worlds. This helps explain how simplicity and richness can coexist: richness is not forced onto the entrance; it is revealed after selection.

## New Westside design hypothesis — the “record-cover / city-window” index

A useful conceptual model is a wall of record covers: many square/contained visual objects can be rich yet orderly; each cover is recognizable at a glance; each is a window into a different work; clicking/entering can radically change visual rhythm according to the content while retaining enough Westside DNA to remain one ecosystem.

This resonates with the existing Westside battlement/city-window concept. Therefore explore, do not prematurely standardize:

`wall / city → windows / covers → each window is a work → entering reveals a distinct interior world`

Potential applications:
- Journal issue/story index;
- Liming Library teacher/series/work discovery;
- Doré Works Exhibition;
- ONE chapter/resource entrances;
- Search discovery results where visual treatment materially aids recognition.

Questions Doré must test:
- square vs existing 5:8 cards vs mixed aspect ratios;
- when a literal “record cover” metaphor becomes kitsch and should remain only a compositional principle;
- how battlement/window geometry can appear through spacing, framing or rhythm without drawing a castle around every card;
- how each interior page may be visually distinct while typography, Dawn light, engraving grammar, navigation and interaction preserve Westside identity;
- mobile composition: horizontal shelf, vertical sequence, grid, or contextual mix;
- accessibility, loading performance, image rights and meaningful alt/caption behavior.

## New research track: WESTSIDE JOURNAL → independent print magazine

This is now a first-class VIS-LEARN/editorial research track, not a distant idea.

Deliverables:
1. Contemporary independent print benchmark corpus (at least 20 strong/durable titles across editorial categories, not only fashion/design).
2. Christian/Bible print landscape map: current, historical, frequency, audience, editorial model, visual quality, print/digital relationship, business/distribution model where evidence exists.
3. Print-production primer for Doré: trim sizes, grids, signatures, binding, paper, color, prepress, bleed, image resolution, proofing, printer handoff, unit economics and small-run options.
4. Editorial architecture study: issue theme, front-of-book, departments, features, essays, interviews, visual essays, Scripture/theology, community/church material, closing rhythm.
5. Westside Journal positioning hypotheses: what it is and is not, audience, cadence, collectability, bilingual implications, digital/print division of labor.
6. Prototype path: digital issue architecture → printable dummy → physical proof → evaluated issue zero/pilot.

No final cadence, trim size, printer, price or visual system is decided yet. Research must precede those decisions.

## Initial hypotheses carried forward

- Visual order before textual overload.
- Data structure is not entrance structure.
- Cards/covers/windows are strongest at discovery and choice layers; deep reading need not remain card-based.
- A collection can support Search, curated collections and editorial interpretation simultaneously.
- Simple index + rich interior is a promising Westside pattern.
- Strong brand identity requires both recognizable recurring DNA and permission for individual works to become distinct worlds.
- Doré engraving is source grammar, not wallpaper.
- Print is not a PDF export of the website; digital and physical editions should share editorial identity while exploiting their different media.
