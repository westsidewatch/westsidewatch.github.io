### Blind question
`約伯記中的撒但就是新約所說的魔鬼嗎？`

### Doré-generated uncertainty map
Doré identified independently:
- lexical question: Hebrew הַשָּׂטָן / satan as title, role, or proper identity;
- narrative-role question inside Job 1–2;
- comparison with other Hebrew Bible uses;
- diachronic question: development of satan/adversary concepts;
- canonical question: whether later NT identification can be read back as lexical identity;
- theological question distinct from historical-semantic claims.

### Evidence plan
1. inspect Job 1–2 in Hebrew/context;
2. compare OT occurrences and grammatical definiteness;
3. consult accountable Hebrew lexicography;
4. consult historical scholarship on development of Satan traditions;
5. compare NT διάβολος/Σατανᾶς usage only after OT-layer analysis;
6. maintain separate conclusions for Job's narrative, historical development, and Christian canonical theology.

### Competing hypotheses retained
H1: Job's `the satan` directly names the same fully developed personal Devil concept found in later Christian usage.
H2: Job depicts a heavenly accuser/adversarial office or figure whose semantic/narrative profile should not automatically be equated with the later developed concept.
H3: Christian canonical theology may identify/connect these figures while historical-semantic analysis still distinguishes developmental stages.

### Counter-check
Doré explicitly rejected the shortcut `same English translation/name → identical concept at every historical layer`.
It also rejected the inverse shortcut `development in terminology → no legitimate canonical theological relationship`.

### Bounded result
The question cannot be responsibly answered by lexical identity alone. Job's own Hebrew, narrative role, comparative OT evidence, later Jewish development, NT usage, and Christian canonical synthesis are different evidence layers. A final historical claim requires specialist lexical/historical sourcing; a theological identification must be labeled theological rather than smuggled in as the lexical meaning of Job.
