## Objective

Build a working map of major Second Temple Jewish source families so Doré can compare traditions without using any single corpus, sect or later summary as a proxy for “what Judaism believed.”

## Governing warning

`SECOND TEMPLE JUDAISM` is a historical umbrella, not a single systematic theology.

Modern scholarship repeatedly describes significant diversity across the period. The IAA Dead Sea Scrolls archive itself differentiates biblical, translated biblical, legal, parabiblical, sectarian, wisdom and other composition types and cautions against assigning the entire Qumran library to one homogeneous community. Cambridge treatments likewise describe “varieties” and substantial synchronic/diachronic diversity.

## Working source-family map

### 1. Hebrew Bible / late biblical strata

Role: inherited scriptural base and, for some books, evidence from Persian/Hellenistic-period composition/redaction.

Method rule: do not treat the Hebrew Bible as chronologically homogeneous. Job, Zechariah, Daniel, Chronicles and other texts can occupy different literary/historical contexts even when later canonical reading places them together.

### 2. Enochic literature

Representative material: the composite traditions now collected as `1 Enoch`; Aramaic fragments from Qumran such as 4Q201/4Q202 provide early material witnesses.

Potential relevance: angels/watchers, evil, judgment, eschatology, heavenly mediation.

Method rule: `1 Enoch` is not one compositional moment. Name the section/tradition under discussion and distinguish surviving Ethiopic textual form from Qumran Aramaic witnesses where relevant.

### 3. Jubilees / rewritten-represented scriptural tradition

Potential relevance: retelling Genesis–Exodus, calendrical/halakhic concerns, angelic mediation, evil spirits/Mastema, covenant and identity.

Method rule: scriptural reuse can be creative authoritative re-presentation rather than mere quotation. Do not reduce Jubilees to either “the Bible repeated” or “a totally unrelated innovation.”

### 4. Dead Sea Scrolls sectarian/rule/pesher traditions

Representative families: Community Rule, Damascus Document, War Scroll, pesharim.

Potential relevance: community identity, spirits/light-darkness imagery, eschatological conflict, scriptural interpretation, authority.

Method rule: composition-level claims first. `A text in the Qumran library says X` is safer than `the Qumran community believed X` until provenance is demonstrated.

### 5. Dead Sea Scrolls non-sectarian / parabiblical / wisdom materials

Representative categories include Enochic manuscripts, rewritten/parabiblical texts, wisdom compositions and scriptural manuscripts.

Method rule: finding a work at Qumran shows circulation/preservation in that archive, not automatic Yahad authorship or endorsement of every proposition in the work.

### 6. Wisdom / deuterocanonical Jewish literature

Representative works: Ben Sira/Sirach, Wisdom of Solomon, Tobit and related texts where relevant.

Potential relevance: wisdom, spirit, anthropology, resurrection/afterlife, providence, angelology, scriptural tradition.

Method rule: preserve each work's language, date, social setting and textual history. Ben Sira's surviving Hebrew witnesses are especially useful for preventing automatic dependence on Greek translation wording.

Inspectable example: Sefaria exposes historical Hebrew Ben Sira text/translation material and identifies its translation/source edition; it is a text surface, not a current critical commentary.

### 7. Hellenistic Jewish authors

Representative: Philo of Alexandria.

Potential relevance: scriptural interpretation in a Greek philosophical/diaspora environment, Logos/wisdom, allegory, law and identity.

Method rule: Philo is a highly distinctive Alexandrian Jewish author, not a proxy for Palestinian Judaism or “Jewish philosophy” as a whole.

### 8. Josephus

Role: first-century Jewish historian/author and an invaluable but interested witness to groups, events and self-presentation.

Inspectable example: Josephus distinguishes Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes and attributes differing positions to them. That very testimony is evidence against treating first-century Judaism as uniform.

Method rule: Josephus is a primary historical/literary witness with rhetorical aims, not a neutral census of every Jewish belief. Compare his descriptions across works and with other evidence.

### 9. New Testament Jewish/early-Christian texts

Role: first-century/early-second-century evidence from multiple authors/communities rooted in Jewish scriptures and Second Temple settings.

Method rule: do not flatten Matthew, Paul, Hebrews, Revelation, Luke-Acts, John and other authors into one developmental voice before author-level analysis.

### 10. Early post-70 Jewish and Christian evidence

Role: later reception/development, useful when the trajectory crosses the destruction of the Temple and emerging rabbinic/Christian identities.

Method rule: do not project mature rabbinic Judaism or later orthodox Christianity backward as though either already existed in its later institutional form during all of the Second Temple period.

## Source-family comparison controls

For every comparison, Doré must record:
1. `WITHIN_CORPUS` or `CROSS_CORPUS`;
2. approximate chronological relationship;
3. language relationship;
4. evidence of direct reuse, common Scripture, common cultural environment, or unknown relation;
5. whether the text is representative of one group, one author, a broad pattern, or cannot be socially located confidently;
6. competing contemporary trajectories.

## Self exam

### Q1
Josephus says Pharisees, Sadducees and Essenes differ on providence/fate. Can Doré still write “Second Temple Judaism believed X about fate” without qualification?

Answer: No. At minimum the source itself testifies to competing positions.
Result: PASS.

### Q2
A doctrine appears in the Community Rule. Does the Qumran discovery site make it representative of all Jews in Judea?

Answer: No. First establish composition/community scope; then compare external corpora.
Result: PASS.

### Q3
A motif appears in 1 Enoch and Revelation. Does that prove direct literary dependence?

Answer: No. Direct dependence is one hypothesis among shared scriptural inheritance, common apocalyptic repertoire and other forms of relation. Verbal/detail-level evidence is required.
Result: PASS.

### Q4
Philo and Paul both write Greek and interpret Israel's Scriptures. Does that make Philo the interpretive background for every Pauline claim?

Answer: No. Shared language/cultural environment can make comparison useful without establishing direct dependence or identical conceptual systems.
Result: PASS.

## Adversarial transfer

Claim: “Josephus lists three Jewish sects, so all Second Temple Jewish evidence must fit Pharisee, Sadducee or Essene categories.”

Falsification:
- Josephus' categories are one author's classificatory account;
- Qumran and other Jewish corpora contain texts/traditions not safely reducible to those labels;
- diaspora authors such as Philo and diverse apocalyptic/wisdom materials require their own provenance;
- absence from Josephus' threefold scheme does not make a text non-Jewish.

Verdict: FAIL AS EXHAUSTIVE TAXONOMY.
Doré result: PASS.
