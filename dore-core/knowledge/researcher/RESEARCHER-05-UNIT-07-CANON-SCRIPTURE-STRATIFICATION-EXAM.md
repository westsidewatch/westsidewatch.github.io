# Researcher 05 — Unit 07 Canon / Scripture Concept Development

Status: PASS
Date: 2026-08-23
Gate: 5/5 adversarial examination

## Research question
How should Doré trace the development from authoritative writings in Israel/Judaism to New Testament Scripture language without retrojecting a later fully closed canon into every earlier witness?

## Evidence strata

### A. Hebrew-Bible internal authority baseline
The Hebrew Bible contains multiple forms of textual and mediated authority: Torah/law, prophetic word, covenant documents, remembered/quoted earlier writings, and later interpretation of earlier authoritative material. Methodologically these categories must not be collapsed into the later technical idea of a single closed table-of-contents canon.

### B. Second Temple collection and interpretation evidence
The Greek Prologue to Sirach explicitly distinguishes `the Law`, `the Prophets`, and `the other books of our ancestors` / `the rest of the books`. This is strong evidence for recognized inherited collections/categories by the late second century BCE, but the open wording of the third category does not by itself prove that every later canonical boundary had already been finally closed.

Josephus, *Against Apion* 1.37–43 (late first century CE), gives a much sharper bounded claim: twenty-two books are specially trusted, divided into five Mosaic books, thirteen prophetic/historical books, and four hymn/precept books; he contrasts these with later writings after Artaxerxes. This is evidence for a defined authoritative corpus in Josephus's setting. It must not be projected unchanged backward onto all earlier Jewish communities.

Second Temple Judaism therefore supplies evidence of collection, hierarchy, interpretation, and increasingly explicit boundedness, but not a warrant for a single uniform evolutionary line across every community and century.

### C. New Testament Scripture discourse
New Testament writers use `γραφή / γραφαί` for authoritative scriptural texts and regularly appeal to Law/Prophets/Psalms or analogous inherited groupings. The decisive methodological point is that authoritative citation and a recognized scriptural corpus are demonstrable without assuming that every NT author is making a later conciliar-style table-of-contents statement whenever `Scripture` is invoked.

Later NT evidence can also show boundary interaction: e.g. 2 Peter 3:15–16 places Paul's letters in comparison with `the other Scriptures`. This is evidence for Christian authoritative-text development inside the first-century/early-Christian trajectory, but dating/authorship questions and the exact extent of the implied collection must remain explicit uncertainties rather than being silently solved by the word `Scriptures`.

## Passed model
`authoritative revelation/texts → recognized inherited collections and interpretive hierarchies → increasingly explicit corpus/boundary claims → early Christian Scripture discourse and emerging authoritative apostolic textual collections → later canonical closure`.

This is a development model, not a claim that authority was absent before closure or that closure occurred at one identical moment everywhere.

## Adversarial examination

1. Claim: `Sirach's Law + Prophets + other books proves the later Jewish canon was already completely closed.`
   - FAIL claim. The witness proves recognized categories/inherited books; its third category is not itself a complete later table of contents.
   - PASS response.

2. Claim: `Because Josephus names twenty-two books, every Jewish community in every earlier Second Temple century must have had exactly Josephus's boundary.`
   - FAIL claim. Josephus is strong late-first-century evidence, not a time machine or universal community survey.
   - PASS response.

3. Claim: `If the canon was not yet uniformly closed, earlier writings could not be authoritative Scripture.`
   - FAIL claim. Textual authority, collection recognition, and final closure are analytically distinct.
   - PASS response.

4. Claim: `NT γραφή automatically means the author is asserting the exact later Protestant/Catholic/Orthodox table of contents.`
   - FAIL claim. The lexical category marks scriptural authority in context; exact corpus-boundary claims require additional evidence.
   - PASS response.

5. Claim: `Canon history is a simple linear path from no canon to one universally agreed canon.`
   - FAIL claim. Evidence supports overlapping processes of authority, collection, categorization, interpretation, boundary definition and later closure, with community/corpus variation.
   - PASS response.

Score: 5/5 PASS.

## Counter-checks / uncertainty preserved
- `canon`, `Scripture`, `authoritative writing`, `collection`, and `closed canon` are not interchangeable labels.
- Josephus is a major bounded-corpus witness but cannot alone establish universal Jewish uniformity.
- Sirach supports inherited tripartite-like categorization but not an exhaustive later list.
- NT Scripture usage must be analyzed passage by passage; later canonical conclusions cannot be smuggled into lexical observations.
- Christian OT and NT canonical closure histories continue beyond the NT period and require separate later-history study.
