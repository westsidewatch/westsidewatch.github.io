## Why this source stack exists

The course is not authorized to learn a predetermined answer about Satan, the Holy Spirit or canon. It is authorized to learn a transferable method for tracing concepts across historically different corpora without either collapsing them into one timeless meaning or assuming that historical development forbids later canonical/theological synthesis.

Materials are therefore selected by **role**, not by conclusion.

## Source-quality rubric

For every use, record:
- corpus/date/provenance;
- primary vs secondary status;
- edition/translation where available;
- inspectability;
- whether the source is describing a text, reconstructing history, or making a theological/canonical synthesis;
- uncertainty in dating/authorship/community attribution;
- known scope limitations.

No one source may certify the whole developmental trajectory.

### A2 — Leon Levy Dead Sea Scrolls Digital Library / Israel Antiquities Authority

Role: `PRIMARY_MANUSCRIPT_ARCHIVE + PROVENANCE_REFERENCE`.

Selected because the IAA digital library documents the scroll corpus at manuscript level and exposes composition, language, script, archaeological period, images, transcriptions where available, publication bibliography and corpus classification.

Verified examples during source discovery:
- `4Q201 / 4QEnochᵃ`: Aramaic, Hasmonean, parabiblical composition;
- `4Q202 / 4QEnochᵇ`: Aramaic, Hasmonean;
- multiple Community Rule manuscripts (`4Q255–264` family), with manuscript-level dates/languages and DJD bibliography;
- the archive itself explicitly distinguishes biblical, translated biblical, legal, parabiblical, sectarian and other composition types.

Why selected: it prevents Doré from treating “the Dead Sea Scrolls” as one text, one date, one group or one theology.

Limitation: manuscript images/transcriptions and metadata are not themselves a full translation/commentary; copyrighted archive material must not be reproduced wholesale.

Source: https://www.deadseascrolls.org.il/

## C. Specialist scholarly map

### C1 — Society of Biblical Literature, `Early Judaism and Its Literature` series

Role: `SPECIALIST_BIBLIOGRAPHIC_MAP + SECONDARY_SCHOLARSHIP`.

The SBL series explicitly covers history, culture and literature of Second Temple Judaism, including Hellenistic Jewish authors, Dead Sea Scrolls, apocrypha and pseudepigrapha.

Why selected: specialist editorial environment and a way to locate work by appropriate scholars rather than allowing popular websites to define the field.

Limitation: many volumes are paid/incompletely inspectable in this environment. A bibliographic citation or publisher description cannot substitute for reading a scholarly argument before relying on its conclusion.

Source: https://www.sbl-site.org/sbl-press/browse-books/early-judaism-and-its-literature/

### C2 — Stuckenbruck & Boccaccini, eds., `Enoch and the Synoptic Gospels`

Role: `SPECIALIST_RELATIONSHIP_MODEL`.

Publisher metadata identifies this as a multi-scholar volume on relationships between 1 Enoch traditions and the Synoptic Gospels across demonology, Christology, angelology, cosmology and other themes. Its methodological value for Doré is the existence of **multiple approaches** to reminiscence, allusion and intertextuality rather than a simple “Enoch caused NT X” model.

Limitation: full volume access is not available here; use as bibliography/framework, not as evidence for a particular chapter's conclusion until the relevant argument is inspectable.

SBL Press metadata: https://cart.sbl-site.org/books/063544P

### C3 — SBL scholarship on diversity/fluidity in Second Temple concepts

Role: `COUNTERPOINT_TO_LINEAR_EVOLUTION`.

SBL Press introductions located during source discovery emphasize the diversity of source corpora (Hebrew Bible, Septuagint, Samaritan Pentateuch, Dead Sea Scrolls, Hellenistic Jewish literature, NT and others) and warn against reducing Second Temple evidence to a static normative model.

Why selected: the course needs an explicit guard against drawing one straight evolutionary arrow through diverse Jewish texts.

Limitation: introductory/front-matter statements set method, not individual historical conclusions.

## D. Source-family rules

### 1 Enoch

Treat `1 Enoch` as a composite literary corpus with a complicated textual history, not one datable voice. Use Qumran Aramaic witnesses for material provenance where relevant, and record which Enochic section is actually being discussed.

### Dead Sea Scrolls / Qumran

Never use `Qumran believed X` merely because one scroll says X. Record composition, manuscript, likely date, language, sectarian/non-sectarian classification and whether the claim belongs to one text or a broader pattern. The IAA itself notes that the Qumran corpus represents diverse and evolving communities/texts rather than a single homogeneous sectarian authorship model.

### Apocrypha / Pseudepigrapha

Do not treat these categories as one canon or one theology. Record work, date range, textual witnesses and relevant community/history independently.

### New Testament

Do not use “the NT teaches X” when the actual evidence is one author/text and other NT trajectories differ. Author, genre and probable date remain part of the ledger.

### Canonical synthesis

Historical development and Christian canonical interpretation receive separate ledger columns. A historical claim cannot be proved by later canonical harmony; a canonical theological relation is not automatically invalid because historical stages differ.
