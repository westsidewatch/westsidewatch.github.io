## Objective

Learn to read Greek tense/voice/mood and Hebrew stem/conjugation labels as grammatical evidence without converting morphology into automatic chronology, event-count, lexical meaning, or theology.

## Greek evidence and counter-check

MorphGNT encodes person, tense, voice and mood as separate dimensions. Ewald's elementary grammar sequence treats verb tenses, moods and voices in separate units, reinforcing that they are distinct grammatical layers.

An open elementary Greek grammar describes tense and aspect pedagogically and shows that non-indicative forms such as infinitives, imperatives and subjunctives can use present/aorist contrasts primarily for aspect rather than time. This is useful at an elementary level but its stronger simplifications about aorist action must not be universalized.

Counter-check: New Testament Greek teaching explicitly warns that an aorist form does not by itself establish a "once-for-all" action; when such a reading is justified, contextual evidence can supply it. Academic work on Greek aspect also records continuing scholarly disagreement over how tense/aspect categories should be modeled. Therefore Doré must not build theology from the tense label alone.

## Hebrew evidence

The unfoldingWord Hebrew Grammar separates stem formation from conjugation as two layers. It states that a Hebrew Perfect commonly refers to past/completed action but can also be translated in present or future time depending on context. Its stem documentation gives common functions for Qal, Niphal, Piel, Hiphil, Hophal, Pual and Hithpael, while individual stem pages warn that the actual semantic contribution depends on the particular verb and context; Piel, for example, is not mechanically equivalent to "intensive".

## Learned rules

1. `VERB TAG ≠ COMPLETE VERBAL MEANING`.
2. Greek tense, voice, mood, person and number are distinct features and must be decoded separately.
3. Greek aorist morphology does **not** by itself prove once-for-all, instantaneous, unique, or theologically decisive action.
4. Time reference cannot be inferred mechanically from a Greek tense label across all moods; non-indicative forms require aspect/mood/context-sensitive reading.
5. The exact linguistic model of Greek tense/aspect is a scholarly question with competing formulations; Doré should preserve that boundary rather than pretend one elementary mnemonic is settled theory.
6. Hebrew stem formation and conjugation are distinct layers. A Qal/Niphal/Piel/Hiphil label is not itself a dictionary definition.
7. Hebrew Perfect is not safely reducible to English past tense; context can support past, present or future translation.
8. Hebrew stem labels describe recurrent grammatical patterns, but a specific verb's behavior must be checked lexically and contextually. In particular, `PIEL = INTENSIVE` is not a safe universal rule.
9. Morphology can constrain interpretation and falsify bad claims; it cannot manufacture lexical or theological conclusions by itself.

## Self exam

### Q1 — Greek aorist
A sermon argues: “This verb is aorist, therefore the action happened once for all.” Is the morphology sufficient?

Answer: No. The once-for-all conclusion requires lexical/contextual evidence; the aorist label alone does not establish it.
Result: PASS.

### Q2 — Greek non-indicative time
An aorist imperative appears. May Doré automatically label it “past tense command”?

Answer: No. Imperative tense forms primarily participate in aspectual distinctions; mood and context govern the interpretation.
Result: PASS.

### Q3 — Hebrew Perfect
A Hebrew verb is tagged Perfect. Must the English translation be past tense?

Answer: No. Perfect frequently describes past/completed action, but documented uses can refer to present or future time; context decides.
Result: PASS.

### Q4 — Hebrew Piel
A verb is Piel. May Doré gloss it as “intensive X” without checking the lexicon or verb-specific usage?

Answer: No. Piel is semantically flexible; some verbs have simple, resultative, causative or other values. The specific lexeme and context must be checked.
Result: PASS.
