## Question

Given the failures, uncertainty maps, WORKING conclusions and evidence requests generated during Research Method I, what courses does Doré itself need next?

This diagnosis is derived from observed research bottlenecks, not from a prewritten seminary curriculum.

## Evidence from Doré's own work

### Gap 1 — Biblical languages are too shallow for researcher-level claims
Observed repeatedly:
- `ruach` could not be responsibly classified by surface translation alone;
- `satan` required Hebrew definiteness, semantic range and diachronic comparison;
- Matthew 2:23 required testing Greek/Hebrew wordplay rather than accepting phonetic resemblance;
- 2 Timothy 3 required Greek `γραφή` usage and semantic scope.

Self-diagnosis: Doré can identify that original-language evidence is needed, but does not yet possess a sufficiently systematic Hebrew/Greek grammar + lexicography discipline to execute all such investigations independently.

Priority: CRITICAL.
Proposed course: `RESEARCHER-03-BIBLICAL-LANGUAGES-I — Lexicon, Grammar, Semantic Discipline`.

### Gap 2 — Textual criticism / manuscripts
Research Method I established textual witnesses as a source family, but Doré has not yet demonstrated competence in:
- manuscript families and witnesses;
- apparatus reading;
- variant classification;
- internal/external evidence;
- separating textual uncertainty from translation differences.

Self-diagnosis: a researcher cannot responsibly handle disputed verses or wording without this.

Priority: HIGH.
Proposed course: `TEXTUAL-CRITICISM-I`.

### Gap 3 — Canon formation and Second Temple context
Blind/practicum questions exposed this directly:
- what `γραφή` could denote in Paul's historical setting;
- development between Hebrew Bible, Second Temple Jewish literature and NT usage;
- when later canonical theology may legitimately synthesize earlier texts.

Self-diagnosis: Doré lacks a disciplined bridge between Biblical World and later canon/history.

Priority: HIGH.
Proposed courses: `SECOND-TEMPLE-JUDAISM-I`; `CANON-HISTORY-I`.

### Gap 4 — Hermeneutics / intertextuality
ONE practicum exposed uncertainty in classifying quotation, allusion, thematic parallel and editorial association. Matthew 2:23 reinforced the problem.

Self-diagnosis: Doré needs formal rules for genre, context, quotation formulae, typology, allusion, sensus boundaries and canonical interpretation.

Priority: HIGH.
Proposed course: `HERMENEUTICS-I — Text, Context, Genre, Intertext`.

### Gap 5 — Historical method
Doré learned generic source evaluation but has not demonstrated researcher-level historical reasoning: dating claims, primary/secondary source distance, argument from silence, anachronism, diachronic development, reconstruction from partial evidence.

Priority: HIGH.
Proposed course: `HISTORICAL-METHOD-I`.

### Gap 6 — Archaeology, geography and chronology as evidence disciplines
ONE showed Doré can refuse invented precision, but refusal is not mastery. It needs methods for site identification, competing locations, archaeological strata, ancient routes, distance models, synchronisms and chronology uncertainty.

Priority: MEDIUM-HIGH.
Proposed course: `BIBLICAL-GEOGRAPHY-ARCHAEOLOGY-I`; `BIBLICAL-CHRONOLOGY-I`.

### Gap 7 — Theology must be layered rather than flattened
The OT-Spirit and Job-satan questions showed a recurring danger: lexical/textual observations, biblical theology, historical theology and systematic/confessional theology can be silently collapsed.

Priority: HIGH.
Proposed course: `THEOLOGICAL-METHOD-I — Exegesis → Biblical Theology → Historical Theology → Systematics`.

### Gap 8 — Church history
Doré's current researcher directory contains ONE and Research Method training but no completed church-history curriculum. Later doctrine, canon, creeds, interpretation and institutional history cannot be researched responsibly without chronology and primary-source awareness.

Priority: HIGH after the biblical-method foundations.
Proposed sequence: `CHURCH-HISTORY-I–IV`.

### Gap 9 — Bibliographic / library science
Doré can say "find a lexicon" or "find scholarship," but managing Dawn Library requires more: editions, authorship, metadata, subject taxonomy, duplicate/version control, provenance, citation graph, authority level and retention policy.

Priority: HIGH for future Dawn Library management.
Proposed course: `RESEARCH-LIBRARY-I — Bibliography, Cataloguing, Provenance`.

### Gap 10 — Learning science / autonomous curriculum control
Doré can now propose a next research action, but has not yet proven that it can convert repeated failures into a curriculum, select textbooks, schedule review, generate exams, detect forgetting, and reopen a supposedly completed unit.

Priority: CRITICAL for autonomy.
Proposed course: `AUTONOMOUS-LEARNING-I — Gap → Curriculum → Study → Exam → Retention`.
