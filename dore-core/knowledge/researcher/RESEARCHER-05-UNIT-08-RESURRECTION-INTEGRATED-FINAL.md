## Stratified evidence

### Hebrew Bible baseline
- Isaiah 26:19 uses awakening/life language for the dead, but its literary setting requires caution before treating every detail as a later systematic resurrection doctrine.
- Ezekiel 37:1–14 uses resurrection-like bodily imagery explicitly for Israel's national restoration; it is therefore important counter-evidence against mechanically converting every revivification text into an individual eschatological-resurrection proof text.
- Daniel 12:2 is the clearest Hebrew-Bible anchor in this sample for postmortem awakening with differentiated everlasting outcomes. Its wording must not automatically be expanded into every later claim about universality, sequence, embodiment, or Messiah.

### Second Temple development
- Second Temple Jewish evidence is diverse rather than a single evolutionary line. 2 Maccabees 7 strongly associates bodily restoration/resurrection hope with martyr fidelity and divine justice, while other Jewish corpora frame afterlife, judgment, restoration, or immortality differently.
- Therefore the bridge from Daniel to the New Testament is development-with-contestation, not a claim that all Second Temple Judaism held one resurrection model.

### New Testament corpora
- Synoptic tradition: resurrection belongs to eschatological dispute and hope; Jesus' argument with Sadducees cannot be reduced to merely repeating Daniel.
- Johannine: John 5:28–29 presents those in tombs coming out to differentiated outcomes, closely resonating with Daniel 12:2 while embedding the event in the Son's authority; John also contains realized-life language, so future and present dimensions must not be flattened.
- Pauline: 1 Corinthians 15 makes Jesus' resurrection the firstfruits and develops transformed embodiment and ordered resurrection. This is materially more specified than the Hebrew-Bible baseline and should be described as Christologically structured development, not retrojected into Daniel.
- Acts 24:15 witnesses hope for resurrection of both righteous and unrighteous, showing a broad judgment horizon without resolving every sequencing question.

## Counter-evidence / anti-retrojection controls
1. Ezekiel 37 FAILS as direct evidence for a fully specified individual end-time resurrection: the text interprets the bones as the house of Israel and restoration from exile/death-like national condition.
2. Daniel 12:2 does not by itself establish every later universal-resurrection detail. Translation/commentary traditions differ on the force of “many”; later universal formulations must not be silently inserted backward.
3. Second Temple evidence is plural. Martyr-resurrection material cannot stand in for every Jewish group's anthropology or eschatology.
4. 1 Corinthians 15's Christ-firstfruits/order/transformed-body configuration is a Pauline Christological development and cannot be treated as already explicit in Isaiah, Ezekiel, or Daniel.
5. John 5's future resurrection must be held alongside Johannine realized eschatology rather than forcing the Gospel into a single temporal scheme.

## Model
PASS model: bounded development with continuity. Earlier Israelite restoration/death-reversal imagery supplies vocabulary and conceptual resources; Daniel supplies a strong postmortem differentiated-awakening anchor; Second Temple witnesses diversify and sharpen resurrection/justice expectations; New Testament corpora receive those resources but configure them differently, especially around Jesus' resurrection, Sonship/authority, transformed embodiment, and final judgment.

Rejected models:
- FLATTENING: every earlier revivification text already means the full NT doctrine.
- RUPTURE: NT resurrection has no meaningful continuity with earlier Jewish texts.
- UNIFORM SECOND TEMPLE LINE: all Jewish witnesses share one anthropology/resurrection scheme.
- RETROJECTION: Pauline/Johannine Christological details are read back as explicit claims of Daniel/Ezekiel.
