## Research question

How should Doré compare a concept across Hebrew, Aramaic and Greek witnesses without assuming either lexical identity or conceptual rupture?

## Evidence learned

1. Translation is evidence, not a transparent replacement for the source language. The Greek Septuagint is an ancient witness whose renderings can preserve interpretation, lexical choice, and in some places a Hebrew Vorlage different from the later Masoretic form.
2. A target-language lexeme must not be mechanically projected backward onto every source-language occurrence. Example class: Hebrew `torah` can be mediated by Greek `nomos`; the lexical mapping does not prove that the complete semantic/conceptual range of either term is identical.
3. Non-rendering is evidence too. Ancient translators can omit a particle, render it variably, explicate it, or reshape syntax. Therefore `no matching Greek word` is not proof that the source concept disappeared.
4. Textual and translational differences must be separated. An LXX/MT difference can arise from translation technique or from a different underlying Hebrew textual form; manuscript/text criticism must precede conceptual inference where the wording diverges materially.
5. Lexical continuity and conceptual continuity are independent variables. The same word may acquire a different discourse role; a different word or transliteration may carry substantial continuity.
6. Transliteration/name development requires its own chain: source form → intermediate linguistic form(s) → target form → discourse role. Similar spelling alone does not establish identity, and changed spelling alone does not establish a new entity/concept.
7. Translation communities and dates matter. “The Septuagint” is not one translator operating at one time; books were translated at different times and may display different techniques.

## Operational mediation matrix

For every cross-language concept claim record:
- source text/form and textual witness;
- target text/form and textual witness;
- whether the relation is translation, transliteration, borrowing, paraphrase, omission, or uncertain;
- plausible alternative renderings;
- whether the difference may reflect a different Vorlage;
- discourse/function comparison in each corpus;
- confidence in lexical relation separately from confidence in conceptual relation.

Allowed conclusions:
- `LEXICAL_AND_CONCEPTUAL_CONTINUITY`;
- `CONCEPTUAL_CONTINUITY_WITH_LEXICAL_CHANGE`;
- `LEXICAL_CONTINUITY_WITH_CONCEPTUAL_DEVELOPMENT`;
- `TRANSLATION_INTERPRETATION`;
- `TEXTUAL_VARIANT_POSSIBLE`;
- `RELATION_UNRESOLVED`.

## Counter-checks

Automatic rejection:
- Greek gloss = exhaustive Hebrew meaning;
- one Greek word = one Hebrew word across corpora;
- LXX difference = translator theology without checking textual variants;
- transliteration = proof of unchanged identity;
- lexical replacement = proof of conceptual discontinuity;
- treating all LXX books as one translation technique.

## Examination gate

### Q1
Hebrew term A is normally rendered Greek B, so a later Greek use of B must mean exactly what A meant in the Hebrew Bible.

Result: FAIL CLAIM. A translation equivalence is evidence for relation, not semantic identity; compare contexts, corpora, date and discourse role.

### Q2
LXX wording differs materially from MT. May Doré infer that the translator deliberately changed the concept?

Result: NO. First test whether the Greek may witness a different Hebrew Vorlage; only then evaluate translation technique/interpretation.

### Q3 — blind transfer
An Aramaic paraphrase expands a compact Hebrew divine-action expression. Does the expansion prove the concept first arose in Aramaic Judaism?

Result: NO. Expansion can make an earlier concept explicit, reinterpret it, or develop it. Establish the Hebrew baseline and the paraphrase's date/function before classifying continuity/development.

### Q4 — blind transfer
A Greek author uses a different lexeme from the usual LXX rendering but performs the same narrative/theological function. Must Doré classify discontinuity?

Result: NO. This is a candidate for `CONCEPTUAL_CONTINUITY_WITH_LEXICAL_CHANGE`; function and conceptual relations require independent testing.

Gate: 4/4 PASS.
