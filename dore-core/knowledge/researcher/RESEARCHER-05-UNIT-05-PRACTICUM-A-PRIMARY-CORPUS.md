## Task
Test whether direct Second Temple and NT samples support a historically responsible Job/Hebrew-Bible → Second Temple → NT adversary-development model without flattening distinct corpora.

## Direct primary sampling
- Jubilees 10:8–10: Mastema petitions to retain a tenth of malignant spirits; permission is granted. This supplies a named hostile administrator under divine constraint, not an independent equal power.
- 1QS / Community Rule: the community is divided into lots of light/darkness; Belial/Angel of Darkness language functions in a sectarian dualistic framework.
- 1QM / War Scroll 1 and 13: Belial leads the hostile lot/army of darkness, while the conflict remains inside God's predetermined eschatological order.
- 1 Enoch 10 and 55–56: Azazel is a leading judged opponent associated with corruption; this is not simply the same narrative office as Job's articulated ha-satan.
- Matthew 4:1–11: the same temptation narrative calls the opponent both ho diabolos and Satanas, giving intra-text identification of Devil/Satan in this Gospel.
- Pauline samples (1 Thess 2:18; 1 Cor 5:5; 7:5; 2 Cor 2:11; 11:14; 12:7; Rom 16:20) show Satan as hindering, tempting/deceiving, disciplinary/afflictive, and destined for defeat; these functions should not be collapsed into one simple role.
- Revelation 12:9 and 20:2 explicitly identify the dragon/ancient serpent with Devil and Satan, a much more synthesized apocalyptic profile than Job alone supplies.

## Specialist/corpus counter-checks
- Cambridge material on the history of Satan explicitly separates Hebrew-Bible satans, intertestamental Enoch/Jubilees/DSS, authentic Paul, Gospels, later epistles, and Revelation rather than treating the NT or pre-NT material as one voice.
- Qumran evidence displays Belial/Angel of Darkness configurations; Jubilees uses Mastema; Enochic traditions prominently use Watcher/Azazel configurations. Diversity is therefore evidence, not noise.

## Developmental conclusion
A bounded development-with-continuity claim passes: adversarial/accusatory/testing motifs available in Hebrew-Bible material are elaborated in multiple, non-identical Second Temple configurations, while NT corpora more frequently use Satan/Devil as a personalized hostile opponent and some NT texts explicitly identify titles/figures. This supports conceptual continuity plus substantial development, but does not prove that Job's ha-satan already possesses the full profile found in Revelation or later Christian demonology.

Historical identity and canonical theological identification remain separate questions.
