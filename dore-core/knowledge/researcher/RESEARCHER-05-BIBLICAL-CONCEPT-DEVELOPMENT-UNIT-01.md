## Objective

Before tracing a biblical/theological concept through time, Doré must stratify evidence by text, corpus, approximate date, language, provenance and claim type. Similar vocabulary across texts is not enough to erase historical distance.

## Core model

Every developmental claim begins with an evidence matrix containing at least:

| Field | Required question |
|---|---|
| Text/composition | What actual work supplies the evidence? |
| Passage | Where exactly is it found? |
| Corpus | Hebrew Bible, Apocrypha/Pseudepigrapha, DSS, NT, early Christian, etc.? |
| Approximate date | What range is responsibly supportable? |
| Witness date | Is the surviving manuscript later/earlier than the proposed composition date? |
| Language | Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek, Ethiopic, etc.? |
| Textual form | Which edition/witness/translation is being used? |
| Community/provenance | What can actually be said, and what remains disputed? |
| Claim type | lexical, narrative, historical, conceptual, canonical/theological? |
| Confidence | high / bounded / disputed / unknown |

The matrix must be built **before** drawing an arrow of development.

## Source observations

### Dead Sea Scrolls archive

The Israel Antiquities Authority archive classifies compositions into distinct types and gives manuscript-level metadata. It also warns against the older habit of attributing all Qumran material to one homogeneous Essene community: the corpus contains different composition types and reflects related/evolving communities and texts.

Concrete check:
- `4Q201` and `4Q202` are Enoch manuscripts in Aramaic, classified as parabiblical and dated paleographically to the Hasmonean period.
- Community Rule witnesses have their own manuscript numbers, dates, languages and publication histories.

Lesson: “found at Qumran” is provenance evidence; it is not automatic proof of single authorship, date of original composition, or shared theology across every scroll.

### Yale historical framework

Dale Martin's first-century Judaism lecture places New Testament texts within the historical sequence from postexilic Judaism through Hellenistic/Hasmonean/Roman periods. His canon lecture distinguishes scripture from the later process of canon formation. Christine Hayes' Hebrew Bible course likewise treats biblical texts in historical/cultural context and distinguishes historical, literary and canonical approaches.

Lesson: the same text can legitimately participate in historical and canonical questions, but the methods must not be silently merged.

## Learned rules

1. `MANUSCRIPT DATE ≠ COMPOSITION DATE`.
2. `FOUND AT QUMRAN ≠ AUTHORED BY THE QUMRAN YAḤAD`.
3. `SECOND TEMPLE JUDAISM ≠ ONE THEOLOGY`.
4. `SAME WORD ≠ SAME CONCEPTUAL STAGE`.
5. `SAME CONCEPT ≠ SAME VOCABULARY REQUIRED` — conceptual continuity can use different terms, so lexical identity is neither necessary nor sufficient.
6. A developmental argument needs an ordered set of independently described evidence points before it can infer continuity/change.
7. Dating uncertainty must propagate into the developmental conclusion; it cannot be hidden after arranging texts in a convenient order.
8. A later manuscript can preserve an earlier composition, so material witness date and literary date must be tracked separately.
9. A canonical reading may place texts in theological relation without claiming that their human authors shared the same historical conceptual formulation.
10. “Early Judaism,” “Second Temple,” “Qumran,” “apocalyptic,” and “Christian” are useful scholarly categories only when they do not replace the actual texts being compared.

## Self exam

### Q1
A manuscript of 1 Enoch is paleographically dated to the first century BCE. Does that prove the relevant Enochic composition was first written in that century?

Answer: No. Manuscript/witness date and composition date are distinct claims.
Result: PASS.

### Q2
A doctrine-like idea appears in Community Rule and 1 Enoch. May Doré say “the Qumran community believed this” about both texts?

Answer: Not without provenance/relationship evidence. Presence in the Qumran library does not make every composition a Yahad-authored confession.
Result: PASS.

### Q3
Job and a New Testament text use historically related adversary terminology. Can Doré place them in one conceptual definition before examining intermediate Jewish evidence?

Answer: No. First describe each endpoint in its own text/corpus; then investigate intermediate evidence and possible continuity/change.
Result: PASS.

### Q4
If a later Christian canonical reading connects two historically distinct texts, must the historical analysis deny the connection?

Answer: No. It must label the connection as canonical/theological rather than pretending it is identical to historical authorial meaning.
Result: PASS.

## Adversarial transfer

Claim: “The Dead Sea Scrolls prove Jews in Jesus' day all believed in a cosmic two-spirit dualism, because the Community Rule contains language of two spirits.”

Falsification:
1. identify the specific Community Rule passage/manuscript/textual tradition;
2. separate manuscript dating from composition dating;
3. establish what group the rule text represents;
4. compare other Jewish corpora rather than generalize from one sectarian composition;
5. distinguish a documented trajectory from universal Jewish belief.

Verdict: claim is overgeneralized as stated.
Doré result: PASS.

## Evidence matrix mini-practicum

### Job `הַשָּׂטָן`
Corpus: Hebrew Bible / wisdom narrative.
Claim layer: narrative/lexical.
Date: literary dating is debated and must not be assigned false precision here.
Immediate result: articulated adversary figure in Job's heavenly scene.

### Zechariah 3 `הַשָּׂטָן`
Corpus: Hebrew Bible / prophetic vision.
Claim layer: narrative/lexical.
Historical setting: Persian-period prophetic context, while precise compositional/redaction questions require specialist treatment.
Immediate result: articulated adversarial figure in a heavenly/judicial scene.

### 4Q Enoch witnesses
Corpus: Second Temple Jewish / parabiblical manuscript corpus.
Language: Aramaic.
Witness period: Hasmonean for 4Q201/4Q202 per IAA metadata.
Immediate result: supplies materially dated witnesses to Enochic traditions; does not by itself specify every section's composition date or settle Satan terminology.

### New Testament `Σατανᾶς / διάβολος`
Corpus: early Christian / NT.
Language: Greek.
Claim layer: lexical/narrative/theological depending passage.
Immediate result: must be examined author-by-author before being used as a single endpoint.

This matrix is intentionally incomplete. Unit 1's task is correct stratification, not premature trajectory construction.
