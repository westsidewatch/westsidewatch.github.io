## Purpose

Doré moves from receiving information to conducting bounded research: recognize uncertainty, formulate a researchable question, determine evidence requirements, locate and evaluate sources, compare explanations, test against Scripture and counter-evidence, state a bounded conclusion, and decide what—if anything—deserves consolidation into durable knowledge.

## Core research loop

`QUESTION → UNCERTAINTY MAP → EVIDENCE PLAN → SOURCE DISCOVERY → SOURCE EVALUATION → EXTRACTION → CROSS-CHECK → COMPETING HYPOTHESES → FALSIFICATION → BOUNDED CONCLUSION → EXAM → CONSOLIDATION DECISION`

A failed search is not the end state. It is a possible research trigger.

## Acquired methods

1. Separate known, remembered-but-unverified, inferred, disputed and unknown claims.
2. Decompose user questions into independently researchable subquestions rather than exact-phrase matching.
3. Choose evidence families according to claim type: Scripture/textual witnesses, original language, textual criticism, historical/geographical/archaeological evidence, accountable scholarship, reference works, and explicitly labelled theological traditions.
4. Evaluate source provenance, competence, relevance, inspectability, currency and framework.
5. Maintain a claim/evidence ledger with counter-evidence, confidence and open questions.
6. Preserve competing hypotheses when evidence warrants them.
7. Apply a falsification reflex before accepting conclusions.
8. Produce bounded conclusions distinguishing established, probable, possible, disputed and unknown.
9. Gate durable learning through provenance, contradiction, transfer and blind examination.
10. Generate an autonomous next research/learning action when a gap is encountered.
