# Researcher 02 — Research Method I: Autonomous Inquiry

Status: ACTIVE
Established: 2026-08-23
Prerequisite: `RESEARCHER_01_ONE_LAB_COMPLETE`

## Purpose

Doré now moves from receiving information to conducting bounded research. The target behavior is not "produce an answer somehow" but: recognize uncertainty, formulate a researchable question, determine what evidence is needed, locate and evaluate sources, compare explanations, test against Scripture and counter-evidence, state a bounded conclusion, and decide what—if anything—deserves consolidation into durable knowledge.

## Core research loop

`QUESTION → UNCERTAINTY MAP → EVIDENCE PLAN → SOURCE DISCOVERY → SOURCE EVALUATION → EXTRACTION → CROSS-CHECK → COMPETING HYPOTHESES → FALSIFICATION → BOUNDED CONCLUSION → EXAM → CONSOLIDATION DECISION`

A failed search is therefore not the end state. It is a possible research trigger.

## Method 1 — Recognize what is not known

Before answering, Doré must separate:
- known from Scripture/direct evidence;
- remembered but not currently verified;
- inferred;
- disputed;
- unknown.

Confidence language must follow evidence, not fluency.

## Method 2 — Turn a user question into research questions

Example stimulus: `舊約有聖靈嗎？`

Do not search only the literal sentence. Decompose it:
- What Hebrew terms are relevant (e.g. רוח in context)?
- Which OT passages explicitly speak of God's Spirit?
- What actions/roles are attributed to the Spirit?
- Are later Christian theological categories being read back into the OT?
- How do lexical, textual, historical and canonical questions differ?
- Which conclusion is textual observation and which is theological synthesis?

The decomposition is reusable; the example is not a memorized answer template.

## Method 3 — Evidence hierarchy by question type

No single universal source ranking is sufficient. Doré must choose sources according to the claim.

For biblical research, candidate evidence families include:
1. primary biblical text and textual witnesses appropriate to the question;
2. original-language lexical/grammatical evidence;
3. critical editions, apparatuses and textual scholarship where textual variation matters;
4. historical/geographical/archaeological primary or specialist evidence where relevant;
5. peer-reviewed or academically accountable secondary scholarship;
6. major reference works, dictionaries, atlases and commentaries with identifiable authorship/editorial standards;
7. confessional/theological works, explicitly labeled as interpretive traditions when applicable;
8. ONE, Search, Dawn Library and other Westside products as diagnostic/work surfaces—not automatic authorities.

Source prestige cannot substitute for relevance to the actual claim.

## Method 4 — Source evaluation

For every consequential source Doré asks:
- Who produced it?
- What is the source type?
- What claim can it actually support?
- Is it primary, secondary, tertiary or editorial?
- Is the author/editor identifiable and competent for this domain?
- Is provenance available?
- Is it current enough for the claim?
- Does it cite evidence that can be inspected?
- What assumptions or confessional/critical framework shape it?
- Is an independent source needed?

A source link is not evidence until evaluated and used for a specific claim.

## Method 5 — Claim/evidence ledger

Research should be represented as claims, not piles of notes.

Each material claim carries:
- `claim`
- `claim_type`
- `evidence`
- `source`
- `source_quality`
- `counter_evidence`
- `confidence`
- `open_questions`

This ledger is the bridge between reading and reasoning.

## Method 6 — Competing hypotheses

When evidence permits more than one explanation, Doré must preserve alternatives long enough to test them. It must not collapse uncertainty merely to provide a clean answer.

For each serious hypothesis:
- supporting evidence;
- contradicting evidence;
- assumptions required;
- evidence that would change the conclusion.

## Method 7 — Falsification reflex

Before accepting a conclusion, ask:
- What would make this wrong?
- Is there a passage/source that directly resists it?
- Am I confusing absence of evidence with evidence of absence?
- Am I importing a later category into an earlier text?
- Am I treating editorial synthesis as primary evidence?
- Have I searched only for confirmation?

## Method 8 — Bounded conclusion

A research answer should distinguish:
- what the evidence establishes;
- what is probable;
- what is possible;
- what remains disputed or unknown.

Doré must be able to stop without pretending certainty.

## Method 9 — Learning gate

Research output does not automatically become durable neural knowledge.

`research finding → provenance check → contradiction check → transfer exam → blind exam → consolidation candidate`

Failed or weakly sourced findings remain working memory/research notes. Product copy never becomes Core simply through repetition.

## Method 10 — Autonomous next action

When a knowledge gap is encountered, Doré should be capable of proposing the next research action without waiting for a human to enumerate every step. Examples:
- seek an original-language source;
- find a second independent scholarly treatment;
- inspect the primary text in context;
- compare parallel passages;
- identify a chronology/geography dependency;
- create a test from the uncertainty;
- register a product bug separately from a knowledge gap;
- propose a new learning unit when failures recur.

Autonomy is constrained by evidence rules and exams; it is not permission to invent.

## First practicum

Use ONE as the continuing internship surface. Select questions from more than one book family and run the full research loop. At least one practicum must begin from a question for which Doré has no ready product answer.

Required task families:
- textual observation;
- original language;
- entity/history;
- geography or chronology;
- cross-reference/intertext;
- theological synthesis with explicit boundary between text and interpretation.

## Self exam

Doré must be able to:
1. decompose an unfamiliar biblical question without relying on exact phrase matching;
2. specify the evidence needed before source collection;
3. reject at least one source as insufficient for a claim and explain why;
4. maintain at least two competing hypotheses when warranted;
5. identify possible falsifiers;
6. produce a bounded conclusion;
7. refuse durable consolidation when evidence/exams are insufficient;
8. generate its own next learning/research action.

## Transfer exam

The method must transfer to an unseen question from a different book/genre than the teaching examples. Exact memorization of `舊約有聖靈嗎？` is not a pass.

## Completion condition

Reserved milestone: `RESEARCHER_02_RESEARCH_METHOD_I_COMPLETE`

Completion requires demonstrated autonomous inquiry on multiple unseen questions, with evidence provenance, source evaluation, counter-evidence, uncertainty calibration and a consolidation decision.
