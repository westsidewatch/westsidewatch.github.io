## Objective

Learn to distinguish genuine continuity, meaningful development, discontinuity and merely superficial similarity when later Jewish/Christian texts reuse or transform earlier biblical language and concepts.

## Method evidence

### Diversity is the default historical control

Modern introductions to Second Temple Judaism emphasize substantial diversity within the period rather than a single normative theology. This means a developmental claim must specify **which text/tradition** continues, revises or rejects which earlier configuration.

### Development can occur through appropriation, not only vocabulary change

Jason Staples' study of the concept of Israel describes a recursive method: first map biblical constructions, then examine how later participants appropriate, perform and develop those traditions. His broader result also shows that a concept can retain core scriptural anchors while being variously defined by different groups.

Method lesson: conceptual continuity may coexist with contested boundaries and new configurations.

### Reuse can be creative and authoritative simultaneously

Hindy Najman's work on Mosaic discourse argues that later Jewish texts can return repeatedly to Moses/Sinai in ways that create new authoritative discourse rather than merely copying an earlier text. Deuteronomy, Jubilees, Temple Scroll and Philo therefore provide a model in which later reuse can be both continuous with a founding tradition and materially innovative.

Method lesson: `DEPENDENCE/REUSE ≠ IDENTICAL CONCEPT` and `INNOVATION ≠ COMPLETE DISCONTINUITY`.

Sources used for the method gate:
- Cambridge, Delbert Burkett, “Varieties of Second-Temple Judaism”;
- Jason A. Staples, *The Idea of Israel in Second Temple Judaism*, introduction and summary;
- Hindy Najman, *Seconding Sinai: The Development of Mosaic Discourse in Second Temple Judaism* (SBL Press metadata/argument summary);
- Andreas Runesson, “What Does It Mean to Read New Testament Texts ‘within Judaism’?” for historical-category caution.

## Four-way classification

For each proposed trajectory, classify the relationship before synthesizing:

### 1. `DIRECT_CONTINUITY`
Later evidence preserves substantially the same conceptual relation/function, with differences that do not alter the core claim.

Required evidence:
- textual/semantic correspondence;
- compatible function in context;
- plausible historical channel or common scriptural inheritance;
- no major contradictory reconfiguration ignored.

### 2. `DEVELOPMENT_WITH_CONTINUITY`
Later evidence preserves identifiable earlier elements but reorganizes, intensifies, expands or relocates them.

This is the most important middle category because many Second Temple → NT relationships are neither simple identity nor rupture.

### 3. `DISCONTINUITY_OR_COMPETING_TRAJECTORY`
Later evidence materially rejects, reverses or competes with an earlier/parallel configuration.

This can occur within the same broad historical period; “later” does not automatically mean “more developed” or “more correct.”

### 4. `SUPERFICIAL_SIMILARITY / NOT_ESTABLISHED`
Shared vocabulary, imagery or theme exists, but evidence is insufficient to infer historical/conceptual continuity.

This is the required default when only resemblance is shown.

## Tests for a development claim

Before labeling development, Doré must answer:
1. What is the earlier concept in its own text—not a later summary of it?
2. What is the later concept in its own text?
3. Which components are genuinely shared?
4. Which components are added, removed, intensified, individualized, collectivized, moralized, cosmicized or otherwise reorganized?
5. Is the relation direct literary reuse, shared scriptural inheritance, common cultural environment, or merely possible resemblance?
6. Are there competing texts from the same period that prevent a single linear story?
7. Does the dating order actually support the proposed direction of development?
8. Does a translation-language shift alter the vocabulary while preserving/transforming the concept?
9. What evidence would falsify the proposed trajectory?
10. Is the final claim historical, canonical/theological, or both in separate layers?

## Self exam

### Q1 — same word
Two texts two centuries apart use historically related words for “adversary.” Is that direct continuity?

Answer: Not automatically. Compare referent, role, narrative function, conceptual associates and intermediate evidence.
Result: PASS.

### Q2 — new detail
A later text adds angels, cosmic geography and final judgment to an older judgment motif. Does innovation prove discontinuity?

Answer: No. It may be development-with-continuity if the older structure is still recognizably appropriated; the relation must be demonstrated component by component.
Result: PASS.

### Q3 — diversity
Two Second Temple texts develop the same scriptural tradition differently. Must one represent “what Judaism evolved into”?

Answer: No. They can be competing/concurrent trajectories within a diverse Jewish environment.
Result: PASS.

### Q4 — canonical reading
A NT author rereads an earlier text christologically. Does historical reconfiguration make the canonical relation “fake”?

Answer: Historical analysis can identify transformation/recontextualization; whether that relation is theologically authoritative is a separate canonical/hermeneutical claim, not settled by the word “development.”
Result: PASS.

## Adversarial transfer A — Israel

Claim: “The word Israel appears throughout Jewish literature, therefore everyone meant the same bounded people-group by Israel.”

Counter-evidence/method:
- Staples' research demonstrates contested and varying constructions of Israel while retaining shared scriptural/restoration anchors.
- Same label can participate in continuity while social/theological boundaries change.

Classification: `DEVELOPMENT_WITH_CONTINUITY + COMPETING_TRAJECTORIES`, not simple lexical identity.
Doré result: PASS.

## Adversarial transfer B — Mosaic authority

Claim: “Jubilees reuses Moses/Sinai, so it merely repeats the Pentateuch and adds nothing conceptually new.”

Counter-evidence/method:
- later Mosaic discourse can claim continuity with founding revelation precisely through creative re-presentation;
- reuse and innovation are not opposites.

Classification: `DEVELOPMENT_WITH_CONTINUITY` is plausible; the exact content requires passage-level comparison.
Doré result: PASS.

## Adversarial transfer C — later = evolution

Claim: “A first-century text has a more elaborate demonology than Job, therefore Job's adversary necessarily evolved in a straight line into that exact system.”

Falsification:
- elaboration alone does not prove genealogical direction;
- intermediate corpora may contain multiple trajectories;
- vocabulary, role and narrative system must be compared;
- dating and possible literary/shared-tradition relations must be established.

Classification: `NOT_ESTABLISHED` until evidence matrix is built.
Doré result: PASS.
