# Doré Multi-Version Researcher Standard

Status: REQUIRED

## Principle

Doré is not considered competent in Chinese or English Scripture by ingesting one representative translation. Researcher-grade competence requires broad knowledge of major versions, their histories, textual bases, translation philosophies, terminology, characteristic renderings, strengths, limitations, reception, and appropriate research uses.

Every version is an independent textual witness. No version is silently treated as the language itself.

## Chinese research corpus

Doré must study and, where licensing permits, ingest and align major Chinese Bible traditions, including at minimum:

- Chinese Union Version / 和合本（traditional and simplified witnesses where relevant）
- Chinese Union Version with New Punctuation / 新標點和合本
- Revised Chinese Union Version / 和合本修訂版
- Recovery Version / 恢復本
- Chinese New Version / 新譯本
- Today’s Chinese Version / 現代中文譯本
- New Chinese Bible / 新漢語譯本
- major historical Chinese translations where research value warrants inclusion
- additional widely used Chinese versions discovered during corpus survey

The list is extensible and must not be interpreted as exhaustive.

## English research corpus

Doré must study and, where licensing permits, ingest and align major English Bible traditions across historical and translation-philosophy families, including at minimum:

- KJV
- ASV
- WEB / WEBU
- RSV
- NRSV / NRSVue
- NASB
- ESV
- NIV
- NLT
- NET
- CSB
- major historical English translations where research value warrants inclusion
- additional widely used English versions discovered during corpus survey

The list is extensible and must not be interpreted as exhaustive.

## Required knowledge for each version

Doré must maintain a sourced version dossier containing:

1. official version name and abbreviations
2. language/script and publication/revision history
3. publishers / translation committees where documented
4. source-text / textual-base information where documented
5. translation philosophy and stated goals
6. characteristic vocabulary, names, divine-name conventions, syntax, register, and punctuation
7. important revision history
8. relationship to predecessor/successor editions
9. ecclesial and scholarly usage context
10. licensing and permitted modes of storage/querying
11. representative translation decisions compared with original-language evidence and peer versions
12. documented strengths
13. documented limitations / recurring cautions
14. Doré's evidence-based evaluation, explicitly separated from publisher claims and community opinions
15. appropriate and inappropriate research uses

## Evaluation standard

Doré must not produce simplistic rankings such as "best Bible version" without a defined task and criteria. Evaluation is multidimensional and evidence-based.

Evaluation dimensions may include:

- textual-base transparency
- formal correspondence
- semantic clarity
- literary quality
- contemporary comprehensibility
- consistency of terminology
- preservation of ambiguity
- handling of Hebrew/Greek idiom
- theological terminology
- names and divine-name conventions
- footnotes / textual-variant transparency
- usefulness for close exegesis
- usefulness for public reading
- usefulness for translation comparison
- historical influence

Strengths and limitations must be demonstrated by evidence and representative passages, not asserted from reputation.

## Comparison engine

For a canonical passage Doré should be able to traverse:

Hebrew/Aramaic or Greek source
-> ancient witnesses where relevant (LXX, Vulgate, etc.)
-> multiple English translations
-> multiple Chinese translations
-> aligned translation decisions
-> lexical/syntactic/text-critical evidence
-> version-specific explanation
-> comparative evaluation

Differences must be classified where possible, for example:

- textual-base difference
- lexical choice
- syntactic choice
- supplied/implicit material
- idiom handling
- interpretive translation
- theological terminology
- register/style
- punctuation
- proper-name convention
- revision/update

## Copyright and access rule

"Must know" does not mean "may copy and store without permission." Public-domain or permissively licensed witnesses may be fully ingested. Copyrighted witnesses require a lawful licensed source, official API, user-authorized source, or other permitted access. When full text cannot legally be stored, Doré should still build a sourced version dossier and comparison capability within permitted quotation/access limits.

## Graduation requirement

Chinese or English Scripture competence is not PASS merely because one 66-book witness ingests successfully.

PASS requires:

- multi-version coverage across translation families
- version dossiers
- canonical alignment
- comparative-difference classification
- original-language-grounded evaluation
- strengths/limitations with evidence
- licensing provenance
- benchmark questions requiring Doré to explain why versions differ, not merely display them
