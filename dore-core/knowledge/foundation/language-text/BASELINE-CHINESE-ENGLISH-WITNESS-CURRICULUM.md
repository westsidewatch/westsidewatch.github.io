# Doré Baseline Chinese & English Witness Curriculum

Status: ACTIVE SELECTION STANDARD

## Purpose

This curriculum closes Doré's foundational Bible-reading phase. Selection is not driven by a target number of versions. A witness belongs in the baseline when a Doré-level biblical researcher would have a material knowledge gap without understanding it because of present circulation, historical influence, translation significance, scholarly usefulness, or its role in a major ecclesial tradition.

The baseline must be broad enough for Doré's identity and narrow enough to preserve a reliable common frame before advanced research. Additional niche or specialized versions become `research_on_demand` unless later evidence shows they belong in the baseline.

## Chinese baseline

### Core circulating / reference tradition
- Chinese Union Version (CUV / 和合本), including the traditional/simplified and Shen/Shangti distinctions where materially relevant.
- Chinese Union Version with New Punctuation (CUNP / 新標點和合本).
- Revised Chinese Union Version (RCUV / 和合本修訂版).
- Today's Chinese Version, Revised where applicable (TCV / 現代中文譯本).
- Chinese New Version (CNV / 新譯本).

### Research-significant traditions
- Lü Zhenzhong Translation (呂振中譯本): independent twentieth-century translation of major value for close comparison and Chinese translation history.
- Studium Biblicum Version (思高譯本): major Catholic Chinese tradition and necessary for cross-tradition research.
- Recovery Version (恢復本): important for understanding a significant Chinese-speaking church tradition and its distinctive translation/annotation choices; evaluation must distinguish translation evidence from doctrinal notes.
- New Chinese Bible / 新漢語譯本 where the edition and access source are verified: important modern research-oriented translation tradition.

The Chinese list is not numerically capped. A version is added to the baseline only when omission would materially weaken Doré's researcher identity. Historical missionary translations and specialized/low-circulation versions normally remain research-on-demand until a research question requires them.

## English baseline

### Historically foundational
- KJV: indispensable for English Bible history, literary influence, and the received-text tradition.
- ASV: important historical formal-equivalence reference and ancestor of later American revision traditions.
- RSV: major twentieth-century revision watershed and ancestor of important later translations.

### Major current / scholarly comparison set
- NRSV / NRSVue: major academic, ecumenical, and critical-study tradition.
- NASB: major formal-equivalence American tradition.
- ESV: major contemporary essentially-literal evangelical tradition.
- NIV: major internationally circulating contemporary translation and a central functional/formal balance point.
- NLT: major functional-equivalence/readability tradition.
- NET: research-significant because translation decisions are extensively documented in notes; text and notes have separate access/licensing considerations.
- CSB: significant contemporary mediating translation tradition.
- WEB / WEBU: permissively accessible modern corpus and useful computational baseline.

NKJV and other major versions should be promoted into the baseline if the version survey shows that their omission creates a material gap in circulation, translation lineage, or research comparison. No quota is imposed.

## What 'read' means

A baseline witness is not complete merely when its verses are retrievable. Doré must acquire:

1. canonical coverage or a documented access route;
2. version history and revision lineage;
3. stated translation philosophy;
4. documented textual base where available;
5. characteristic terminology and style;
6. representative comparison passages;
7. known strengths and limitations, with sourced evidence;
8. relation to predecessor/successor versions;
9. ecclesial/scholarly usage context;
10. lawful access policy and provenance;
11. ability to explain important differences against Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek evidence.

## Access is independent of curriculum status

A baseline version may be Local Corpus, Licensed API, External Reader/MCP, or Human Only. Copyright restrictions affect transport/storage, not whether Doré is expected to understand the version. Unknown permission remains restrictive.

## Stop rule for foundational reading

The foundational Bible-reading phase ends when:

- Hebrew/Aramaic and Greek original-language corpora are operational;
- LXX and Vulgate baseline witnesses are operational;
- the Chinese baseline above has been read to researcher standard through lawful access;
- the English baseline above has been read to researcher standard through lawful access;
- cross-version comparison benchmarks demonstrate that Doré can explain differences rather than merely retrieve text.

At that point the milestone is `BIBLICAL_CORPUS_READING_COMPLETE`.

Doré then enters the research phase. New Bible versions are normally read on demand as research questions require them. They do not automatically enter foundational statistics or semantic baselines.

## Research-on-demand admission rule

A later witness may be activated when it is needed to investigate a concrete question in textual history, translation history, theology, reception, denomination/tradition, language change, or a distinctive rendering. Doré must compare it against the established baseline before assigning evidentiary weight.
