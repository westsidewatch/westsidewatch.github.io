## Initial external research directions

The first scan identified useful patterns to investigate further:

- Library of Congress digital collections: large holdings are made navigable through collection grouping, gallery/list modes, sorting, contextual collection descriptions, and browse paths rather than a flat resource dump.
- Livingstone Online redesign: explicitly combines scholarly heritage with navigation for diverse audiences; its architecture separates homepage, section pages, content pages, manuscript viewer, and browse/search surfaces.
- Curationist: separates targeted search, curated collections, and editorial features, showing how discovery and guided curation can coexist.
- Museum of the Bible online experience: separates online exhibitions, streaming media, and videos into understandable content modes instead of flattening all media into identical cards.
- Brooklyn Museum redesign case: treats every page as an exploration doorway, combines searchable archives with curated collection areas, and emphasizes high-quality imagery and accessibility.
- OCLC CONTENTdm / digital-collection practice: responsive collection viewing, search refinement, embedded media/maps, brand customization, and discoverability are baseline requirements for serious digital collections.

Doré should broaden this research beyond these seeds and retain citations/provenance for design conclusions.

## Visual-upgrade principles already fixed

- Westside Watch / 西望 remains the public brand; Doré remains infrastructure/editor/visual worker rather than becoming a competing public brand.
- Preserve established brand character where still useful: classical/biblical editorial tone, Cormorant Garamond + Noto Serif TC family, restrained Dawn Gold / 初光金 language, stone/light/archive sensibility.
- Do not turn the upgrade into generic SaaS cards, dashboard chrome, or an AI-chat aesthetic.
- Visual beauty must improve comprehension and use. A page that looks impressive but obscures hierarchy, overflows on mobile, hides actions, or weakens reading/watchability fails.
- Real content should drive prototypes. Test with actual Three Morning Star teachers/series/works, real subtitles/PDF/video states, and real Scripture relations rather than placeholder-only mockups.
- Responsive behavior, alignment, typography, content density, keyboard/accessibility behavior, loading/performance, and long-corpus navigation are part of visual quality, not post-design cleanup.
- No ecosystem-wide production visual rewrite should be declared complete without representative desktop/mobile verification and cross-product consistency checks.
- The first comprehensive visual upgrade should establish a recognizable **Westside Watch visual grammar**, not merely improve isolated pages.
- Doré's original engraving/illustration language should become one of the major brand-recognition layers. It should be integrated with Dawn Gold, stone, light, typography, editorial photography, maps, manuscripts, video imagery, and contemporary interface needs rather than applied as a universal decorative filter.
- Doré-style imagery should be used semantically: to help identify biblical scenes, books, people, themes, narrative moments, transitions, or editorial moods. It should not be added where it competes with the actual content or creates repetitive visual noise.
- Visual navigation is a first-class information architecture tool. Image, card, title, hierarchy, and motion should help the eye understand where to go before the reader is asked to process dense text.
- Entry surfaces should generally favor low text density and strong visual recognition; information density may increase progressively as the reader moves deeper into teacher → series → work → study/read/watch states.
- Cards are primarily for discovery, orientation, and choosing a path. Deep reading/watching pages should progressively reduce the feeling of a card grid and support focus.
- Every major surface should feel visually related even when its layout differs. Main Site, ONE, Join, Search, Liming Library, Westside Stories, journals, devotionals, and future Doré conversation should share enough visual DNA that removing the logo would not erase brand recognition.
- Shared identity does not mean identical layouts. Each product may have its own spatial behavior while inheriting common imagery language, typography, light/gold/stone logic, spacing rhythm, navigation behavior, and motion discipline.
- Desktop and mobile share content structure and brand language but do not have to share the same composition. Mobile is a first-class composition target, not a shrunken desktop.

## Brand visual direction — Engraving + Dawn + Navigation

The first visual-system upgrade should deliberately combine three existing strengths into one coherent system:

### 1. Doré engraving as brand memory
Doré's original engraving/illustration language is not merely content illustration; it can become a high-recognition visual signature of Westside Watch. The objective is not to make every page look nineteenth-century. The objective is to let the eye repeatedly encounter a distinctive biblical image language that belongs to this ecosystem.

Possible roles include:
- major collection/section entrances;
- biblical-book and narrative navigation;
- editorial feature openers;
- teacher/series contextual imagery where historically/content-appropriate;
- background crops, details, line-work fragments, or tonal overlays used with restraint;
- transitional moments between browse and deep reading;
- selected loading/empty/discovery states where meaningful.

Doré should study how to pair engraving density with contemporary whitespace, typography, UI controls, video stills, manuscripts, maps, archival photographs, and living church imagery so the system feels intentional rather than nostalgic or visually crowded.

### 2. Dawn / light as state language
Dawn Gold and light should not become a blanket color treatment. Light can communicate hierarchy, entrance, discovery, progression, completion, or focus. Existing Westside Watch patterns such as dark/grey → pale gold → Dawn Gold should be evaluated as part of a wider state/attention language rather than isolated decoration.

### 3. Visual navigation as the reader's first map
A visitor should not need to understand the database before exploring it. Image-led cards and curated visual groupings should create an immediate map of possible directions. The ideal effect is: **rich enough to invite exploration, ordered enough that the eye always knows where to look next.**

The visual system should increasingly support a spatial model:

`vista → entrance → gallery/collection → series/work → focused reading/watching/study`

Text density should generally rise with depth rather than being maximal at the entrance.

## Visual consistency test

For representative pages across the ecosystem, Doré should periodically test this question:

> If the Westside Watch logo and product name were temporarily hidden, would a familiar reader still recognize this as belonging to the same Westside Watch visual world?

Recognition should come from a combination of image language, typography, composition, light/gold/stone treatment, spacing rhythm, motion, controls, editorial restraint, and navigation behavior—not from a single repeated ornament.
