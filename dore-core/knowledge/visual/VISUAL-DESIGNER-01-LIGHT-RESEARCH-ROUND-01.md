## External observations

### Gustave Doré — atmospheric construction

University of Toronto Library discussion of Doré's *Idylls of the King* emphasizes that his images frequently build mystery/disturbance through light, steel-engraving texture, and scale. Landscapes, forest, cave, castle towers and cliffs dwarf human figures; apparently tranquil large compositions reveal unsettling details only after closer viewing. Transferable lesson: atmosphere is produced by hierarchy, scale, delayed detail recognition and differentiated texture, not by adding a bright overlay.

### Huis Marseille — heritage translated into system

Wieden+Kennedy's rebrand for Huis Marseille derives a contemporary identity from the museum's 17th-century architectural heritage and an original engraved letterform on the facade. The historical source is translated into a modernized logotype and broader typographic/layout system across digital and print. Transferable lesson: inherit a source's structural logic, not its surface wholesale.

### The Branch Museum of Design — architecture as ownable grammar

The Branch identity used Pope's architectural blueprints as the base for a flexible contemporary system. The stated challenge was to preserve historical character without making the institution feel trapped in the past. Transferable lesson: an ownable source constraint can generate a flexible family that changes without breaking identity.

### MIT Museum exhibition identities — research precedes form

Interval describes beginning exhibition identity work with content research, questions and conceptual/visual possibilities before sketches are narrowed into focused directions. Transferable lesson: Doré must not open Penpot first; the design hypothesis must precede drawing.

### MCA Chicago — structural metaphor, not decoration

MCA Chicago's identity derives its grid from both Chicago's urban grid and the museum facade, then extends the principle into typography and other identity behavior. Transferable lesson: a visual device earns brand status when it has semantic/architectural cause and can organize multiple surfaces.

## First transferable LIGHT hypotheses

These are hypotheses for testing, not governing brand law.

1. **Light needs a source.** Every beam/edge/glow must imply an origin, direction and obstruction condition.
2. **Atmosphere is relational.** Light is only legible against differentiated foreground/midground/depth values and material behavior.
3. **Occlusion creates credibility.** Broken or partial light is more spatially believable than uniform rays.
4. **Scale creates awe.** A large atmospheric field with restrained localized detail is closer to Doré logic than many equal-weight decorative marks.
5. **Darkness retains information.** Shadow must contain line density, texture, contour or structural clues; dead black is not Doré-like darkness.
6. **Gold is an event, not a material fill.** First Light Gold belongs to the contact edge / first touched surface / rare focal trace.
7. **Negative space is active.** Untouched Living Paper can function as the brightest light and should not automatically be filled.
8. **Detail should have hierarchy.** Some marks may only appear on closer inspection; equal-density hatching everywhere kills atmosphere.
9. **Asset ≠ illustration.** LIGHT-01 must be crop-friendly, scalable and edge-entering so it can participate in a page without becoming a standalone picture.
10. **System before family.** LIGHT-02..06 are prohibited until LIGHT-01 proves a reusable grammar.

## What the failed Penpot attempts taught

The previous outputs treated vector format as a proxy for visual quality. This was incorrect. A vector can still be a color block, empty group, generic ray field or structurally meaningless shape.

New rule:

`vector/editable` is a production property, not a design-quality property.

Design quality requires visible composition, spatial causality, atmosphere, hierarchy, material logic and successful human visual judgment.
