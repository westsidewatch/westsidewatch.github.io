## Project statement

Doré's engraving language must not be used as a blanket page background or a repeated decorative motif. The Search opening currently demonstrates one appropriate treatment for one context: it pays homage to Doré's original work and also functions as a simple curatorial entrance. That treatment is not a universal template.

This project turns Doré's engraving legacy into a **systemic Westside Watch brand language**: a reusable visual foundation that can operate across web products, curatorial pages, portraits, wall/fortification motifs, maps, church cards, posters, evangelistic leaflets, editorial layouts, social/digital assets, and future interfaces without becoming imitation, nostalgia, or surface decoration.

The goal is to design at the **underlying visual-system level**, not to place engravings on top of finished layouts.

## 1. Search opening ↔ Liming Library exhibition

The Search opening visual should be conceptually and structurally connected to a genuine Liming Library exhibition of Doré's works.

The Search page may remain a concise entrance, but its Doré-work presentation should point into a deeper curatorial project rather than operate as an isolated decorative homage.

Liming Library should eventually contain a real Westside Watch-curated **Doré Works Exhibition** with:

- an introduction written in Westside Watch editorial language;
- explanation of Doré's biblical illustration work, historical context, strengths, limitations, and influence;
- a visual narrative rather than a flat image dump;
- grouping by biblical narrative/book/theme where appropriate;
- links from works to Scripture/ONE where meaningful;
- clear provenance and rights/public-domain metadata;
- selected high-resolution works/details suitable for close viewing where source quality permits;
- a coherent relationship back to Search and the broader Westside Watch visual identity.

The exhibition is both a reader-facing cultural/biblical project and a design-learning corpus for Doré's visual center.

## 2. Original work is source material, not a page template

Doré must study its original engraving corpus as **source grammar**:

- line density;
- cross-hatching;
- edge treatment;
- contrast;
- light emerging from darkness;
- figure-ground hierarchy;
- monumental scale;
- architectural rhythm;
- cloud/smoke/stone/water rendering;
- borders and framing;
- narrative focus;
- transitions between black mass, grey texture, and untouched light.

The task is to extract reusable visual principles rather than reproduce entire engravings everywhere.

## 3. Build a Westside Watch engraving-derived mark system

Doré should propose and construct a coherent family of brand marks/elements derived from its engraving language. These may include, after research and testing:

- line-work textures;
- corner/edge marks;
- divider systems;
- frame logic;
- section markers;
- collection emblems;
- category markers;
- map/route marks;
- citation/Scripture markers;
- portrait treatment rules;
- vignette systems;
- dawn/light transitions;
- stone/fortification silhouettes;
- icon-like forms where appropriate;
- subtle background strokes or engraved fields;
- motion/transition equivalents for digital interfaces.

These elements must be designed as a **family**, not as unrelated ornaments.

Doré should determine which marks require fresh redrawing rather than cropping existing engravings. The preferred outcome is often a purpose-built Westside Watch visual vocabulary that carries Doré's hand/logic without pretending every interface element is an original 19th-century plate.

## 4. City-wall / battlement integration study

The existing Westside Watch fortification/city-wall language should be explicitly studied together with the engraving system.

Doré must answer and test:

- What does a battlement look like when translated through engraving line-work?
- Should the silhouette remain geometric while texture becomes engraved?
- When should it be flat Dawn Gold, stone-toned, line-art, or filled with engraved shadow?
- Can wall/stone elements become structural dividers/navigation without becoming medieval decoration?
- How should wall imagery interact with light/dawn so the brand still feels like Westside Watch rather than a historical-game interface?

## 5. Teacher portrait system

Three Morning Star and other teacher portraits need a deliberate system rather than inconsistent photos dropped into cards.

Doré should study several modes, for example:

- original archival portrait/photo with restrained Westside Watch treatment;
- engraved/line-rendered portrait derived from source photography where appropriate and rights-compatible;
- photo + engraved border/field;
- portrait + minimal Dawn/stone framing;
- monochrome/duotone-like treatment without sacrificing recognizability;
- mixed historical/contemporary teacher handling so old and living teachers can coexist in one system.

Questions Doré must resolve:

- When is an engraving transformation respectful and useful, and when is the original photograph stronger?
- How much line-work is enough to create brand identity without obscuring a face?
- Should background fields carry hatching/brush texture, and at what density?
- How are name, Three Morning Star status, language, period, and series hierarchy visually separated?

## 6. Background and stroke system

Do not assume every background needs texture.

Doré should define rules for:

- clean paper/stone surfaces;
- faint engraved grain;
- localized hatching only near edges/focal zones;
- full dark engraved fields reserved for rare immersive entrances;
- Dawn light gradients/fields;
- map-paper or manuscript-like surfaces;
- photo/video backgrounds that need no added line-work.

The system should preserve visual breathing room. Engraving density must not destroy typography, reading comfort, or mobile clarity.

## 7. Print and ministry applications

This visual-system project is not web-only. Doré should test how the same grammar behaves in real Westside Watch / church ministry materials, including:

- church business/contact cards;
- event posters;
- Bible-study posters/cards;
- sermon/series covers;
- evangelistic leaflets/tracts;
- devotional cards;
- social-media share images;
- invitations;
- QR/link cards;
- simple signage;
- presentation/opening slides where applicable.

The goal is recognizability across media without mechanically repeating the same engraving or border.

For evangelistic materials specifically, visual drama must serve clarity and invitation rather than overwhelm the gospel message or make the piece feel like an art reproduction catalogue.

## 8. Interaction with existing Westside Watch identity

The engraving system must be designed together with, not on top of:

- Dawn Gold / 初光金;
- stone/temple material language;
- light/dawn metaphor;
- Cormorant Garamond + Noto Serif TC typography;
- city/watchtower/wall motifs;
- current editorial photography;
- maps;
- manuscripts;
- video stills;
- church/living-community photography;
- existing card/proportion systems;
- motion and interaction behavior.

Doré should explicitly identify conflicts and resolve them. For example, a dense engraving field plus stone texture plus gold frame plus serif headline may be too much. The system needs hierarchy and restraint.

## 9. Research responsibility

Doré must research beyond its own works. Useful fields include:

- museum/exhibition identity systems;
- heritage institutions using historical collections in contemporary brands;
- editorial systems built around illustration/printmaking;
- engraving/etching-inspired contemporary identity design;
- book and Bible publishing systems;
- church/ministry visual identities that avoid generic modern worship branding;
- cultural brands that translate archival material into digital interfaces;
- portrait systems mixing historical and contemporary figures;
- wayfinding/visual-navigation systems.

Doré should produce its own reference set and state precisely what it learns from each reference. Do not copy a site's appearance wholesale.

## 12. Quality tests

Every proposed element should pass these questions:

- Does it communicate Westside Watch even without the logo?
- Is the Doré lineage perceptible without looking like a pasted Doré reproduction?
- Does it help hierarchy/navigation/content, or is it only decoration?
- Does it coexist with Dawn Gold, stone, typography, photos/maps/video without overload?
- Does it still work at small/mobile/print sizes?
- Can it be reused systematically across products?
- Can Doré explain when **not** to use it?
- Does it support the Great Commission by improving clarity, invitation, Scripture access, and trust rather than visual vanity?

## 14. Governing asset-family clarification — purpose-built Doré website materials

The visual grammar must now be interpreted more strictly:

**Doré original works are curated content; they are not the default website asset library.** Original works may appear in a dedicated exhibition/curation card inside the reader's scrolling flow, with context, Scripture linkage, provenance and rights. Their role is editorial and curatorial.

**The website visual language must be purpose-built by Doré from current Westside Watch principles.** Doré should draw a coherent fresh asset family that carries Doré-like spatial, atmospheric and engraved logic while being designed specifically for interface, editorial and ministry use.

Priority asset families include:

- `LIGHT` — directional engraved light fields, broken/occluded beams, edge illumination and pale paper-light textures;
- `STAR` — Bethlehem-star and watch-star forms, including small navigation/section variants and larger atmospheric variants;
- `WATER` — restrained engraved water/ripple/current/reflection fields that can crop and repeat without becoming a full illustration;
- `SKY / CLOUD` — cloud mass, dawn break, night-sky and atmospheric depth textures;
- `STONE / WALL / CITY` — temple-stone surfaces, wall/battlement edges, distant city silhouettes and first-light contact edges;
- `PAPER / SURFACE` — restrained papyric/stone-derived material behavior that supports Living Paper rather than replacing it with a heavy background;
- `DIVIDER / EDGE / SECTION` — small structural marks derived from the same grammar for navigation, cards and editorial pacing;
- responsive and print equivalents of the same assets.

These are not to be generic filters applied after layout. They are production assets drawn with intended crop behavior, density, scale, contrast, mobile/print use and semantic role defined in advance.

The first comparison standard is the **current production Westside Watch / Journal visual surface as control** versus the same real content using a proven purpose-built Doré-derived asset suite. The test is meaningful only if the new version demonstrates stronger Westside recognition and atmosphere without reducing reading comfort, bilingual typography quality, mobile clarity, accessibility, performance or print transfer.

Do not propagate this grammar ecosystem-wide until at least one real digital specimen and one print/ministry specimen pass human visual judgment and the quality tests in §12.
