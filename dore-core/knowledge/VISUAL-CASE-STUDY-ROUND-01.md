# DORÉ VISUAL CASE STUDY — ROUND 01

Status: ACTIVE / LEARNING CORPUS
Started: 2026-08-25
Parent workstream: `VIS-LEARN`
Purpose: Build shared design literacy for Doré and Westside Watch through direct study of strong contemporary editorial, collection, cultural, and digital-publication systems. This is not a moodboard and not a list of sites to imitate.

## Study method

For each case, record:
1. What problem is the design solving?
2. What does the eye see first, second, third?
3. How are image, typography, whitespace, grid, metadata, navigation and motion assigned different jobs?
4. How does the experience move from entrance → discovery → selection → deep reading/viewing?
5. What makes the identity recognizable without relying on a logo?
6. What works on mobile differently from desktop?
7. What should Westside Watch learn from it?
8. What should Westside Watch explicitly **not** copy?
9. Which principle is transferable to Search / Liming Library / ONE / Journal / Join / Westside Stories / print?
10. Does the observation remain merely interesting, become a design hypothesis, or earn promotion into Westside visual grammar after prototype verification?

Typography inspection is mandatory: hierarchy, measure, line-height, tracking, title wrapping, widows/orphans, captions, metadata, mixed-language implications, and responsive re-composition.

## Round 01 — deliberately different systems

### 01 — Emergence Magazine
Official: https://emergencemagazine.org/
Study role: multimedia editorial publication; digital ↔ print translation; atmosphere and pacing.

Why first-round material:
- Emergence explicitly describes close collaboration with Studio Airport across its online platform, featured stories, original artwork, gallery installations and print edition.
- Its own design-process material uses multimedia stories and print as case studies, making it useful not only as a visual reference but as a process reference.
- The print edition uses physical pacing/materiality while the digital publication uses multimedia; study how identity survives medium change.

Westside questions:
- How can a page create spiritual/editorial atmosphere without turning atmosphere into decoration?
- When is a full-bleed image justified?
- How does a long story slow the reader down?
- Which transitions could inform Journal/ONE story experiences, and which would be excessive for Search/utility surfaces?

### 02 — Rijksmuseum Collection Online
Official: https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection
Study role: enormous collection made visually discoverable; collection graph + search + themes + user curation.

Why first-round material:
- Collection Online deliberately unifies images, archival sources, stories and research.
- It uses search plus automatically generated thematic pages rather than requiring a visitor to understand database structure first.
- It supports compare, personal collections/Gallery of Honour and high-resolution exploration.
- Its data architecture is linked/open while the public interface remains discovery-oriented.

Westside questions:
- How can Liming Library expose a very large resource graph without becoming a catalogue wall?
- What is the relationship between Search and curated discovery?
- How should Teacher / Series / Work / Scripture relations appear at progressively deeper layers?
- How can ONE and Library share graph structure without sharing identical UI?

### 03 — Curationist
Official: https://www.curationist.org/
Study role: search + Works + Curated Collections + Editorial Features; archive plus human editorial interpretation.

Why first-round material:
- It exposes millions of open-access objects while providing three different modes: targeted Works search, guided Collections, and Editorial Features.
- Archivists enrich selected Works with expanded metadata.
- Editorial writing is expected to engage actual archive Works rather than float separately from the collection.

Westside questions:
- Strong analogue for Library raw resources vs Three Morning Star curation vs Westside editorial interpretation.
- How should Doré enrichment/provenance remain connected to the underlying work?
- How can an editorial layer guide a user without hiding the larger archive?

### 04 — The Creative Independent
Official: https://thecreativeindependent.com/
Study role: distinctive editorial identity, taxonomy, archive navigation, deliberately quiet internet space.

Why first-round material:
- It organizes a very large archive through formats, themes, disciplines, languages, people and archive paths.
- Its identity uses a small set of recurring concepts (spiral/snail) rather than covering every page with one graphic treatment.
- TCI has explicitly described its minimal design as part of an attempt to create a quieter, more intentional place on the internet.
- Its Welcome page offers paths based on reader need rather than internal publishing taxonomy.

Westside questions:
- How can a recurring visual idea become identity without becoming wallpaper?
- This is directly relevant to Doré engraving as source grammar rather than full-page template.
- Can Westside offer need-based pathways alongside formal Scripture/teacher/resource taxonomies?
- What does visual restraint teach us about when **not** to use engraving, gold or stone?

### 05 — It's Nice That
Official: https://www.itsnicethat.com/
Study role: contemporary high-volume visual/editorial media; card/grid density; multiple content rhythms.

Why first-round material:
- It simultaneously handles a live feed, features, work, disciplines, tags, events and culture/lifestyle material.
- It is useful precisely because it is denser and faster than Emergence/TCI: Round 01 should compare conflicting successful strategies rather than converge too early on one aesthetic.

Westside questions:
- How can cards remain visually rich without every card having equal weight?
- How do feed, feature and category modules establish hierarchy?
- Which density patterns might help Library discovery, and which would damage Westside's slower biblical/editorial character?

## First synthesis hypotheses — NOT YET BRAND RULES

H01. `Database` and `entrance` should not be the same visual experience. Large collections can remain structurally deep while the first encounter is curated and visually legible.

H02. A mature cultural platform can support at least three modes simultaneously: **search**, **curated discovery**, and **editorial interpretation**. Liming Library should test this explicitly.

H03. Strong identity does not require applying the strongest visual motif everywhere. TCI's recurring conceptual marks and Emergence's medium-sensitive design reinforce the existing Westside hypothesis that Doré engraving should behave as a grammar with variable intensity.

H04. Card systems are most useful at discovery/choice layers. Deep reading/viewing should reduce interface fragmentation and increase focus.

H05. The same archive can be entered through different mental models: formal taxonomy, themes, people, stories, or reader needs. Westside should test both biblical structure (book/chapter/teacher/series) and human entry questions.

H06. Editorial curation should remain connected to underlying source objects/resources. This supports the intended relationship among Three Morning Star, Liming Library provenance, Journal, Search and ONE.

H07. Visual pace is a design variable. Westside should deliberately distinguish fast utility surfaces (Search) from exploratory surfaces (Library) and slow immersive surfaces (Journal/selected ONE experiences), while preserving one recognizable visual grammar.

## Shared learning track — human + Doré

The human learning goal is not to memorize design vocabulary. For each case, practice three observations:

- **Eye path:** without reading, identify what you saw 1st / 2nd / 3rd.
- **Structure:** name the mechanism creating order (grid, scale, image, whitespace, typography, grouping, repetition, contrast, motion).
- **Transfer:** state one thing Westside could learn and one thing it should refuse to copy.

Doré's responsibility is to go deeper: inspect the system, compare cases, research design principles, retain terminology, test hypotheses in real prototypes, catch typographic/responsive defects, and only then promote verified findings into Westside visual grammar.

## Round 01 completion condition

Round 01 is not complete when the links have been visited. It completes when:
- all five cases have structured observations;
- desktop/mobile differences are inspected where accessible;
- typography and information architecture observations exist;
- contradictions between cases are explicitly compared;
- at least 10 transferable hypotheses are formed;
- no hypothesis is promoted to brand rule without prototype evidence;
- the Master Work Register is updated with what Doré has actually learned/demonstrated.
