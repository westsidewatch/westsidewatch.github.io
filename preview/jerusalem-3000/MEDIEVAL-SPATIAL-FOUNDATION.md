# Medieval and Ottoman spatial foundation

The Crusader, Ayyubid/Mamluk and Ottoman phases now enter the same independent district/path compiler as Aelia, Byzantine and early Islamic Jerusalem. One small local JSON pack supplies the three layouts. The old stylized Crusader and Ottoman generators remain available as prototype code, but no longer own these active scenes; the Ayyubid/Mamluk inherited Crusader overlay is also suppressed. This changes presentation ownership, not a claim that historic structures vanished.

## Sources and limits

- [IAA Aderet Eliyahu excavation](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/Report_Detail_Eng.aspx?id=8529): Crusader parallel market streets and shops along the earlier Cardo context.
- [IAA Bet Ha-Bad excavation](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/Report_Detail_Eng.aspx?id=26417): north–south market context and late medieval market references.
- [IAA Cotton Market quarter study](https://publications.iaa.org.il/cornerstone/vol10/iss1/49/): Mamluk market access to the Haram and associated services.
- [IAA Mount Zion excavation](https://hadashot.iaa.org.il/Report_Detail_Eng.aspx?id=25008&mag_id=124): southern fortification phases, Ayyubid dismantling and Ottoman rebuilding context.
- [IAA northwestern fortification study](https://publications.iaa.org.il/atiqot/vol65/iss1/26/): layered defenses near the modern New Gate area. This does not introduce that later gate into an earlier phase.

These provide broad relationships, not model coordinates. All district envelopes, illustrative access links, parallel street spacing, reserved core envelopes, widths and six-metre cell heights are authored design geometry. The western core reservation is only a design exclusion; the linked market source does not locate or measure a church. No historical city boundary, exact demographic distribution, individual building, continuous wall circuit or complete ancient street network is certified. Empty model space does not prove historical absence. Composite periods must not imply simultaneous survival throughout the date range; the cotton-market context belongs to the later Mamluk part of the combined Ayyubid/Mamluk phase.

## Coherent scope

| Phase | District envelopes | Paths | Coarse emphasis |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Crusader | 4 | 5 | Market-axis context, parallel market lanes and southern layout |
| Ayyubid/Mamluk | 5 | 4 | Northern/western Haram approaches and cotton-market connection |
| Ottoman | 6 | 5 | Compact southern layout, market and access context |

All six independent later phases share the union camera window and the same fixed metric comparison map. Their coordinates fit inside the previous three-phase envelope, so the extension does not enlarge the comparison extent. Generation uses the existing DEM and road/reservation exclusions, produces ledger blocks rather than semantic houses, and preserves distinct per-phase identities. Later fates remain unresolved; proximity does not create verified reuse links. Evidence filters and backward switching use the same records as rendering.

The old generic rectangular city walls are withdrawn from active Crusader/Ottoman presentation. Their historical replacements remain an explicit water/defense gap until source plans can be registered. This package completes district/path registration for these three scenes, not all six historical domains. Full foundation acceptance remains false. Architecture detail remains paused. The next coherent package is the outside-walls/modern pair: retaining the old-city context while registering expansion districts and routes, before adjacent-era transition acceptance.

The pack is fetched once through the shared loader. It adds no remote imagery, models or terrain downloads; no measured token, bandwidth or frame-rate savings are claimed.
