# B25 — coordinated altar and ritual-service ensembles

Continues first/earlySecond/Herodian sanctuaries with distinct literary choices and explicit authored details. It does not complete the precinct programme or historical geometry admission.

## First temple: selected dimensions, not arbitrary enlargement

The previous4×4×2.5m placeholder altar is replaced by a selected [2Chronicles4:1](https://www.biblegateway.com/verse/en/2%20Chronicles%204%3A1) budget:20×20×10cubits, or9×9×4.5m using the inherited0.45m convention. The source choice is stored with the temple data. Kings does not supply the same complete dimension set; the literary tradition is not a surveyed Solomonic altar. The three-tier/horn silhouette, placement and bronze finish are authored. Overall dimensions are tested in actual geometry. The existing approach clears the new footprint without moving the road, court or temple.

Two cylindrical pillars use the selected [1Kings7:15–22](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=1+Kings+7%3A15-22&version=WEB) convention:18cubit shaft,12cubit circumference,5cubit capital. Radius is derived from circumference rather than chosen for visual emphasis. Capital silhouette is a simple authored lathed form, not recovered lily/pomegranate/net decoration. Comparison with Chronicles' pillar figures remains unresolved. Centres move0.5m forward within the existing court so the larger capitals clear the porch; this remains an illustrative location. Shafts meet the display court floor; the group stays below the main temple roof.

A shared bronze material serves the new altar/pillars and is disposed with the royal assembly. Existing sea/laver models are retained. This does not certify the unchanged survival of the selected altar/pillars throughout the late-first-temple phase; that phase still uses the existing representative transform/ruin projection with unverified specific furniture history.

## Herodian laver and northern service band

[Middot3:5–6](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Middot.3.5) describes rings north of the altar, eight short pillars, tables, and the laver between altar and porch slightly south. [Middot5:2](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Middot.5.2) supplies relative band distances. Primary texts were read in the full Middot audit; this round rechecked the source context.

- Laver: genuinely open lathed bowl and support, between porch/altar and south of the axis, clear of the20cubit-wide porch stairs. Radius1.5cubits/base1cubit/bowl2cubits and lateral placement14cubits south are **authored**, not measures supplied by3:6.
-24rings: selects six rows of four, while preserving the four-rows-of-six alternative in metadata. Rings lie within an authored24cubit-deep band starting8cubits north of the altar edge. Individual spacing, orientation and radius are display choices.
-8short pillars with plain timber caps. Pillar2.2m height/cap0.2m and lateral spacing are authored; hooks and their precise arrangements remain unbuilt.
-7table positions interleaved laterally with the8pillars, in the nearer service row. **Seven is an authored display count, not a count supplied by Middot3:5.** Table height0.85m/size1.4×0.9m and supporting legs remain illustrative.

The complete135cubit north/south budget, wall thicknesses, all work-band edges and remaining spaces are not certified as closed historical geometry. The existing altar/courtyard position is retained. Full six-room service ensembles, water/drainage routes and source-admitted ancient footprints still await coordinated admission; this is not a complete priestly-service reconstruction.

All service parts merge into the existing inner-temple evidence batch and inherit its phase/evidence/ruin controls. No new ledger or survival claim. Repeated torus/column/bowl geometry is cached; no textures/models or source API requests are fetched at runtime.
