# B28 — precinct context rather than an isolated temple

## Diagnosis of the blank ground

The prototype had concentrated visible construction inside the small temple envelope. Early Second Temple reservations remove residential parcels over145×140m, while the authored court is118×104m and previously had only low walls and an altar. Its unbuilt court-edge ensemble made this appear forgotten. The royal-stage housing admission deliberately uses the David ridge, not the northern sacred/royal region. Thus first-temple surroundings also lack full urban/royal context. Blank modern DEM inside these areas is not archaeological proof of empty ancient ground.

These are partly missing precinct models, partly intended open courts, and partly unlocated context. They are not all the same problem. This package makes the court ensemble visible; it does not fill every unknown with houses or certify the complete royal precinct. Broader northern housing, royal-palace interfaces and independent footprints remain unfinished.

Unlocated royal halls are not silently replaced with random filler structures.

## Early Second Temple court ensemble

Josephus, [Antiquities11.4](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Josephus/ant-11.html), describes cloisters around the inner temple in his later literary account of the rebuilding. This is not an excavated Persian court plan and does not supply exact dimensions or establish identical Hasmonean fabric.

Three independent display portico strips now articulate the west/east/north sides of each existing early court. The south entrance remains open. Strip depth4m, roof datum3.8m,0.35m square column profile, bay spacing at most6m and placement2m inside the existing court are authored. No Herodian25cubit column height or100cubit sanctuary is copied into either early model. Each period inherits its own source/evidence/lifecycle binding; repeating a grammar does not assert masonry survival.

Floors have original-DEM-following support faces computed with the existing grid/triangle-crossing approach helper. Strip datums include original interior terrain peaks; every column top meets its roof. This is a display construction, not an ancient terrace survey. The entire public court is not filled or terrain-flattened. Open areas have explicit context notes and an ensemble camera rather than being labelled vacant historical land.

## Herodian continuation

The existing Royal Stoa gets two rows of clerestory piers and upper beams to articulate its three-aisled height hierarchy. Existing185×32m envelope, side/centre roof heights,12authored longitudinal column positions and platform placement remain unchanged. The openings are real gaps between supporting piers. Their count, profile and glazing/roof detail are not supplied by the source and remain schematic. [JosephusAnt15.11](https://avande1.sites.luc.edu/jerusalem/sources/antiquities15.htm) is the existing basilical/height source; this package does not resolve the old Stoa/platform/Antonia registration gaps.
