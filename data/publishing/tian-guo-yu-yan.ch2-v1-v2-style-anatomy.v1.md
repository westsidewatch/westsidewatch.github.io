# 《天國語言極簡史》第二章 v1 / v2 文體解剖 v1

Status: WRITING TRAINING / COMPARATIVE ACCEPTANCE
Inputs:
- `tian-guo-yu-yan.ch2-pre-huang-writing-experiment.v1.md` — frozen
- `tian-guo-yu-yan.ch2-post-huang-writing-experiment.v2.md` — frozen comparison
- `tian-guo-yu-yan.huang-renyu-writing-study.v1.md`

Purpose: identify what v2 genuinely improved, what vitality v1 possessed and v2 weakened, and convert the result into an author-specific grammar. The goal is not Huang imitation. The goal is to remove machine habits without flattening the native voice of 《天國語言極簡史》.

## 0. Verdict

v2 is structurally more mature than v1, but it is not yet the target voice.

v1's main defect is **narrator overpressure**: it repeatedly stops material in order to announce meaning, dramatize discovery, or manufacture a landing. v2 removes much of this and lets evidence remain in the room longer.

v2's new risk is **historical-report flattening**: after removing too many pressure points, some passages become evenly competent. The book must not trade AI theatricality for museum-label prose.

Therefore the house rule is not `less narrator = better`.

It is:

> **Narrator pressure must be earned by accumulated material. Facts carry the passage by default; authorial pressure enters only where the book's own inner action requires it.**

Chapter 2 inner action remains: **嘆息 / 用力**. A successful passage should gradually make effort felt, then allow mortality to put a crack through that effort. It must not explain this mechanism while performing it.

## 1. Opening: Nimrod → Erech/Uruk

### v1 tendency

The opening says Uruk was large, lists buildings / warehouses / ration recipients, then concludes:

> 城市大到一定程度，人的記憶已經不夠用了，於是泥土上開始留下數字和符號。

This is readable, but the last sentence converts historical material immediately into a neat causal explanation. It has the cadence of popular-science narration: fact → meaning → landing.

### v2 change

v2 stays with grain, beer, livestock, workers and rations, then writes:

> 文字在這座城市很早的一項工作，是幫助人管理另一群人每天吃掉的東西。

This is stronger because it does two jobs without announcing them. It remains concrete, yet quietly introduces administration and human organization.

### Preserve

- Genesis geographical adjacency: Nimrod's kingdom → Erech/Uruk.
- Ordinary administrative matter as entrance into civilization.
- Food/ration as a human-scale counterweight to monumental architecture.

### Further correction

Do not let every archaeological paragraph end with a generalized historical proposition. Sometimes the ration tablet itself is enough.

**Grammar:** prefer `specific object → human action → next specific object` over `specific object → abstract conclusion`.

---

## 2. Brick world: v1 vitality that must survive

v1 contains:

> 石頭不在手邊，他們就自己做出石頭來。

This sentence is dangerous and valuable.

Danger: it is compact enough to become an automatic quotable line; repeated sentences of this kind produce AI polish.

Value: it catches Chapter 2's inner action — **用力** — in one physical gesture. The plain is short of stone; human beings manufacture a substitute. It also belongs to the author's native theological imagination: humans receive a world, then exert themselves upon it.

v2 removes the line and becomes materially more patient, but also loses some voltage.

### Admission rule

Do not ban strong sentences. Ban **scheduled strong sentences**.

A strong sentence may remain when:
1. it grows directly from concrete material already established;
2. it advances the chapter's inner action;
3. the paragraphs around it do not contain competing landing sentences;
4. it does not explain its own significance afterward.

If admitted, `石頭不在手邊，他們就自己做出石頭來` should be allowed to stand once and then be left alone.

**House grammar: earned compression, not automatic aphorism.**

## 3. Material sequence: the best gain in v2

v2's brick passage makes the work sequential:

`取土 → 加水 → 模具 → 曬 → 燒 → 搬 → 吃飯`

This is better than repeatedly describing the builders as ambitious, proud, organized or strenuous. The verb sequence performs **用力**.

The line:

> 城牆可以很高，神廟可以很大，到了晚上，工人仍然要領口糧。

has life because scale changes without a theatrical announcement: monument → evening → worker → food.

It should be retained as a model of **scale movement**. The author can move from civilization to a stomach without saying “讓我們把目光轉向普通人”.

**Grammar:** scale changes by adjacent fact, never by camera-direction language.

---

## 4. Gilgamesh: v2 fixes the narrator, but can recover scene pressure

v1 contains several explicit pressure interventions:

> 這句話出現在他的冒險以前。
>
> 死亡還沒有等到史詩後半部才來教訓他。
>
> 人會死這件事，他已經知道。

The insight is important: mortality precedes the famous Enkidu crisis. But three successive interpretive beats overstate it.

v2 compresses the point into chronology: the early Sumerian text already has Gilgamesh recognize life's boundary before Enkidu's later death in the standard epic tradition. This is historically and narratively cleaner.

However v2 risks becoming synopsis:

> 洪水、遠行、不死的秘密和那株最後失去的植物，使這條路繞得很遠……

This moves too quickly across material that may deserve scene magnification in the final book.

### Future rule

When a fact changes the emotional direction of the chapter, do not explain it repeatedly; **slow the scene instead**.

For Gilgamesh:
- first knowledge of mortality can be stated calmly;
- Enkidu's death can receive sensory / bodily residence;
- the immortality journey can expand according to curiosity value;
- no narrator sentence needs to say “this changed everything.”

**Grammar:** replace explanatory emphasis with selective scene magnification.

## 5. Name: v1 explains the waterway too early

v1:

> 兩件事相隔著不同的文本傳統，卻都保留了一種很古老的辦法：人留不住自己，至少還可以留下名字；名字若還不夠，就把它放進一座城裡。

This is intellectually attractive but structurally premature. It names the underground waterway while the reader is still walking above it.

v2 instead puts royal names into actual bricks, foundations and later museum collections. The connection to Genesis 11 follows afterward.

This is a major improvement.

### House rule: submerged motif

For an underground-waterway motif:
- first occurrence: material only;
- second occurrence: altered material;
- later crossing: juxtaposition may become visible;
- theological naming waits until the accumulated returns require it.

Do not write `this echoes...` merely because the author has noticed the echo.

**The author may know the waterway before the reader. The prose should not therefore put up a sign saying UNDERGROUND WATERWAY.**

---

## 6. The “come” problem: v2 improves it but still explains too much

v1:

> 所以創世記在造城以前先保存了一段對話。他們彼此說：「來吧。」
>
> 然後磚開始出現。

This is highly staged. “所以” announces interpretation; the isolated quotation and isolated landing manufacture revelation.

v2 lets labor accumulate first, then writes that such work requires people to understand one another and speech to become collective action. This is conceptually stronger.

But v2 still says:

> 這樣的工程首先要求許多人能彼此明白，也要求一句話可以从一个人口中出来，成为许多人的行动。

This may still be one explanatory sentence too many. The final manuscript should test whether the surrounding labor sequence plus the biblical quotation already lets the reader perceive this.

### Stronger future method

Let the verbs of Genesis do more work:
- “they said to one another”
- “come”
- “let us make”
- “let us build”

Place those verbs against the material labor already established. The relation between language and coordinated action can emerge without a mini-thesis.

**Grammar:** before writing an interpretive bridge, test whether textual adjacency can carry the bridge.

---

## 7. Endings: both v1 and v2 reveal different risks

v1 ending:

> 牆已經不在那裡了。磚還在。名字也還在。

It is memorable but too perfectly engineered. Three short beats reveal the writer arranging significance.

v2 ending:

> 泥土于是被一块一块做成可以垒起来的形状。城在后面，塔也在后面。

This is quieter and physically grounded, but it may be too resolved as an insertion ending. It closes the paragraph aesthetically instead of necessarily returning to the canonical Chapter 2 current.

### Final-book rule

An expansion does not need its own ending.

A digression inside a chapter should often **re-enter the host manuscript while still moving**. If every excursion has a polished conclusion, the book becomes a chain of essays.

**Grammar:** prefer re-entry over closure.

## 8. AI-trace taxonomy after the experiment

The experiment now identifies at least eight recurring machine traces to test throughout the manuscript:

1. **表演式發現** — “等等 / 有意思的是 / 更驚人的是 / 注意這裡”. HARD BAN.
2. **密集落地句** — every paragraph receives a punch-line. Suppress.
3. **自動金句** — compression generated because a paragraph seems to require quotability. Suppress unless earned.
4. **解釋回聲** — narrator names a motif immediately after juxtaposing it. Delay.
5. **假懸念** — question or withheld answer used only to keep reader attention. Ban unless a real unresolved research/theological problem exists.
6. **機械反轉句** — repetitive `不是A，而是B`. Frequency gate; use only when a real distinction requires correction.
7. **摘要式人物** — major emotional events compressed into plot synopsis while minor facts are expanded. Correct through scene-magnification hierarchy.
8. **小論文封口** — every digression receives a concluding thesis before returning. Replace with re-entry.

These are not lexical blacklists except #1. They are rhythm / structure diagnostics.

## 9. Positive house grammar v0.1

The writing system should not be defined only by prohibitions. The experiment yields positive rules:

### A. Material carries thought
Use brick, mud, bitumen, ration, wall, hand, body, road, breath, wind before abstract nouns when the material can perform the thought.

### B. Earned compression
A short strong sentence is permitted when long factual accumulation has earned it. One is enough. Do not explain it afterward.

### C. Selective magnification
Ordinary historical movement can remain quick. When the chapter's inner action changes, time may slow sharply.

### D. Scale without signposting
Move naturally among person / object / city / century / Scripture / present by factual adjacency, not “now let us turn”.

### E. Submerged recurrence
Motifs return before they are named. Meaning accumulates across distance.

### F. Curiosity before utility
A factual excursion does not need immediate theological usefulness. If it opens a real world and remains inside the book's attractor, allow it to live.

### G. Re-entry, not mini-conclusion
A digression should usually flow back into the host prose rather than close like a self-contained article.

### H. Narrator pressure is scarce
The narrator may interpret, judge evidence, make theological claims and create emotional pressure, but these interventions are scarce resources. Spend them where accumulated material cannot do the work alone.

### I. Human irregularity
Paragraph lengths, sentence pressure and rhetorical intensity should follow material, not alternate according to a polished template.

### J. The reader discovers without being congratulated
Never tell the reader that a connection is clever, surprising, beautiful or important. Construct the conditions in which it can be perceived.

## 10. What a v3 should do

Do **not** produce v3 by simply editing v2 sentence by sentence.

A v3 experiment should reconstruct the passage from the same evidence under this grammar and test four specific changes:

1. recover one or two earned high-voltage lines from v1 without restoring narrator overpressure;
2. magnify one Gilgamesh mortality scene rather than summarize it;
3. remove at least one remaining explanatory bridge around collective speech / “來吧” and rely on textual adjacency;
4. re-enter the canonical Chapter 2 instead of giving the insertion its own polished ending.

Acceptance question:

> **Does the passage feel written because the author had something to see, rather than because the prose engine knew what a good paragraph should sound like?**

If yes, the Huang experiment has been absorbed into the book's own voice. If no, continue training before whole-chapter reconstruction.
