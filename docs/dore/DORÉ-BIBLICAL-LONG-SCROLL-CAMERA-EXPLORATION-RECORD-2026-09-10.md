## 0. Purpose of this record

This record preserves the current camera-language exploration before the project is decomposed into engineering stages.

The next phase must use **this record and the canonical narrative documents as its starting material**, and perform a new DORÉ Exploration focused on a practical question:

> **How should every engineering step be built, in what order, with what mature zero-additional-cost technologies, and with what validation gates, so that the final continuous Genesis-to-Revelation camera can actually be made?**

This file is intentionally broader than an implementation spec. It records the artistic, cinematographic, theological, technical, and production decisions that the engineering plan must preserve.

# 1. The work is ONE continuous shot

The project is not a sequence of shots joined to look continuous.

It is conceived as:

> **ONE CAMERA · ONE CONTINUOUS PATH · GENESIS → REVELATION**

The virtual camera begins at the beginning and does not die, reset, or hand consciousness to another camera until the final New Heaven / New Earth.

The 241 canonical Doré originals are **not 241 shots, slides, cards, or pages**. They are canonical visual anchors encountered by the same continuing camera.

The camera may change its movement grammar, height, scale, lens, speed, focus, spatialization method, and relation to the world, but the viewing consciousness must remain continuous.

The correct planning object is therefore not a traditional shot list. It is a single **Genesis-to-Revelation Camera Trajectory** containing many camera phrases and visual anchors.

The eventual project-description animation should be created by connecting all camera positions and trajectories into a playable visualization. When the full trajectory is drawn, the long scroll itself is largely described.

The first camera moment is **not** “Let there be light.”

> **起初**

All visible text in the finished work must be bilingual:

- **Traditional Chinese**
- **English**

All quoted English Scripture must use the **King James Version (KJV)**.

Scripture is not intended to behave like subtitles, UI panels, or kinetic-typography decoration. It should **quietly emerge inside the biblical world**.

The exact rendering grammar remains an exploration subject.

# 4. The established order of what becomes alive

The project’s existing narrative constitution remains controlling.

## First: Light / Glory becomes alive

The first thing that truly moves by itself is light.

The golden light is not a flat tint, modern glow, gradient, particle system, or decorative overlay. It should be expressed as **golden engraved linework** that can move along and through Doré’s black-and-white engraved world.

Once born in Genesis, this light becomes a persistent global state. It does not reset at every illustration.

It continues through the entire Bible toward the New Heaven and New Earth.

## Second: Grace / Life becomes alive

Light is not the only thing allowed to move.

Where Scripture narrates life, grace, healing, miracle, restoration, resurrection, living creation, or decisive action, the world must be allowed to become alive.

Examples include:

- animals moving;
- a lame person standing and walking;
- eyes opening;
- Lazarus rising;
- water responding;
- bread being broken;
- hands touching;
- wind, cloud, garments, wings, water, terrain, and architecture moving where narratively appropriate;
- a seed germinating;
- vines growing;
- fruit appearing;
- living creation responding to the biblical word.

Engineering constraints must never negate a miracle explicitly narrated by Scripture.

## Third: The Word / Scripture becomes alive

The third living element is Scripture itself.

The whole scroll may be visually silent, while the words of Scripture quietly appear throughout it.

This should not become flying verses or repeated title-card effects.

The words should feel as though they were latent in the world and have become visible.

# 5. Living creation is part of the camera vocabulary

The project must not reduce “life animation” to human body motion.

Biblical living creation can become a camera carrier and temporal carrier.

A particularly important example is the vine.

In John 15, Christ says:

> 「我是真葡萄樹，我父是栽培的人。」  
> “I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman.” — John 15:1, KJV

A vine may therefore become genuinely alive in the visual language: shoot, tendril, leaf, branch, fruit, attachment, growth through time.

A camera could follow a growing vine rather than a person. The vine could travel across Doré linework, stone, architecture, or the edge of one visual anchor and become the transition into another biblical space.

Other possible living-creation camera carriers include:

- seed and root;
- wheat;
- fig tree;
- lilies;
- river / living water;
- wind through fields;
- fish;
- sheep;
- dove;
- birds;
- cloud;
- fire;
- star.

The governing rule is not “animate the background.”

It is:

> **Life becomes alive where Scripture gives that life meaning.**

# 6. Camera language must be large enough to prevent AI repetition

A major risk has been explicitly identified:

AI discovers one successful device and repeats it mechanically.

Examples of unacceptable repetition include:

- repeated over-the-shoulder passages;
- repeated eye → pupil transitions;
- repeated push-ins;
- repeated full-3D movement merely because 3D is available;
- repeated aerial reveals whenever a scene is large;
- repeated body-occlusion transitions;
- repeated wide → close → pull-back miracle grammar;
- repeated use of the same height, direction, energy, or scale pattern.

Therefore the project must **not** begin the complete Camera Path Atlas until the camera-language vocabulary is sufficiently mature.

A dedicated **Doré Cinematic Language Bible** should first be explored and established.

The target is not a list of textbook shot names. It should be a sufficiently broad set of distinct camera grammars, each defined by:

- what the viewer experiences;
- what biblical narrative function it serves;
- how it enters;
- how it exits;
- which spatialization level it needs;
- whether it has theological risk;
- how rare it should be;
- whether it should be available only once;
- what recent camera grammars should inhibit its reuse.

Current camera-language families to explore include at least the following.

## 7.6 Monumental / epic camera

The project must explore how Doré’s already monumental compositions can become **more monumental through camera language**, rather than merely through more visual effects.

Key principle under exploration:

> **Scale is often revealed by contrast rather than by starting wide.**

Possible scale expansion:

micro detail  
→ human body  
→ crowd  
→ architecture  
→ geography  
→ cosmic scale.

A person can first establish scale. The camera then retreats, rises, or changes spatial relationship until the person becomes tiny and the world becomes enormous.

A battlefield can begin at hoof / dust / weapon / hand scale and expand into army / valley / geography.

A monumental scene can also reverse this direction and dive from geography back into a single human detail.

# 8. Eagle / aerial camera: Scripture determines when the device is legitimate

The project must distinguish between **camera grammar inspired by biblical language** and literal insertion of a creature.

Genesis 1:2 uses the Hebrew verb often discussed in relation to hovering / brooding / fluttering, and Deuteronomy 32:11 uses the same verb in an eagle image. This may inform the **quality of hovering camera movement**, but it does **not** authorize inserting a literal eagle into Genesis 1:2.

Therefore the Genesis opening may explore a weightless, hovering camera grammar without visually placing an eagle where Scripture does not place one.

Revelation, however, contains explicit eagle / flying-creature imagery. In such contexts, a real creature-led aerial camera can become legitimate.

A future epic example to explore is an eagle or flying-creature-led transition into a vast end-times battlefield / Armageddon-scale geography.

The intended effect is not a generic drone shot.

It can be a genuine **scale transition**:

ground-level human / horse / dust  
→ bird enters  
→ camera is carried upward  
→ battlefield opens  
→ terrain becomes true three-dimensional geography  
→ Doré’s monumental composition is discovered at a much larger spatial scale.

This is the kind of moment where full 3D may be justified.

# 9. 3D is not a default style; it is a scarce narrative resource

The project must not become “3D because we can.”

Every segment should answer:

> **If this were not full 3D, what essential narrative or camera possibility would be lost?**

If the answer is only “it would look less impressive,” full 3D is probably not justified.

Spatialization should be selected minimally and intentionally.

Current engineering categories remain useful:

- `FLAT`
- `2.5D`
- `NVS` / novel-view synthesis
- `FULL_3D`

Full 3D should be reserved for scenes whose camera language genuinely requires major spatial freedom or scale revelation.

Candidate areas for later exploration include, without yet locking them:

- Red Sea;
- Babel;
- Temple / Jerusalem;
- selected Cross geography;
- selected Resurrection movement;
- Revelation throne / cosmic visions;
- Armageddon-scale geography;
- New Jerusalem.

The important principle is **scarcity**. A rare spatial ability retains power because it is not constantly displayed.

# 10. Doré originals must become navigable spatial scenes where needed

A plain high-resolution JPEG plus SVG paths and CSS transforms is not sufficient for genuine camera movement such as:

- passing a shoulder;
- circling a figure;
- moving behind foreground geometry;
- walking into a road;
- revealing previously occluded space;
- changing parallax substantially;
- changing viewpoint rather than merely zooming.

The project therefore requires real spatialization technologies where the chosen camera grammar demands them.

Mature or promising technology families already identified for continued evaluation include:

- monocular depth estimation;
- semantic / instance segmentation;
- layered 2.5D reconstruction;
- point-cloud / mesh reconstruction;
- novel-view synthesis;
- Stable Virtual Camera / SEVA;
- GenWarp;
- Wonder3D and related single-image multiview reconstruction;
- 3D Gaussian Splatting / single-image Gaussian approaches;
- explicit geometry + missing-region reconstruction;
- trajectory-controlled motion methods;
- Blender camera curves;
- Three.js camera playback;
- GSAP scroll-linked camera control where appropriate;
- Unreal-style rail/crane concepts as virtual-production references, even if Unreal is not used in final runtime.

The project must distinguish three provenance layers:

**A. Canonical Doré pixels** — actually present in the original.  
**B. Reconstructed Doré space** — geometry/depth inferred from the original.  
**C. Inferred unseen space** — content that the original never depicted but must be reconstructed for continuous camera travel.

C must never be represented as though Doré literally drew it.

The preferred production principle is:

> **Preserve and warp known Doré information wherever possible; generate only what must be revealed because the camera exposes previously unseen space.**

# 11. High-resolution rule

All Doré anchors used for camera travel must have sufficient resolution for crop, push, settle, texture, reconstruction, and close observation.

The immutable element is the **canonical Doré identity**, not a poor-quality source file.

If an existing 241-registry file is too low-resolution, the project should use or create a high-resolution restoration/reset of the **same Doré original**.

The restoration must preserve:

- composition;
- figures;
- narrative;
- authorship;
- Doré ID;
- Scripture mapping;
- provenance.

It must not become a newly invented “Doré-style” image.

If ONE already contains a high-resolution reset/restoration of the same original, it should be reused.

# 12. Micro action: large composition stays Doré, meaningful life happens locally

A strong current directing principle is:

> **Macro composition stays Doré. Micro action carries life.**

The large Doré composition should not be generically animated everywhere.

The camera can discover very small actions that the original viewer might have overlooked, and by enlarging them make them cinematic events.

Examples under exploration:

- thumb pressure breaking bread;
- hand reaching to garment fringe;
- a foot accepting weight;
- toes pressing the ground;
- eyelid opening;
- a finger moving in darkness;
- water touching an ankle;
- breath beginning;
- vine tendril attaching;
- seed splitting;
- a leaf uncurling.

The technical benefit is also significant: localized movement reduces deformation, face drift, background hallucination, and destruction of engraving identity.

A large camera move can coexist with very small character animation.

# 13. Camera movement must be motivated, but the vocabulary must not become formulaic

The existing motivated-camera principle remains useful:

> **Why does the camera move now?**

Possible motives include glory, grace, Scripture, event, revelation, transition, scale, witness, discovery, concealment, or living creation.

However, motive alone is insufficient. Two different scenes can have the same motive and still require different camera grammar.

Therefore the future camera planner must consider both:

1. **Narrative motivation** — why move?
2. **Vocabulary diversity** — why move this way, given what the camera has recently done?

# 14. Camera memory / repetition guard

The future DORÉ camera system should maintain a memory of recently used camera language.

Possible recorded attributes include:

- movement family;
- direction;
- height;
- scale;
- lens behavior;
- push / pull tendency;
- orbit usage;
- over-shoulder usage;
- POV usage;
- eye / pupil usage;
- aerial usage;
- 3D level;
- transition carrier;
- camera energy;
- scale expansion / compression;
- camera-to-subject distance;
- stillness duration;
- whether the world moved while camera held;
- whether camera moved while world held.

The next camera phrase should not blindly repeat the recent successful grammar.

The project should explore a **Camera Repetition Guard** that can detect local and long-range repetition.

This should be more sophisticated than a fixed cooldown such as “do not repeat an over-shoulder shot for 20 minutes.”

It should understand similarity across multiple camera dimensions.

# 15. Rare grammar and one-time grammar

Not every camera device should be equally available.

The future cinematic language library should distinguish:

- common grammar;
- uncommon grammar;
- rare grammar;
- one-time grammar.

Examples of potentially rare grammar include:

- pupil / eye-world passage;
- creature-led aerial camera;
- extreme vertical revelation;
- full geographic unfold;
- major world transformation;
- full cosmic scale transition.

A device may be powerful precisely because the viewer has not seen it before.

The future Director Audit should detect **RARE GRAMMAR OVERUSE**.

The Doré project has an advantage over conventional film because it has one continuous virtual camera and therefore can record every camera phrase structurally.

# 18. Rhythm: continuous shot does not mean continuous intensity

A one-take Bible covering enormous historical and theological scale will fail if every moment is spectacular.

The camera must breathe.

A future Camera Score should be able to identify long periods of sustained high energy and flag **EPIC ENERGY SATURATION**.

An epic scene may need to be followed by stillness rather than a larger epic scene.

Possible rhythm contrast:

- battlefield → hand;
- flood → water drop;
- crowd → one face;
- miracle → quiet aftermath;
- temple → fabric;
- geography → seed;
- cosmic → breath.

The project should explore both **Reader Time** (scroll/narrative progression) and **Living Time** (subtle life that can continue when the viewer pauses), without yet locking a final implementation.

# 21. Previs principle: camera path before miracle production

Miracles, character motion, and world motion should be created **after the relevant camera path is directed**.

The project should not follow the common AI workflow:

> animate an image → then decide how to show it.

Instead:

> direct the camera → determine what the viewer can actually see → animate only the meaningful action required by that camera.

This keeps the animation lighter, more controllable, and more faithful to Doré.

# 22. Zero-additional-budget production constraint

The only intended monetary investment is the user’s existing ChatGPT Plus subscription.

There is **no additional production budget**.

Therefore every production path selected by DORÉ Exploration must have a credible route with **$0 additional paid-service cost**.

This does not mean the visual ambition should be low.

The objective is:

> **big-budget-film effect with near-zero additional monetary cost.**

The project should favor, where practical:

- open-source software;
- local models;
- existing ChatGPT / Codex / Work capabilities already included in the user’s plan;
- Blender;
- Three.js / WebGL / WebGPU;
- mature free libraries;
- existing GitHub / Cloudflare capabilities;
- deterministic preprocessing;
- reusable asset reconstruction;
- selective rather than universal expensive computation.

“High-budget scene” should therefore mean **high engineering / computational complexity**, not paid API spend.

# 23. GPT-6 strategy

GPT-6 is available to the user but its usable token allowance is very limited.

The project should therefore be designed so that GPT-6 is **not required for success**.

DORÉ Exploration should first prove that every major problem can be solved using the existing project stack, GPT-5.6-level reasoning/coding, Codex/Work where available, and mature zero-additional-cost tools.

GPT-6 should be treated as an optional final multiplier.

The ideal final state is that the whole project is already buildable without GPT-6.

Only then, if useful, scarce GPT-6 tokens can be spent on the highest-value residual questions, such as:

- finding a small number of missed directing opportunities after quantitative audit;
- resolving a uniquely difficult spatial transition;
- reviewing a mature full camera path for genuinely non-obvious creative weaknesses;
- improving a particularly complex final implementation.

Even “director-level review” should first be handled by the engineered Doré Director Audit rather than by default spending GPT-6 tokens.

# 24. DORÉ Exploration itself is part of the production method

The project assumes that sufficiently deep DORÉ Exploration can often replace the need for a more powerful model.

The exploration standard is not merely “find an interesting idea.”

For each major cinematic or engineering problem, exploration must ask:

1. **Artistic:** Is this camera language worth using here?
2. **Biblical / theological:** Is it faithful to Scripture and does the camera position imply anything the work should not claim?
3. **Cinematographic:** Is there mature professional precedent or a coherent visual grammar?
4. **Technical:** Can it actually be produced, not merely described?
5. **Doré fidelity:** Can the original engraving identity survive the method?
6. **Continuity:** Can it remain inside the one unbroken Genesis-to-Revelation camera?
7. **Diversity:** Does it add a distinct camera grammar rather than repeat a recent success?
8. **Cost:** Can it be done with zero additional paid-service cost?
9. **Runtime:** Can it ultimately be delivered in the intended Web experience or a practical fallback?
10. **Auditability:** Can its camera and world behavior be represented in the future Camera Score / Audit system?

Only solutions that survive these questions should enter engineering.
