# Doré Textual Difference Research v0.1

## Goal
Doré must explain why biblical witnesses differ without collapsing witness identity or pretending that a reference mismatch alone proves textual originality.

## Cause families
1. **Versification** — the same or overlapping material is numbered differently: psalm superscriptions, chapter boundaries, verse splits/joins, Greek/Latin numbering.
2. **Canon/source scope** — a source contains books, sections, additions, or identities outside the shared Protestant `bible.ref` namespace.
3. **Textual tradition / textual base** — editions differ because manuscript traditions and critical editorial decisions include, omit, bracket, relocate, or choose different readings.
4. **Translation layer** — after textual-base identity is established, wording can differ because of lexical choice, syntax, idiom, register, translation philosophy, and target-language conventions. This layer must never be inferred from reference mismatch alone.

## Research boundary
A structural correspondence can say "these references point to corresponding passage material". It may not say "this reading is original" unless an explicit textual-critical study with evidence supports that claim.

## Evidence anchors
- SBLGNT Introduction: the SBLGNT was established by comparing major critical/printed Greek editions and records thousands of variation units; inclusion/omission/substitution are explicit textual-variant categories. https://www.sblgnt.com/about/introduction/
- SBLGNT John: John 7:53–8:11 is presented with an explicit textual note concerning manuscript support and placement. https://www.sblgnt.com/download/revint/64-John.pdf
- H. B. Swete, *Introduction to the Old Testament in Greek*: LXX/Greek Esdras identities do not map naively onto modern Ezra–Nehemiah chapter identities; 1 Esdras contains material parallel to Chronicles/Ezra/Nehemiah plus material without a Hebrew parallel. https://ccel.org/ccel/swete/greekot/greekot.iv.iii.html

## Graduation requirement
Every validated structural phenomenon must have a cause family, confidence, evidence basis, and correspondence policy. High-confidence benchmark loci must classify correctly. Remaining medium-confidence phenomena are allowed only when explicitly preserved as research candidates rather than normalized or silently dropped.
