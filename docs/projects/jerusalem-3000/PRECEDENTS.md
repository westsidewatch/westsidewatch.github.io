# Precedent study — 4D historical city reconstruction

Date: 2026-09-24

## Finding

No reviewed precedent currently combines Jerusalem's full multi-millennial urban biography, one georeferenced terrain, reversible construction/destruction, object-level evidence status, Biblical event routes, and publication-grade web presentation in one system.

The closest methodological precedent is **EPFL DHLAB — Jerusalem 1840–1940**. It explicitly frames Jerusalem as a 4D city reconstruction problem and proposes a shared geodatabase whose 3D geometry is dynamically regenerated when geometric information changes, with metadata/paradata attached to the model. Jerusalem 3000 should treat this as a major architecture precedent, while extending the temporal span far earlier and later.

## Precedents to absorb

### EPFL DHLAB — Jerusalem 1840–1940
Absorb:
- 4D rather than isolated 3D snapshots.
- shared geodatabase as authority.
- dynamically regenerated geometry.
- metadata/paradata/non-geometric evidence attached to reconstruction.
- collaborative incremental reconstruction.

Do not copy:
- its 1840–1940 temporal boundary.
- any assumption that one modern documentary regime can cover earlier archaeological eras.

### Virtual Angkor / Visualising Angkor
Absorb:
- LiDAR/DEM/GIS as ground truth beneath reconstructed city fabric.
- evidence-based model library rather than one monolithic mesh.
- archaeological annotations and texts placed at their geographic findspots.
- iterative dialogue between reconstruction and scholarship.
- city-scale procedural distribution of reconstructed assets.

Jerusalem 3000 extension:
- add explicit temporal object lifecycle and reversible destruction/rebuilding.

### Rome Reborn
Absorb:
- procedural generation for large quantities of ordinary urban fabric.
- handcrafted high-authority monuments + procedural background city.
- city-scale performance strategy.

Jerusalem 3000 extension:
- never allow procedural plausibility to erase Evidence Architecture.

### Virtual Illés Initiative
Absorb:
- Jerusalem-specific archival overlays.
- historic maps, documents and 3D models in one immersive GIS environment.
- collaborative annotation.

Jerusalem 3000 extension:
- use as a strong nineteenth-century source layer, not as a universal city model.

### Muristan in Jerusalem
Absorb:
- one place represented in different periods.
- database-linked 3D objects, excavation results, photographs and drawings.
- structural development from antiquity to today.

### OpenBible.info 3D Temple Mount
Absorb:
- browser-native WebGL/static-host viability.
- explicit source disagreements attached to geometry.
- automated geometric verification.
- reconstruction-not-survey disclaimer at object/system level.

### Virtual Angkor / Herod's Temple story layers
Absorb:
- historical/biblical stories as spatial events attached to locations.
- route/story layer above terrain and city geometry.

## Canonical synthesis for Jerusalem 3000

Jerusalem 3000 architecture should now be treated as five coupled ledgers:

1. **Terrain ledger** — immutable geospatial ground.
2. **Object ledger** — walls, gates, roads, buildings, water systems, monuments.
3. **Lifecycle ledger** — birth, build, transform, ruin, burial, reuse, rebuild.
4. **Evidence ledger** — observed / reconstructed / inferred / disputed + sources/paradata.
5. **Event ledger** — Biblical/historical routes, scenes and texts bound to time + space.

Rendering is a projection of those ledgers at time T, not the authority itself.

## Hard conclusion

Do not search for a single template to clone. The mature precedents solve different pieces. The correct next architecture is to combine EPFL's 4D geodatabase logic, Angkor's evidence/geospatial model library, Rome Reborn's procedural urban generation, and Jerusalem-specific archival/archaeological layers into the existing Jerusalem 3000 authority model.
